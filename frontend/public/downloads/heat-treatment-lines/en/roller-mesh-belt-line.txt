Mesh-belt Annealing & Tempering Line | Inquiry Checklist

Start with four items; unknown information may remain blank:
1. Workpiece and material: part name, grade and incoming condition.
2. Target process: specific annealing objective or tempering requirements for already quenched parts.
3. Output: hourly/shift output, shifts and changeover frequency.
4. Contact: phone, WeChat or email.

Also provide any available:
- Drawings, typical/maximum dimensions, unit mass and photos.
- Loading photos, orientation, layer depth and collision/mixing restrictions.
- Material certificates, initial microstructure and previous processing/quenching records.
- Process curves, soaking/cooling requirements and permitted delays.
- Hardness, microstructure/mechanical targets, oxidation/decarburization requirements and inspection standards.
- Space, energy, gas, cooling/exhaust conditions and adjacent interfaces.

Confirm together during design:
- Annealing and post-quench tempering are separate routes. Shared equipment requires checks of zones, atmosphere, cooling, clearance and recipe changes.
- For long soaking or slow cooling, compare belt and other furnaces using output and furnace length.
- Calculate belt residence, workpiece through-heating and effective soaking separately.
- Capacity uses net workpiece loading; account for tooling/belt mechanical and thermal loads separately. Do not deduct downtime or empty-belt losses twice.
- Allocate supply responsibilities for loading, heating, cooling, discharge, auxiliaries and site interfaces.
- Agree on representative materials, loading, sampling, inspection methods and delivery/acceptance responsibilities.
