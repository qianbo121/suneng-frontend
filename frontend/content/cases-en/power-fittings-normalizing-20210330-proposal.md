Loading-frame dimensions and furnace-chamber dimensions need separate agreement: one describes a fixture, the other the equipment's internal space. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s RT4-180-10 documents dated 30 March 2021 specify a 2200 × 1200 × 800 mm chamber and “maximum loading-frame external dimensions” of 1800 × 1000 × 700 mm. The latter are not defined as the overall limit for a fully loaded assembly. Workpiece projections, lifting lugs and spacers still require separate checks on the loading drawing.

## What does each set of dimensions constrain?

The quotation heading refers to the loading frame, not the complete frame-and-workpiece envelope. A frame drawing establishes the frame's maximum outline; a loading drawing must also show the actual loaded outline. The source provides neither complete dimensional reference definitions nor permissible clearances calculated from the difference. That difference cannot simply be allocated to projecting workpieces or lifting attachments.

## Checking workpieces, lugs, spacers and changes to supports

The following checks arise from the two dimension sets; they do not establish that the original project had these attachments. Where workpieces extend outside the frame, mark projection direction and length, then compare the loaded outline with the chamber, door passage and required clearances. This goes beyond the frame itself, so 1800 × 1000 × 700 mm cannot directly serve as the permissible full-load envelope.

For added lifting lugs, establish whether they remain attached in the furnace or are removed after lifting. Confirm whether the frame dimensions include them and check the outline passing through the door. For spacers, show whether they sit inside or beneath the frame and assess the resulting height and contact positions rather than empty-frame height alone.

Support changes also require contact checks. The proposed bogie uses load-bearing refractory masonry and 30 mm-thick heat-resistant hearth plates, with resistance strips around the chamber and beneath the bogie hearth. Changes to leg number, position or contact area must be compared with the hearth structure and bottom heat-transfer space. Meeting the envelope or the listed 3 t alone does not establish suitability of a new arrangement.

After the space check, assess the actual process curve and fully loaded arrangement. The proposal lists two-zone control, furnace uniformity ≤±10°C and control accuracy ≤±1°C, but does not fully define loaded sensor locations or assessment methods. These proposed criteria do not establish that every part in a new frame meets its process requirements.

## Which wiring is covered by the historical quotation?

The RT4-180-10 quotation of 30 March 2021 includes 13% VAT and proposes design, manufacture, transport, installation and commissioning. External secondary wiring and cable trays between furnace and control cabinet are included; foundation materials and construction are excluded.

Match cable and tray supply, installation/termination and pricing scope to the actual route between furnace and cabinet. Recheck any change in cabinet position, length or routing. Furnace hearth plates and site foundations are different items; inclusion of the furnace does not establish inclusion of its foundations.

### Do these documents establish a complete quenching installation?

If discharge transfer, a cooling medium or subsequent operations are required, list their equipment and interfaces separately. A process name in the quotation heading does not establish that those services are included.

### What existing information is enough to start a comparison?

Provide a frame drawing with dimensional references and a fully loaded drawing. Mark workpiece projections, attachments entering the furnace, spacer height and leg positions, and list workpiece and frame weights separately. Material, principal cross-sections and process requirements support the later thermal assessment; cabinet position and foundation conditions belong in the interface schedule.
