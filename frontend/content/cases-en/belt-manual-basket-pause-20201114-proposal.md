A Suneng muffle-type protective-atmosphere mesh-belt furnace proposal dated 14 November 2020 directs parts through a discharge opening into a movable basket inside the oil tank. When the basket is full, the source requires the belt to stop while an empty basket is exchanged manually. Basket exchange is therefore part of the heating process's operating cycle. Capacity comparisons must consider receiving volume, exchange time and the residence of parts already in the furnace; the proposed figure of approximately 60 kg/h is not proof of sustained acceptable output.

## Continuous furnace transport meets batch basket exchange

| Stage | Original arrangement | Check required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Furnace discharge into oil | Parts fall directly into a movable basket inside the oil tank | Reliable receiving and permissible load per basket |
| Full basket | Pause the belt, pull out the basket manually and insert an empty one | Effect of the pause on residence time of parts inside the furnace |
| Basket mechanism | 4 roller sets and 1 locking mechanism | Positioning, locking state and access for exchange |

The source describes no alternative discharge route capable of receiving parts continuously and does not promise that the furnace belt keeps running during exchange. Cycle assessment should therefore check receiving available, full-basket pause, empty basket positioned, and transport resumed as one set of states. This organises the proposal's movement relationships; it does not introduce unconfirmed operator instructions.

## Assess changes in residence time before hourly output

The effective belt width is 180 mm, heated length 2100 mm and effective height 50 mm. The parameters list a belt speed of 10–114 mm/min and maximum quenching output of 60 kg/h. The final-acceptance section also states that actual output varies with part geometry and dimensions, individual weight, loading, metallurgical conditions and process requirements. Speed, usable loading space and hourly capacity must therefore be considered together. Dividing heated length by belt speed alone cannot establish identical heating times for every part.

When transport stops for basket exchange, parts already in the furnace remain there longer. Use actual basket capacity and exchange frequency to check whether this additional residence is compatible with the parts' heating requirements. If not, the feeding rhythm, receiving organisation or equipment proposal must be changed. Simply increasing belt speed afterwards to recover output is insufficient. The source does not define heating and atmosphere-control programmes during belt stoppage, and this article does not invent holding settings or automatic restart logic.

## A basket receiving parts in oil needs more than a dimensional check

The proposed basket has openings for quenching-oil circulation, and the tank description lists a circulating oil pump for cooling and vigorous agitation. Permissible basket loading must consider part stacking, oil-flow paths and required treatment results. The belt's surface-load limit cannot be used as a basket's total weight allowance. Positioning and locking of full and empty baskets, and alignment with the discharge opening, should also be verified so that the receiving end has defined states alongside the furnace-feed parameters.

The proposal uses methanol dripping to generate a protective atmosphere and lists audible and visual alarms for loss of water and drive abnormalities. Oil-tank cooling is optional in the parameter list, while the structural section describes an external cooling-water jacket and circulating oil pump. Reconcile the actual cooling configuration and define how belt stoppage and basket exchange interact with atmosphere supply, extraction, oil level and temperature, and cooling faults. Alarm names do not constitute a complete response table, and a flowmeter alone does not establish independent closed-loop carbon-potential control.

## Acceptance should include real basket exchanges

Suneng proposes equipment positioning and installation and the secondary electrical work between the equipment and control cabinet. The buyer provides building power, utility pipework, cooling water and acceptance-test parts. Capacity verification should use agreed parts and loading, recording continuous feeding, the full-basket pause and restart after exchange. Required results, such as distortion and hardness, should be checked under the agreement. Running an unloaded belt cannot establish that manual basket exchange meets production requirements.

Initial discussions can start with part geometry and material, individual and per-basket loads, permissible variation in heating residence, and existing lifting or basket-exchange arrangements. Defining receiving and exchange before comparing belt width and power makes suitability for the intended production easier to assess.
