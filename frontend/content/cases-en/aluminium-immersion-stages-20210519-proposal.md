Two 2021 Suneng aluminium-alloy quenching-furnace proposals time door opening to basket contact with the quenching medium separately from contact to complete entry of the effective zone, with four settings for the second stage. Rapid-quench assessment must define each start/end event and load orientation. The shortest setting is not the complete door-opening-to-full-immersion time.

## Two movement stages cannot become one generic quenching time

| Proposal or stage | Source time | Timing boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 m proposal, first stage | ≤3 s | Door opening to basket contact with the quenching medium |
| 3 m proposal, first stage | ≤5 s | Door opening to basket contact with the quenching medium |
| Both proposals, second stage | Four settings: 5, 8, 12 and 15 s | Basket contact with medium to complete entry of the effective zone |

The first stage ends at basket contact, which need not mean all workpieces are immersed. The second ends at complete entry of the effective zone. Basket bottom, lowest workpiece and highest workpiece occupy different positions. Loading drawings, liquid-level datum and detection methods must jointly define how events are recognised. Touching the water surface is not full immersion.

## Reconcile the whole-transfer five-second statement with the staged table

Both structural descriptions also define transfer from door opening until all parts are in the water and require less than 5 seconds. This conflicts with the parameter tables' separate contact and selectable immersion stages. The faster figure cannot be selected for promotion, and simply adding the stages does not establish an agreed complete process time.

Mark the start of door movement, the object first contacting liquid and the full-immersion boundary, then establish whether stages follow without interruption and include valve response and movement waits. Finally reconcile the agreement and test table. Four settings are a proposed adjustment range; choosing one for production requires actual material, geometry and process requirements.

## Check long and square baskets against their own liquid-level datum

The 7 m draft gives an effective working zone of 7000 × 1500 × 2500 mm; the 3 m draft gives 3000 × 3000 × 2500 mm, both length × width × height. Equal height does not establish interchangeable descent speed, baskets or immersion states. Basket dimensions are for the tendering party to determine. Each drawing still needs envelope, lifting lugs, part arrangement and water level.

The process first moves the quench-water tank to its designated position beneath the furnace, then opens the door and lowers the basket. After the tank withdraws, the loading platform moves beneath the basket for lowering and unhooking. These handovers outside the timing boundary also require position conditions. A sufficiently fast lowering mechanism does not establish readiness to test the complete transfer route.

## Temperature-recovery test loads are not rated loads

The 7 m draft proposes a rated load of 1.5 t, but limits furnace-temperature recovery within 40 min to loading ≤1000 kg. The 3 m draft proposes 2 t, while recovery time applies to loading ≤800 kg. Rated load, recovery testing and immersion-time testing should each identify workpieces, basket and additional loads. Light-load recovery conditions cannot be extended directly to rated full load.

Water-tank cooling and furnace circulation are also separate systems. The source provides furnace hot-air circulation and proposes tank jet circulation, outdoor cooling and level detection. Immersion tests need agreed medium temperature, level and loading conditions. Fan ratings or pump lists do not replace records of those conditions.

## Information for a similar enquiry

Provide workpiece/basket loading drawings, material grades, target processes, tank levels and the site transfer layout. Define door opening, liquid contact and immersion as observable assessment events, then agree settings, lifting mechanisms, equipment quantities and test methods with Suneng.
