Suneng's roller-hearth quench-heating furnace proposal for bulldozer cutting edges, dated 17 October 2020, specifies five heating zones and three water-cooled circulation fans, one each in zones three, four and five in the middle-to-rear section. Circulation is arranged by position in the heating process; there is not a fan in every zone. For similar cutting edges, assess thickness, furnace transport and the rear equalising section together. Fan count alone cannot establish either insufficient provision or achieved temperature uniformity.

## Match the zones before comparing circulation

| Proposal item | Source parameter or position | How to interpret it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heating zones | 5 zones | Divisions for electrical heating and temperature control throughout the furnace |
| Circulation-fan positions | One each in zones 3, 4 and 5 | Described as the middle-to-rear equalising section; do not relocate them to the first two zones |
| Circulation-fan motor power | 1.5 kW each, 3 fans total | Fan motor ratings, not heating power per zone |

The source describes cutting edges moving continuously on heat-resistant steel rollers, with heating and soaking taking place during transport. The last three zones are expressly described as equalising zones with circulation fans. Configuration comparisons should therefore consider the temperature process required as the parts reach those zones and whether residence time suits the cutting edges. The source gives no individual lengths for the first two or last three zones. Dividing the 12000 mm working length equally into five sections would not establish actual zone residence times.

## Different thicknesses cannot be compared at one transport speed alone

The proposed workpieces are plate-like parts such as bulldozer cutting edges, 260–3000 mm long, 100–550 mm wide and 12–120 mm thick. Select representative parts using actual materials, thicknesses, placement and process requirements, then check their temperature condition on entering the equalising section and whether requirements can be met before departure. Fitting different parts within the 570 mm-wide, 160 mm-high working space does not establish that one speed and soaking arrangement suits them all.

The three fans circulate hot gas within their respective zones; the source also uses radiant tubes above and below the load. Shielding of radiation and airflow changes with part width, thickness and spacing. Compare configurations using a loading drawing, rather than treating fan power as evidence of through-thickness heating capability. The source specifies neither particular material grades nor an internal workpiece-temperature measurement plan. This article does not add a supposedly verified core-heating process.

## The measurement location determines what uniformity means

The equipment parameters state a maximum working temperature of 950°C, furnace-temperature uniformity of ≤±7.5°C and temperature-control accuracy of ≤±1°C. Each zone has one duplex thermocouple connected to control and recording instruments. These are proposed furnace-zone measurements and indicators, not results for cutting-edge core-temperature differences or hardness uniformity after press quenching. Verification should define the effective zone, test temperature, loading and measuring points, with the required workpiece heating condition and downstream quality requirements agreed separately.

The fans expressly have water-cooled mounting bases, and the user provides water supply and drainage and related auxiliaries. Circulation-system review should also establish cooling-water connections, fan operating-state signals, and heating and transport responses after faults. The source's fan-overload protection primarily concerns the motor; it does not replace a complete procedure for parts in the furnace after cooling failure or fan stoppage. Nor does the design statement about long-term operation at 950°C establish verified service life.

## Furnace verification must connect to final cutting-edge inspection

The press-quenching machine, loading rack and discharge rack are buyer-supplied. The furnace is intended to heat and equalise the cutting edges before quenching. The source proposes 3–5 trial furnace loads for final acceptance, with inspection of hardness and hardness uniformity, metallurgical structure and flatness. To assess whether circulation in the last three zones suits the intended parts, record the agreed loading and heating process and complete corresponding product inspections with the user's press-quenching conditions. These are proposed verification requirements, not completed project test results.

Suneng proposes design, installation and commissioning of the supplied furnace and controls. The buyer provides external equipment and site utilities under the division of responsibilities. Initial discussions should assemble cutting-edge materials and dimensions, spacing, the target process curve, existing press-quench receiving arrangements and cooling-water conditions. These allow heating and circulation to be matched zone by zone rather than comparing only total power or fan count.
