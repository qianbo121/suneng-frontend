A 2022 Suneng continuous cylinder-curing proposal provides cylinder rotation both on a seven-position storage rack and during furnace transport, with manual resin scraping after preheating. Compare storage, transfer, scraping and furnace residence together. Continuous conveying does not mean the absence of manual stations or waiting.

## The storage rack does more than hold parts, and the furnace route includes scraping

The process first loads cylinders manually or by manipulator onto a seven-position rack, then transfers them to the continuous-furnace loading table. An internal chain moves and rotates them. Preheating, manual scraping and further heating/curing follow in sequence. The source retains handling choices at two transfers; it does not establish fully automatic robotic loading and unloading.

The proposal calls for scraping after preheating to 60°C, while labelling the scraping section ambient temperature. That label does not mean the cylinder itself has cooled to ambient or that manual-contact conditions are satisfied. Operating method, separation/protection, resin collection and exhaust interfaces need confirmation against the resin process and actual workpiece temperature. This page does not turn the flow description into direct operating instructions.

## Forward speed and cylinder rotation are separate adjustments

Drive sprockets advance the support frames synchronously, while a separate rotation chain engages fixture sprockets so cylinders turn as they travel. The draft lists average forward speed of 45–65 mm/min and cylinder rotation of 5–40 r/min. Their units and functions differ; faster rotation cannot replace residence in a heating section.

The storage rack also has separate conveying and rotation drives. Rotation while waiting before the furnace and during heating therefore needs separate arrangements. Changing cylinders or mandrel fixtures requires checks of supports, sprocket engagement, positioning after transfer and rotation continuity at both locations, rather than maximum diameter and length alone.

## Twelve minutes per cylinder is not twelve minutes of total furnace residence

The proposal gives a nominal interval of 12 min per part, adjustable to the process curve. Support-frame pitch is 660 mm. Assuming one cylinder at each position and uniform continuous movement, 660 ÷ 12 = 55 mm/min, within the proposed 45–65 mm/min range. This checks the interval between adjacent loaded positions, not total preheating and curing compliance for each cylinder.

Actual matching also requires section lengths, heating/curing times, empty positions, scraping and transfer waits. Waiting capacity at seven storage positions depends on occupancy and transfer methods; multiplying seven by twelve does not establish accommodation of any 84-minute stoppage.

## Resin collection and combustion exhaust are different interfaces

Removable resin-collection trays beneath the supports facilitate later cleaning. The gas-heating section separately describes combustion inside W-shaped radiant tubes, with flue gas passing through a heat exchanger to a common exhaust header. Drip collection and combustion flue gas do not replace process extraction for resin-curing volatiles.

The reviewed draft does not explicitly connect resin type, quantity per batch, volatile-release conditions and internal process-exhaust flow. Similar projects need these inputs before defining furnace extraction, external treatment and responses to abnormal stoppage. A gas-combustion exhaust fan does not establish complete curing extraction.

Suneng proposes the continuous curing furnace, seven-position rack and corresponding drives/controls, with on-site installation and commissioning. Customers can first submit representative cylinder and fixture-sprocket drawings, resin-curing curves, scraping methods and upstream arrival intervals. Assess storage and manual stations against furnace residence across the complete process, then agree loaded integration and temperature checks.
