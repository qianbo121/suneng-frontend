Suneng's mesh-belt austempering proposal for spring parts, dated 18 May 2026, places an adjustable extraction device at the heating-furnace discharge to prevent salt vapour entering the chamber. After the parts leave the salt tank, separate residual-salt blow-off and water washing are proposed. These provisions act at different locations and on different materials: extraction cannot replace removal of salt from parts, and a washing tank cannot replace extraction after the furnace. Procurement should trace the routes of both parts and gases to establish connections and supply responsibilities.

## Distinguish the three locations by what each treats

| Location | Proposed treatment | What it cannot replace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heating-furnace discharge | Extraction device with adjustable extraction rate | Removal of salt from workpiece surfaces |
| Residual salt on parts after the salt tank | Heating tubes and a blow-off fan | This arrangement does not establish complete salt removal |
| Subsequent immersion washer | Water washing, additional rinsing nozzles and water blow-off | Gas extraction after the furnace or subsequent emissions treatment |

Discharge extraction addresses the connection between the furnace and salt tank. The source says its purpose is to prevent salt vapour entering the chamber; it does not define a complete exhaust-treatment system. Check the relative positions of the extraction opening and discharge passage, external ducting and the treatment destination, and coordination of extraction with feeding and atmosphere supply. Adjustable extraction alone does not establish compliance with site emissions requirements.

Blow-off acts on parts leaving the salt bath, and water washing further treats salt carried out on them. Compare cleaning arrangements using the actual part geometry, overlaps and loading pattern to identify which areas can be blown and rinsed, and agree a residue-inspection method. Fans and rinse nozzles do not prove that every shielded area is adequately cleaned or that a complete salt and washing-liquid recovery loop exists.

## Salt-temperature maintenance, cooling and level control have separate functions

The salt tank includes heating and temperature maintenance, agitation and an air-cooled tube-bundle cooler. The proposal says heating stops and air cooling operates when the salt temperature is too high, while a low salt-level limit triggers audible and visual alarms. The downstream washer uses water and float-controlled automatic make-up. These level provisions differ. Automatic water make-up for the washer must not be attributed to the salt tank, and the salt tank's low-level alarm must not be expanded into automatic salt replenishment or every possible abnormal-condition shutdown interlock.

The source concerns spring parts in materials including 65Mn and 45Mn. It proposes nitrate-salt austempering and lists a salt-tank temperature-control range of 200–450°C. This is the proposed equipment range, not a universal process recipe for different grades. The adopted process and design documents must define the final salt composition and application conditions, control of carried-in materials, and measures against backflow or splashing between the salt tank and washer. This article does not derive salt-mixing instructions from a single mixture statement in the sample document.

## Reconcile washing temperature and recording channels in the final scope

The flow diagram describes washing at ambient temperature, whereas the detailed equipment section specifies heated washing at 80–90°C. This affects the washing-tank heat source, capacity and operating conditions. Neither value should be arbitrarily selected as final. After confirming the cleaning method, reconcile the recording channels: the introduction states four-point recording, while the control section specifies a two-channel instrument. The source lacks a wiring schedule sufficient to confirm recording of furnace, salt and wash-liquid temperatures together.

The effective heating dimensions are 400 × 4000 × 100 mm, recorded as width × length × height. Maximum quenching throughput is stated as approximately 100–200 kg/h. Actual belt loading, salt-bath residence and downstream washing must be verified together. Heating-furnace width or the quoted throughput alone does not establish acceptable whole-line output. An enquiry should include part drawings, loading conditions, the proposed process and residual-salt requirements, followed by separate acceptance provisions at the extraction point, salt-tank exit and washer exit.

Suneng proposes the heating furnace, isothermal salt tank, washing tank and associated controls. The buyer provides foundations, cooling-water interfaces, process media and secondary cables and conduits between equipment and control cabinet. Subsequent exhaust and wash-liquid treatment are not fully developed in the current schedule and should be added to the actual division of supply. Sequential passage through three machines does not mean all site interfaces are complete.
