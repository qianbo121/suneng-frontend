Suneng's 2017 mesh-belt normalising and tempering line proposal links post-normalising workpiece cooling to inlet hot-air preheating and a water-cooled cooling hood. Hot cooling air returns to the inlet preheating chamber; water in the hood absorbs radiant heat. The air path has a defined heat user, while the water is only described as available for other uses. Check the two circuits separately rather than counting both as realised energy savings.

## Trace two separate heat-recovery paths

| Stream | Proposed destination | Purchasing interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hot air from rapid cooling | Induced-draught fan sends it to the inlet preheating chamber | Explicit furnace-entry preheating arrangement; check connections and exhaust |
| Remaining preheater exhaust | Ducted outside the workshop | Not fully closed air recirculation; define the discharge connection |
| Water in the cooling hood | Protects the hood and absorbs radiant heat; hot water may be reused | External demand and usable heat are not established by “may be reused” |

Upper and lower air-cooling pipes, a blower and hot-air extraction fan are located in the discharge rapid-cooling chamber, directing hot air to the inlet preheater. That chamber has upper/lower hot-air ducts and a roof exhaust duct. This supports proposed reuse of cooling air for inlet preheating, but not achieved airflow, preheating temperature or recovery efficiency. Check duct supply limits, inlet preheating location and final exhaust connection on one layout.

## A hot-water outlet does not establish a site heat demand

The hood water primarily removes radiation from hot workpieces and protects the hood; other use is an additional possibility. Without a user needing heat at matching temperatures and times, the wording does not establish steam substitution or energy savings. For connection to an existing hot-water system, equipment and heat-user parties should jointly specify supply/return conditions, flow and available periods, preserving the hood's cooling requirements. This is an interface-review method derived from the proposal, not a completed project heat calculation.

The buyer prepares a cooling pool or tower, and the seller provides cooling-system piping drawings. Hot-water reuse must therefore fit the original cooling system. Confirm destinations, materials and installation responsibilities; “complete equipment installation” does not allocate all workshop-side work. Secondary-wiring responsibilities differ between sections and require a final responsibility list.

## Assess cooling against workpiece acceptance requirements

Proposed capacity is approximately 1000 kg/h, explicitly dependent on workpiece geometry, individual weight, piling, metallurgical conditions and process requirements. Final acceptance also requires distortion and hardness after normalising/tempering to meet jointly agreed limits. Inlet preheating and recovery alone do not establish process suitability. Validate the arrangement with representative workpieces, loading and cooling requirements.

The proposal lists approximately 1000 kg/h with workpiece and process conditions attached.

It separately requires furnace-temperature uniformity of ≤±5°C within the same zone, using three points each at 900°C in the normalising furnace and 650°C in the tempering furnace. These are internal temperature-test conditions, not rapid-cooling outlet workpiece temperatures or hot-water utilisation results. They are not converted into a validated energy-saving percentage.

## Useful information for discussions

Provide representative workpieces and piling arrangements, post-normalising cooling requirements, and site air-duct and cooling-water layouts. If hot-water recovery is intended, add the proposed heat users and operating periods. This allows the preheating circuit to be checked first and water-side utilisation assessed afterward, without committing to an unsupported saving figure.
