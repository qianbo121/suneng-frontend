Reusing fans and flues still requires checking their interfaces with the new combustion system. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s 20 m bogie-hearth furnace retrofit proposal dated 13 November 2018 retains oil heating and adds natural-gas combustion. Both can operate independently while sharing the original exhaust and combustion-air systems. New air branches and changeover valves close the original oil-burner air branches during gas operation. Reuse therefore includes changeover design and verification, not simply leaving old equipment untouched.

## Define the two independent operating modes

The proposal primarily concerns post-weld stress relief of large welded parts, including pressure vessels, boiler drums, headers and tube panels, at 550–800°C. Its arrangement retains oil heating and adds gas. “Both systems can operate independently” does not establish simultaneous co-firing; that mode is not described in the cited text.

Draw the open and closed air paths for oil and gas operation separately, marking shared fans, mains, flues and pressure-control points. If available pressure, flow or adjustment range cannot serve the new burners, revise utilities or combustion configuration. Previous furnace operation does not establish suitability for the new system.

## Changeover valves select branches; interlocks must cover shared services

The new gas main lists a manual ball valve, filter, regulator, vent valve, high/low pressure switches, flowmeter and fast shut-off valve. Low gas pressure or fan failure closes gas and raises an alarm. Automatic pre-ignition purging, flame monitoring and abnormal flame-loss shut-off are also described. Integrate these with the actual states of reused fans and old/new branches.

Before retrofit, equipment and site providers should define permitted fuel-changeover states, isolation of unused branches, valve-position and air-pressure feedback, fuel shutdown on shared fan or power failure, and recovery verification. Partial protection descriptions do not establish that all existing equipment has the necessary feedback. Put unknowns into the modification list and verification responsibilities rather than assuming the retained controls cover them.

Define gas-station handover, upstream/downstream regulator and burner-inlet pressures separately on the piping drawing. Historical furnace-side pressure statements differ; this article does not select one as a current procurement condition. Reconcile gas-station conditions, pipe pressure loss and burner requirements before determining regulation and valve scope. No unresolved operating pressure or purge duration is supplied here.

## Separate zoned heating from workpiece recording

The proposal has one zone per burner: 16 zones and 16 furnace monitoring points on the sides, distributed relative to the opposite burners. Separate workpiece junction boxes and 24-point recording are buyer options. Furnace-zone counts are not workpiece-point counts, and optional load recording cannot be assumed included in the base retrofit.

For post-weld heat treatment, provide workpiece structure, weld locations and required recording positions, then map these to loading and zones. Where quality depends on particular workpiece temperatures, corresponding load measurements are needed; zone records alone cannot replace them. The proposed effective-zone temperature difference of ≤±10°C at the end of holding is not evidence that every weld or core meets its requirement.

## What is reused, and what does Suneng propose to do?

Seller scope includes repairs around burners and the opening, natural-gas piping and valves, gas combustion, air branches and changeover devices, new gas controls and maintenance of existing oil controls. The structural section explicitly reuses the fan, air main and exhaust. Match the general “air-supply system” listing to actual repairs, replacement and installation; it does not establish a new fan.

Buyer scope includes foundations, utilities and related construction, power to the gas-control low-voltage cabinet, and replacement ageing cables for the oil controls. Recheck the gas-station handover location on site. The supplier provides retrofit system/structural drawings, electrical schematics and installation/wiring documents to make new/old responsibilities explicit.

Cold/hot commissioning, furnace uniformity, energy efficiency and flue-gas testing are proposed acceptance activities. The purpose of added gas and controls can be explained, but without tests and comparable operating conditions there is no basis for claims of quantified fuel savings, emission reductions or uniformity improvements. This is a retrofit approach and verification scope, not achieved project results.

## Two common retrofit questions

### Does retaining the flue remove the need to reassess furnace pressure?

No. Shared exhaust must be checked for actual capacity, control interfaces and combustion-interlock coordination in the proposed modes. If condition or regulation capability is unsuitable, revise the scope. Reuse describes equipment origin, not verified fitness.

### What information helps progress this retrofit?

Collect original fan, flue, valve and control drawings, operating records by mode and current equipment condition. Add intended workpieces, post-weld requirements, gas-station conditions and the permitted shutdown window. Use these to assess shared systems and changeover interfaces before planning construction, commissioning and verification.
