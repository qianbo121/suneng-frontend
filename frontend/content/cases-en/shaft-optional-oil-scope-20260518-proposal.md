Suneng's proposal dated 18 May 2026 for small metal shaft parts covers heating for oil quenching of materials including 40Cr, while listing washing and tempering equipment as buyer-supplied. The equipment schedule includes one oil tank, but the detailed structural description marks it as optional. The final scope must therefore define the tank and lift, oil-temperature recording, cooling water, and subsequent washing and tempering together. The heating-furnace name alone does not establish that the entire process is included.

## Confirm whether the oil tank is selected, then define its functions

| Item | Original proposal | Implication for supply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quench-heating furnace | 75 kW, three heating zones | Furnace control and downstream oil-temperature control concern separate objects |
| Oil tank and lift | One set in the equipment schedule; optional in the structural description | The adopted list must specify the tank, cooling, agitation and workpiece lift |
| Washing and tempering | Marked as buyer-supplied in the equipment schedule | Provide external equipment and process connections; do not assume inclusion |

The detailed oil-tank section lists a circulating cooling pump, vigorous agitation, a workpiece lift and a residual-oil blow-off fan. The control-cabinet scope also includes the quenching furnace, oil tank and lift. Selecting the optional oil tank therefore involves more than a mechanical checklist: connected motors, oil-temperature signals and alarms, and the external cooling-water connection must also be confirmed. If the buyer supplies a separate tank, the parties still need to identify their interface boundaries.

## Keep the three furnace temperatures separate from oil temperature

The heating furnace has three zones and three thermocouples. The oil-tank section separately lists one temperature-sensing and control thermocouple. The recording section specifies a four-channel paperless recorder for furnace and oil-tank temperatures. On this functional basis, the channel schedule should identify the connection, name and time correspondence for each of the three furnace zones and the oil tank. The fourth channel must not be treated as an installed workpiece-temperature channel.

The introductory reference to three-point furnace-temperature recording and the later four-channel recording of furnace and oil temperatures may describe different scopes. A numerical difference alone does not establish a conflict. Procurement must determine which signals will actually be connected. Any change in oil-tank selection should be reflected in the instruments and control-point schedule, rather than leaving a recording list without matching equipment.

## Buyer-supplied downstream equipment does not remove process requirements

The source process includes heating, oil quenching, washing and tempering, while washing and tempering are also described as optional or buyer-supplied. This is a supply boundary; it does not mean that washing or tempering may routinely be omitted after oil quenching 40Cr shafts. The process must follow the part requirements and adopted documents. Existing downstream equipment should be checked for receiving arrangements, permissible oil carry-over, loading format and capacity. The proposed heating-furnace figure of approximately 150 kg/h must not be presented as continuous whole-line output.

The effective heating dimensions are 300 × 3500 × 100 mm, recorded as width × length × height. Fit also depends on actual shaft placement and loading depth. A single part's length or weight cannot establish throughput. The proposal provides no completed hardness or distortion tests for this group of parts, so this article does not treat performance descriptions in the equipment introduction as delivered results.

## Oil quenching and the carbon-bearing atmosphere need defined interfaces

The source proposes dripping methanol or ethanol into the furnace, where it decomposes to form a protective atmosphere, and describes a water-cooled return passage for the mesh belt. For a comparable purchase, design documents should also define atmosphere supply and shutdown conditions, extraction at the oil-entry opening, responses to abnormal oil level or temperature, and loss of cooling water. General overtemperature-alarm wording does not prove that all these specific interfaces are included. An oil tank with an unsettled scope cannot be regarded as a complete oil-quenching system.

Suneng proposes installation and commissioning of the supplied equipment. The buyer is responsible for foundations, the cooling-water source and main pipes and valves, quenching medium, trial materials, and secondary cables and conduits between equipment and control cabinet. Initial discussions should provide shaft materials and dimensions, loading arrangements, quality requirements and details of existing washing and tempering equipment. Then confirm the oil-tank selection and mechanical, control and utility interfaces to create an executable division of work.
