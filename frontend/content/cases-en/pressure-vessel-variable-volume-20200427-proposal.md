A variable-volume furnace's effective heating space depends on alignment between the movable rear wall, seals and selected heating zones. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s proposal dated 27 April 2020 for pressure vessels and other welded parts fixes the rear wall to the bogie frame. Bogie positioning creates 5 m, 10 m or 15 m heated lengths, with rear-wall compression seals and volume-setting interlocks. Check loading, seal contact and burner control for each mode. Selecting a shorter length alone does not establish verified operation for small parts.

## Three effective lengths created by rear-wall positioning

The proposal is for post-weld stress relief, with effective zones of 5 × 7 × 7 m, 10 × 7 × 7 m and 15 × 7 × 7 m. Length changes; width and height remain 7 m. Physical chamber dimensions are separately 15.3 × 7.3 × 7.15 m. External equipment dimensions, physical chamber and effective zone serve different purposes; none directly replaces the loaded workpiece-and-support envelope.

| Items to align when changing volume | Historical proposal basis | Procurement confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rear-wall position | Fixed to the bogie frame; moves with the bogie into compression | Bogie position and loading region for each effective length. |
| Rear-wall seals | Pneumatic pushrods press refractory-fibre blocks against chamber walls | Contact, compression and feedback at each position. |
| Heating/control | Select the corresponding heating region; volume settings and interlocks | Burners, temperature signals and fault responses for that region. |

For a short part in the 5 m mode, start with the complete workpiece/support layout, then confirm that the wall forms the required boundary and selected zones/sensors cover the load. If parts or supports extend beyond the effective zone, switching off burners alone cannot preserve the original uniformity definition.

## Preserve the seal relationships between rear wall, bogie and front door

The source separately describes front-door seals against furnace/bogie, bogie side seals and furnace/rear-wall seals. Structural gaps above and beside the rear wall are closed by pushrods and sealing blocks. Check each contact surface; one travel-switch “in position” signal is not complete sealing verification.

Bogie travel is interlocked with side seals, door and rear wall; raised-door safety pins are proposed. Actual drawings must define compressed/released states, bogie position and permissible actions. Cylinder numbers and seal groups require approved drawings, not a single isolated quantity statement.

## Confirm zones and workpiece sensors for every volume

Operating temperature 800°C and maximum design temperature 850°C are distinct. Determine welded-part treatment curves from material, structure and process requirements; maximum design temperature is not the routine process setting.

The proposal has 12 furnace-control zones, one per burner, plus 16 workpiece-temperature channels for display, recording and alarms. For 5 m or 10 m operation, define enabled burners, furnace points and required load points. Do not scale their counts by length or automatically claim that the 16 monitoring channels regulate temperature.

The proposed holding accuracy ≤±10°C is conditioned on temperatures above 550°C and effective-zone measurement at the end of holding. It is not evidence of testing in all three modes. If multiple modes are required, define each mode's effective region, loading, sensors, test temperature and assessment period. One mode's record cannot verify every mode.

## The concept does not establish a fuel-saving percentage

Selecting heated length for the workpiece is a proposed design direction. Gas use also depends on loading, curve, temperature, sealing and start/stop operation. A percentage length reduction is not a gas-saving percentage. Cold/hot commissioning, uniformity and efficiency tests are proposed acceptance tasks, not measured achievements.

Gas fast shut-off, flame detection and air/gas-pressure alarm protection are also provided in the proposal. Changing zones requires corresponding gas, combustion-air, exhaust and furnace-pressure conditions, with switching logic confirmed in control documents. Volume adjustment must include operating conditions and fault interlocks, not just space.

Provide representative long, medium and short workpiece drawings, supports/loading, post-weld requirements and site energy/layout. Suneng's proposed scope includes furnace, front door, rear wall, bogie, seals and associated heating/control. Compare space, movement, temperature and acceptance mode by mode before choosing reusable configurations.

The buyer supplies foundations, buildings and utilities. Blocks and fixtures are also buyer-supplied, but the seller designs them using buyer-provided product geometry. That design responsibility applies to blocks and fixtures. To assess support positions across modes, hand over geometry and load distribution during design, not total weight alone.
