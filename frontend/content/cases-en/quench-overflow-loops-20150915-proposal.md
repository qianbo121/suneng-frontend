A 2015 group of Suneng quench-oil, water-tank and associated storage-tank documents separately describes in-tank agitation, overflow storage and external circulation. Selection should identify where workpieces cool, where displaced liquid goes and which equipment removes heat from circulation. Storage volume cannot simply be added to usable workpiece-quenching space.

## Distinguish external volume, liquid fill and usable quench space

The ZHC-255 draft gives external dimensions of 7000 × 4500 × 11000 mm and liquid fill of 255 m³. The CYC-50 storage tank separately lists 5000 × 4000 × 3000 mm and 50 m³ of stored liquid. External dimensions describe installation space; liquid fill describes proposed medium quantity. They are not interchangeable. Full immersion of workpieces with lifting fixtures also requires clear internal dimensions, supports and working level.

The source links overflow storage to workpiece entry, liquid-temperature rise and supply state. Draw normal working level and the level after immersion separately, then check overflow openings, connecting pipes and storage reserve. A 50 m³ tank appears in both the 18 m³ and 255 m³ main-tank drafts. Current evidence does not establish separate tanks or a shared arrangement; general drawings and equipment lists must reconcile it.

## Agitation moves liquid; heat exchange needs a heat-removal path

The six-agitator version places three propeller agitators on each long side, circulating liquid through guiding structures. A separate heat-exchange section lists a 200–250 m² spiral-plate exchanger, filter and circulation pump. The agitators address internal flow; the exchanger needs a cooling-side medium. Agitator operation or pump flow alone does not establish temperature recovery through repeated furnace batches.

The group also has a four-agitator ZHC-255 version, whose relevant section lists a circulation pump, piping and storage circulation without expressly including the same exchanger as the six-agitator version. Retain it as a separate version. Six agitators, older electrical power and a new exchanger cannot be assembled into one definite list. Select the governing version before confirming flow paths, cooling-side conditions and responsibilities.

## Platforms, sludge baskets and piping occupy internal space

The proposal uses a load-bearing platform for workpieces and racks, with bottom flow openings, a sludge-removal basket and fall-protection structures. Actual fit therefore needs a section showing platform height, space below parts and flow outlets. Comparing external tank dimensions with part length is insufficient. The source also mixes support capacity, single-quench weight and per-hour units; this page derives no new net-workpiece tonnage.

The CYC-50 supply table lists rectangular oil and water storage tanks separately. The buyer provides foundations and utilities, while the supplier provides foundation, embedded-part and piping design information. Enquiries to Suneng can first list main tank, storage tank, exchanger, cooling-side equipment and connecting pipes, then assign design, supply and site construction for each. Providing foundation data does not include foundation construction.

## Acceptance must distinguish structure, level and repeated cooling

The draft specifies a 120-hour leak test during fabrication and, after completion, 72 hours fully loaded with no deformation or leakage. These address tank condition, not workpiece-loaded temperature, circulation and cooling performance. Procurement should also establish medium, parts and lifting fixtures, loading intervals and cooling-side operation so that each criterion has a defined test object.
