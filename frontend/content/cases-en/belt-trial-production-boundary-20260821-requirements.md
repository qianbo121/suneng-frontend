A project requirements record lists both a production mesh-belt furnace and a small experimental mesh-belt furnace. Both are intended to reproduce the same heating, holding and binder-removal conditions, but the experimental unit has no capacity requirement, and production loading and target output remain unconfirmed. After successful small-specimen trials, production loading, effective heated length and exhaust-maintenance conditions still need assessment. Equipment size alone cannot establish production capacity.

## Shared process conditions do not mean identical equipment tasks

The record assigns the experimental furnace to small-specimen verification, temperature-curve development, and atmosphere and binder-removal trials; the production furnace is for batch-volume operation. Retain actual trial materials, form, weight, fixtures and temperature records, and identify which conditions are to continue in production and which change with loading density and continuous feeding. Without a capacity assessment for the experimental furnace, acceptable specimens do not establish hourly production-furnace output.

## Overall length of 10–15 m is not effective heated length

The source gives 10–15 m as total furnace length and states that heating and cooling zones still need detail. It separately estimates belt speeds assuming 10 m or 15 m of effective heated length. That assumption cannot simply be applied to overall machine dimensions. First separate heating ramps, the two holding stages, atmosphere-isolation sections and cooling on the layout, then check speed using actual effective lengths and process times.

The approximately 158–175 minutes in the record is an estimated heat-treatment duration based on an assumed initial temperature and heating rates. It expressly excludes entry/exit atmosphere isolation and detailed cooling time. It is neither a measured feed-to-discharge line cycle nor a basis for increasing belt speed to meet an undefined output target.

## Observe binder removal in trials and provide cleaning access in the layout

The record specifically notes that exhaust deposits on internal duct walls as it cools. It therefore proposes removable sections or inspection openings and space for periodic cleaning. Trials can provide design input on actual material emissions and deposition. Production-furnace review must additionally consider sustained feeding, duct routes, cleaning access and connections to plant treatment facilities. The ability to connect an outgoing duct does not complete the exhaust-system design.

Material names and release percentages reflect the requirements described at the time and still need actual material documentation. Nitrogen and a small amount of nitrogen/hydrogen gas are described as alternatives; oxygen content, hydrogen fraction and corresponding system conditions remain undecided. This article adopts neither as a confirmed process and supplies no gas-mixing or emissions-treatment parameters.

## Establish the basis of operating-cost estimates, then check real loading

The production-furnace record provisionally estimates electricity using 220 kW installed power, while expressly requiring steady-operation power to be calculated from loading, insulation and heat losses. Procurement can use this to prepare site electrical conditions, but installed power is not fixed hourly consumption. Nor can a multiple of the experimental furnace's estimated power determine production-furnace pricing. Final configurations and responsibilities for both units require separate technical documents after actual specimens and production targets are confirmed.
