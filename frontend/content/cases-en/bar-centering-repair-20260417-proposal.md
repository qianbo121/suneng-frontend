A bar-heating walking-beam furnace retrofit adds inlet length measurement and centring, while levelling the walking beams and adding side guides and a discharge limit. These address entry position, movement inside the furnace and the discharge boundary respectively. Check them along the route of an individual bar.

## Establish where the bar loses its intended position

The project uses a walking-beam furnace with two moving and three stationary hearth beams, a single row of bars, end feeding and internal roller discharge. The agreement requires individual length measurement for bars 80–100 mm in diameter and 1400–1650 mm long. For a similar fault, distinguish misalignment before entry, drift during walking after successful centring, and overtravel beyond the intended outlet stop.

## Length measurement must refer to the same centreline as movement

The device outside the inlet measures bar length, then cylinders and push rods on both sides adjust its position across the furnace width. The draft connects distance measurement and cutting-machine data and requires measurement time and available batch information to be recorded. Acceptance with actual bars should establish which bar a recorded length belongs to, whether the centring movement acts on that bar and whether its final position permits entry.

First reconcile the mechanical centreline and software position datum. The early draft gives a 1900 mm working width, while the centring calculation uses 2400 mm furnace width and a 1200 mm centre. Drawings must establish the dimensions behind these references. The programmed 1200 mm is not automatically the installed centre, and one completed software movement does not prove correct centring.

Measurement requirements also differ: the functional section allows no more than 5 mm error, acceptance states ≤±10 mm, and the sensor table separately gives ±2 mm. Sensor accuracy, assembled measurement-system error and machine acceptance are distinct. Agree test bars, measuring positions and assessment basis before acceptance.

## Internal guides and the outlet limit do different jobs

Mechanical work includes levelling the walking beams, adding side rollers to locate and guide moving beams, and installing a discharge-end limit switch. The first two address stability of the mechanism and bar movement inside the furnace; the switch controls the moving beam's end position. Inlet centring cannot replace mechanical alignment, and an added outlet switch does not establish elimination of drift along the route.

Complete replacement of support blocks, cantilever-roller replacement and hydraulic refurbishment also relate to this route. For an existing furnace, check support contact faces, moving-beam travel, outlet roller positions and signals together in drawings and commissioning records. These are proposed repairs, not evidence that drift or support-block damage has already been eliminated.

## Independent controls still exchange signals with the old furnace

The new distance-measuring and centring system is independent of the original furnace controller but is not isolated from the production line. It must receive bar-in-position signals and identifiers from the cutting machine and return measurement-complete signals. Necessary signals are exchanged with the original controller through contacts or communications. Define what the old system provides, what the new system awaits and which conditions permit the next movement, then commission the interfaces together.

A future interface to the factory production-management system is reserved; the buyer develops that system. Providing length data and an interface does not establish a completed factory-wide platform. Early responsibilities assign new-system wiring, joint programme commissioning and backup delivery to Suneng, while the buyer must provide cooperation from the existing system.

## What establishes readiness for acceptance?

The agreement separates cold trials, covering movements, signals, interlocks and measuring/centring, from hot trials covering the furnace, support blocks, burners and temperatures. The 1100°C requirement concerns billet core temperature, and ±5°C concerns temperature along bar length. Neither can be replaced by distance-measurement accuracy or one furnace-instrument reading.

For similar work, provide records of where drift occurs, incoming sizes, existing support-block and beam drawings, inlet/outlet photographs and original control interfaces. Define position and movement boundaries before deciding replacement scope. The agreement requires a separate completion-acceptance report after acceptance; the technical agreement itself does not prove that these checks have occurred.
