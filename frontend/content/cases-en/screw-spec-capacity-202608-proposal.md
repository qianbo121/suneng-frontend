A series of Suneng screw mesh-belt line proposals changes the target from 500 kg/h to 300 kg/h and revises loading and residence conditions for each size. In the second round, calculations using a 900 mm belt and 9 m effective heated length still put the longer sizes below 300 kg/h. Equipment selection should therefore use the actual product mix, rather than applying nominal output to every screw.

## A target figure alone does not determine capacity

The first round ties 500 kg/h to representative ST4.8 × 38 screws, their material and case-depth requirements, loading of 40 kg/m² and steady production. The second round explicitly redesigns the line, reducing effective heated length of the co-diffusion furnace from 10 m to 9 m and proposed installed power from 250 kW to 200 kW, with changes to the tempering furnace and other equipment. These are design revisions for one project, not two verified production lines.

## Long screws require both loading and residence checks

The draft calculates output as belt width × loading mass per square metre × effective heated length ÷ furnace residence time. Loading mass and residence are both inputs requiring verification. Although the table uses the term actual capacity, this round provides proposal calculations without corresponding production-test records. The figures below show only the consequences of those assumptions; they are neither measured capabilities nor general process parameters.

For ST5.5 × 75, 34 kg/m² and 50 minutes give 0.9 × 34 × 9 ÷ (50/60), or approximately 330 kg/h. ST6.3 × 100 at 31 kg/m² and 55 minutes gives approximately 274 kg/h; ST6.3 × 115 at 28 kg/m² and 60 minutes gives approximately 227 kg/h. With the same belt width and heated length, the latter two sizes cannot simply be assigned 300 kg/h. These differences arise from the source inputs and do not establish universal loading densities or diffusion times for all long screws.

The first round also needs recalculation: 900 mm belt width, 40 kg/m², 10 m effective length and 45 minutes give approximately 480 kg/h by the same formula. These inputs alone do not substantiate 500 kg/h. Because residence time and other conditions differ between rounds, the two nominal figures cannot directly establish an actual percentage change in output after redesign.

## An 11 m option does not cover every long size

The second round offers 11 m and 12 m extensions. Retaining 28 kg/m² and 60 minutes for ST6.3 × 115 gives approximately 277 kg/h at 11 m. Geometrically, 300 kg/h requires approximately 11.9 m; 12 m gives approximately 302 kg/h. Whether 11 m suits the project depends on actual product shares and acceptable output. Another size exceeding 300 kg/h does not establish full coverage. Thermal behaviour, downstream tempering and transfer capacity after extension still require a final agreed proposal.

## Use effective production hours for monthly planning

The second round targets 75–100 tonnes/month with 16 hours/day of production and 8 hours of hot standby. Only sustained output of 300 kg/h throughout all 16 effective production hours gives 4.8 tonnes/day and approximately 15.6–20.8 production days. A high share of long screws, size changes, heating and waiting invalidate those days as a direct production commitment. Hot standby also does not establish that all production-restart time is included.

Before confirmation, provide actual proportions of each size, permissible loading and acceptance requirements. Representative loaded-trial records should then establish loading density, effective residence and acceptable output on a consistent basis. The source also leaves product acceptance standards and gas supply for confirmation. Its recommended material processes, atmosphere proportions and standby steps are not presented here as executable operating procedures.
