Retort leak testing and methanol dosing do not directly establish oxidation-free or decarburisation-free treatment. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s muffle mesh-belt furnace proposal dated 17 November 2024 combines a heat-resistant steel retort, supporting-plate/belt transport and methanol atmosphere supply in an oil-quenching/tempering line. Separate retort sealing, atmosphere supply and workpiece-quality verification when comparing equipment.

## Leak testing concerns the retort, not a direct product result

The retort is welded from Cr25Ni20 heat-resistant steel plate and requires a gas-tightness test. The belt uses heat-resistant wire; corundum balls beneath movable hearth plates support reciprocation that drives belt transport. Retort, transport and heaters have separate structural arrangements. The term “protective atmosphere” does not remove checks of seams, opening and discharge connections.

Request the retort-test scope, method and criteria, mapping the inlet, drop opening and atmosphere connections. A leak-test record describes only its agreed test scope. Workpiece oxidation, decarburisation and other surface criteria still need material, process and inspection evidence; retort grade cannot replace them.

## Reconcile two supply-quantity statements before ordering

The atmosphere section lists 2 solenoid valves and 2 methanol flowmeters; the later structural description lists 1 of each. Do not select one as final or add the counts together. The supplier must clarify whether they serve different interfaces and which version governs, then align piping, supply list and controls.

General overtemperature/broken-thermocouple alarms and control interlocks do not replace detailed actions for methanol supply, furnace openings and shutdown faults. Confirm supply permissions, stop conditions and the relationship between operation and protection. This article adds no flow settings or gas recipe, and a flowmeter is not atmosphere-quality detection.

## Check transfer and cooling ownership from oil tank to tempering furnace

The proposed sequence is manual loading, quench heating, sliding through the drop opening into the oil-tank elevator, transfer to the tempering inlet belt, and discharge into a basket after tempering. This is a proposed route, not evidence of continuous operation. Check actual part sizes/stacking through dropping, lifting and tempering entry; heating-section width alone does not establish passage through the line.

The oil section lists 40–80°C and says quench-fluid cooling uses existing buyer equipment or separate buyer supply. Later text describes an external closed cooling tower, oil pump and piping. Clarify whether these are interface requirements or included supply; structural description does not make the tower a default inclusion. Reconcile tank dimensions and the “≥7 m³” volume with effective liquid level and internal mechanisms.

## Retain both equipment and representative-workpiece acceptance records

Proposed output is approximately 100 kg/h, explicitly dependent on shape/dimensions, individual weight, stacking, metallurgical conditions and process. Final acceptance also checks deformation and hardness against agreement. If oxidation/decarburisation levels are required, define actual quality criteria first. Intended performance is not an achieved result.

Hot uniformity checking is specified at 800°C in the quench furnace, measuring three points in one zone to ≤±5°C. It does not establish uniformity at every process temperature or load. Buyer scope includes primary wiring and water/gas interfaces; seller scope includes secondary wiring/materials from cabinet to furnace electrical inputs. Match actual atmosphere and cooling conditions to trial records.
