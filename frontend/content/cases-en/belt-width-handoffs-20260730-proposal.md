A July 2026 Suneng roller-supported mesh-belt line proposal connects quenching, oil-tank lifting, oil removal, washing and tempering, with belt widths changing along the route. Conveyor review should examine where parts land, how they spread again and whether drive monitoring covers the actual handovers, as well as belt widths.

## Wider downstream belts still need a defined entry pattern

The proposal is a continuous quenching-and-tempering line for small and medium metal parts. After oil-quench lifting, parts pass through oil removal, immersion/spray washing and blow-drying before tempering. Belt widths are 1000 mm in quench heating, 1100 mm in the oil-tank lift, 1200 mm in oil removal, 1300 mm in washing and 1400 mm in tempering. One belt does not run through the entire line.

| Location | Proposed belt width | Handover to observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quench-heating furnace | 1000 mm | Landing area in the oil-tank lift section |
| Washer | 1300 mm | Distribution into immersion, spraying and blow-drying after oil removal |
| Tempering furnace | 1400 mm | Distribution at the tempering inlet after washing |

Wider downstream sections alone do not establish even spreading. Drops, landing positions, guides and relative speeds determine central concentration, edge losses and inlet piling. The draft requires the oil-tank lift to prevent dropped parts and belt jamming but lacks a complete handover drawing for the representative parts. Check the smallest parts, parts prone to interlocking and actual layer depth.

## Effective height and treatment time depend on loading

This named proposal gives effective zones of 10000 × 1000 × 150 mm for quenching and 14000 × 1400 × 150 mm for tempering. The 150 mm is available effective height, not permission to pile any load to that depth while retaining process results. Representative dimensions, arrangement, thickness and process curves require buyer confirmation.

The listed transit ranges are 30–150 min for quenching and 40–200 min for tempering, while oil-removal speed must match adjacent stages. Accelerating one section to eliminate an inlet pile requires reassessment of its immersion, washing, drying or heating time. Confirm throughput section by section under actual loading.

## Shaft rotation does not establish every successful handover

The quenching furnace uses roller-driven mesh transport, with synchronised two-stage drives for preheating and heating. Drive shafts have rotation detection and the belt has tracking alarms. Oil-tank, oil-removal, washing and tempering sections separately list their own drives and protection. These signals describe mechanism states but do not monitor the same objects.

A rotating shaft alone proves neither absence of belt slip nor absence of bridged parts at a chute or successful entry to the next section. Mark monitoring points on a conveyor drawing: drive monitoring, belt monitoring and handovers needing accumulation or missing-part detection. The draft calls for sequential upstream drive interlocking but supplies no complete accumulation-detection and fault-response table. That phrase does not establish automatic detection of every workpiece blockage.

## Use representative parts and define site and treatment boundaries

The proposal uses approximately 1000 kg/h as a design reference and explicitly links output to parts, layer loading and process time. Checks should cover continuous conveying, every handover, washed surface condition and the full heat-treatment route. Acceptable empty-furnace temperatures and representative loaded production are different evidence.

The main text calls for 72 h trial production, while the assessment table specifies 24 h continuous loaded operation. Reconcile test duration, loading and fault statistics before acceptance; the shorter period cannot simply be selected as an agreed standard.

The supply table assigns equipment units and secondary cabinet-to-furnace wiring to Suneng, with installation guidance and commissioning. The buyer arranges foundations/pits, unloading and placement, primary wiring and external water, power and gas connections. Exhaust and wastewater need interfaces with the industrial park's external treatment facilities. This historical interface description does not make a complete package include every site construction and external treatment activity.
