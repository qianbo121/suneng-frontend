Two Suneng discussion drafts for the same superalloy tube-and-plate project propose a sloping-hearth bar-heating furnace and a bell-type plate/coil annealing furnace respectively. The first connects turnover feeding, internal rolling and push-out rollers; the second relies on an overhead crane for loading and bell movement. Site compatibility begins with these two different handling routes.

## Distinguish equipment by workpiece form

The bar proposal lists diameters of 40–120 mm and lengths of 1000–1800 mm for heating in a gas-fired sloping-hearth furnace. Plates and strip coils correspond to a resistance annealing furnace with an inner bell. Both belong to the same construction project, but the documents do not establish that every product passes through both machines or that the furnaces are interchangeable.

## Link bar positioning, side discharge and pusher cooling

Bars are positioned on a V-roller conveyor by a stop, turned into the furnace, moved along the proposed sloping hearth to the discharge groove and pushed out. The source uses a screw-adjusted stop for length setting and a hollow, water-cooled discharge pusher. Length changes require checks of positioning, turnover and push-out endpoint together, rather than chamber fit alone.

The draft has two descriptions of the external discharge-roller supply boundary, already listed for clarification. Mark Suneng's supply endpoint, the buyer's receiving start, roller elevation and handover state on the layout. A roller conveyor beside the outlet does not establish interface, drive and control responsibilities.

Pusher cooling is another interface. Responsibility for the circulating-water station needs confirmation because included in seller scope and optional remain unreconciled. Agree the water source, supply/return connections and equipment-side monitoring together. A supplied water-cooled pusher does not imply that the external cooling station is included in the same price.

## Furnace loading capacity and crane capacity differ

The plate/coil draft lists a maximum furnace load of 30 t, loading height no greater than 1500 mm and staged handling of the inner and outer bells. Furnace loading describes the hearth's proposed load. Crane assessment must separately consider actual bells, workpieces and lifting-fixture weights, lift height, travel and landing space. The 30 t value is not a selection conclusion for every lift, nor are bells and the full workpiece load necessarily lifted together.

The proposed seal uses double grooves at the bell base, fibre rope and self-weight compression. Landing and positioning should be checked with sealing: placing a bell on the platform does not mean it has passed a leak test. The draft separately assigns inner bell, locating provisions, lifting fixtures and outer-bell stand to the seller, with the crane buyer-supplied. Check each item in the handover list.

## Batch time includes cooling and crane waits

The bell-furnace draft provisionally assumes natural cooling and bell removal by the buyer's crane. Rapid cooling, a cooling bell or an independent cooling station needs separate confirmation. Batch turnaround should include loading, bell placement, heating/holding, cooling, bell removal and unloading in sequence, then account for any other equipment using the crane. Empty-furnace heating time is not a complete loaded-batch cycle.

Both drafts leave representative materials, processes and utilities for confirmation. Gas capacity, electrical power and atmosphere suggestions are not signed-off configurations. Similar projects should first provide actual part drawings, treatment objectives, production rhythm and workshop handling plans, then define each machine's energy and process conditions separately.

## Use two interface drawings for procurement

One drawing can trace bars from the loading rack to the external receiving rollers; the other can cover plate/coil loading, inner/outer bell lifts and cooling/standing positions. At every handover, define supply, installation, cooperation and acceptance responsibilities, then check cold movements and loaded processes with actual workpieces. This comparison describes proposed operating routes, not a record of the project entering production.
