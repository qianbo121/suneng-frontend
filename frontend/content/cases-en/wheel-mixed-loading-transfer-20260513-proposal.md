A Suneng discussion draft for continuous wheel heat treatment proposes basket-free, three-level roller-hearth transport, mixed-size feeding and an alignment mechanism at each discharge level. Assessing suitability requires tracing feed arrangement, transfer between levels, immersion after heating, water draining and entry to the ageing furnace. Nominal row counts alone do not establish actual capacity.

## Define each row's arrangement before counting its parts

The draft distinguishes quantity-based control for identical sizes from available-space control for mixed sizes. If the last position has insufficient remaining space for the next wheel, that wheel is to be returned. It also describes a nominal arrangement of 5 columns × 3 levels and a mixed-load arrangement of 4–6 columns × 3 levels. Procurement should establish the product combinations behind each description, rather than assuming that every size permits 15 wheels per transfer.

## Basket-free transport still needs support checks for every wheel

The source uses rollers, with separate feed, heating and discharge drives on each level, and proposes support by multiple rollers per wheel. Removing baskets removes one type of reusable fixture; it does not remove the need to assess different diameters, rim geometries and orientations. Actual wheel drawings can establish contact points, transitions between drive sections and whether alignment changes the intended orientation. One roller's load rating is not the capacity of a complete level or line.

## Alignment on three levels must connect to shared transfer

The alignment mechanisms are at the solution-treatment furnace outlet, and quench-transfer timing starts with door movement and ends with complete product immersion. Verification should use the agreed arrangement and record the handover between each level's discharge, receiving by the lifting frame and descent. Complete immersion of the last part is the end condition. Neither empty-frame descent nor first-part immersion is an adequate substitute.

After quenching, the proposed draining mechanism tilts wheels on all three levels simultaneously by approximately 60–70°, then sends them to the ageing-furnace staging rack. Water-trapping locations, positioning during tilting and downstream receiving orientation require checks with actual wheel sizes. A tilt angle on a drawing does not establish absence of retained water or movement for every size.

## Use one counting basis for output, mixed loads and tests

Cycle-time units, nominal quantity per operation and hourly output in this draft cannot yet be reconciled. This page therefore does not adopt its annual-capacity or cycle promises. Later technical confirmation should define whether one operation means one row or one lift cycle, whether counts combine all three levels, how different wheel sizes are counted, and how returns, rearrangement and downtime are recorded. Include these rules in line testing.

The source also lists reversals, added sections and merging of existing inlet/outlet logistics, plus modification of the original control cabinet. These site works affect continuous material flow and need responsibilities defined alongside the new-equipment list. Civil subcontracting and customer foundation preparation are described differently and require a final scope table. A description of the whole line does not automatically include all site engineering.
