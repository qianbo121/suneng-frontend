Suneng's mesh-belt heat-treatment line proposal uses initial air blow-off/de-oiling at the oil-tank elevator exit, followed by detergent washing, rinsing and blow-off before tempering. Separate carried-over quench oil, washing and residual cleaning liquid. A blower does not replace cleaning-performance inspection, and automatic water replenishment does not prove consistently acceptable cleaning-fluid condition.

## Distinguish stages by what they remove

| Stage | Proposal | Review object |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oil-tank elevator exit | Blower for initial de-oiling | Actual oil carryover and downstream cleaning burden |
| Washing | 2 immersion washing machines; process shows detergent wash and rinse | Each machine's medium, cleaning and rinsing duty |
| Final blow-off | Removes adhering cleaning liquid above the lifting belt | Residual liquid on surfaces and in retention-prone features |
| Bath management | Oil/water separation, automatic water top-up and temperature measurement/control | Surface oil, water level and temperature separately; not a cleanliness judgement |

The list specifies two immersion washers, the process diagram separates detergent washing and rinsing, and an individual-machine description summarises detergent washing, rinsing and blow-off as three working zones. These can coexist but do not establish identical sprays, baths and fans inside both machines. Define each machine's duty, medium and connections in layout and itemised lists. Two machines do not necessarily mean two identical complete cleaning sequences.

## Oil separation, top-up and temperature control are distinct

The proposal combines industrial detergent and pressurised nozzle washing, with an oil/water separator removing contaminated surface oil. The tank has automatic top-up and fluid-temperature control. These manage oil, level and temperature respectively. Restoring water level does not establish restored concentration, contamination condition or cleaning capability. For acceptance, specify incoming workpiece oil contamination, intended medium and replacement/maintenance conditions, then verify against actual surface results.

No definitive spray-pump model or power is adopted here: the overview gives 2.2 kW, while later text gives 3.0 kW and retains “quench-oil” pipeline-pump wording. The supplier must confirm a list appropriate to the cleaning medium before pump selection or supply pricing. A washer heading does not make all descriptions beneath it consistent.

## Check actual residual liquid at the tempering inlet

Blow-off above the washing elevator precedes transfer to tempering. For holes, recesses or overlapping parts, inspect likely liquid pockets from actual geometry, then assess the proposed blowing method and loading orientation. The source's “blown clean” is not reported as a completed drying test, and no universal residual-water limit is invented for different parts.

Final acceptance output of approximately 500 kg/h remains conditional on shape, individual weight, piling and process, with separate distortion and hardness checks. If cleaning is a key deliverable, add surface-oil and residual-liquid criteria and inspection methods. Whole-line temperature records or continuous discharge do not establish cleaning conformity.

## Match supply and site preparation to the actual media

The sample includes quenching, oil-tank lifting, washing, tempering and controls. The buyer provides agreed site water/gas connections and primary/secondary wiring; the seller provides lists and undertakes equipment positioning and installation. Confirm contaminated-oil and drainage connections, collection destinations and equipment-side limits. A tank drain does not include workshop treatment facilities. Provide workpiece geometry, incoming oil condition, cleaning objectives and site drainage conditions to assess the two-stage arrangement.
