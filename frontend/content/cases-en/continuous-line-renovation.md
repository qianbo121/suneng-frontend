Suneng's 2015 retrofit documents for three 1250 mm continuous annealing and pickling lines propose changing from natural gas to cooled fuel gas, renewing fuel pipes and burners, adjusting controls and modifying the solution-treatment section. They treat fuel, combustion air and exhaust differently: fuel lines are enlarged and renewed, combustion-air ducts partly modified and reused, and external exhaust ducts recommended for retention with a new induced-draught fan. Fuel conversion is more than burner replacement, and reuse still requires review of the existing system.

## Apply separate reuse decisions to the three systems

| System | Original proposal | Purchasing check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fuel main, branches and burners | Convert to cooled fuel gas; enlarge and renew pipes | Gas quality, pressure, flows at different loads and valve-group matching |
| Combustion air | Existing supply judged adequate by proposal calculations, with local changes for zone power | Actual fan condition, pressure/flow and new zone interfaces |
| External exhaust | Retain compactly arranged ducts; replace 30 kW induced-draught fan with 75 kW unit | Flue-gas flow, temperature, resistance and fan operating point |
| Project and zoning | 3 lines; 5 heating chambers and 10 control zones per furnace proposed | Interfaces and shutdown windows for each line |

The economic analysis uses calorific values of 8500 kcal/Nm³ for natural gas and 1450 kcal/Nm³ for cooled fuel gas. To provide similar heat input, it proposes approximately 5–6 times the original fuel-gas volume. This describes the historical adaptation approach, not current gas composition, calorific-value variation or pressure. The proposal also provides terminal drainage arrangements for moisture and tar. Flow through an old main alone does not establish suitability.

## Use the same operating basis for fuel and exhaust quantities

The documents calculate gas consumption and flue-gas quantity separately for rated output, maximum capacity and all burners at maximum configuration. These concern different duties. Select an exhaust fan against a defined load, gas temperature and duct resistance. All-burners-open gas demand is not normal consumption per tonne of steel. Increasing motor power from 30 kW to 75 kW alone does not establish extraction performance, energy savings or emissions compliance.

Combustion air and exhaust are separate systems. Air-duct retention rests on the proposed air-volume calculation and local adjustments; exhaust-duct retention includes the condition of fan replacement. Obtain existing fan data, duct layouts and operating condition, then compare them with the new combustion configuration. These reviews cannot substitute for each other.

## Relate chambers, zones and strip-temperature measurements

The retrofit changes 13 control zones to five heating chambers separated by disc rolls, with ten control zones. Each chamber receives an oxygen-measurement system. The source explicitly uses these signals for air/fuel-ratio tracking and correction within limits. This is proposed control equipment, not evidence of achieved uniformity or zero emissions. High-temperature strip measurement at the furnace outlet and low-temperature measurement at the solution-section outlet also concern different locations. One furnace-temperature curve cannot replace both strip readings.

## Compare supply scope and phased implementation

Work extends beyond combustion to local lining repairs, solution-section air-cooling hoods, mist cooling, sprays, drying and extraction locations. Cable removal, purchasing and wiring are buyer responsibilities, requiring coordination with the seller's control retrofit and commissioning assistance. The agreement phases the first line separately from the other two, with dates determined by the buyer according to site conditions. It does not promise the same restart interval for every existing furnace.

The economics use historical fuel prices, output and operating time. They are proposed calculations, not achieved energy savings or business returns. Reuse the system-by-system review method, then form conclusions from comparable current energy metering, accepted output, emissions tests and trial records. Contract and technical-agreement obligations define work and inspection scope but do not replace delivery or acceptance evidence.

## Verify results from operating records

This proposal covers three 1250 mm continuous annealing and pickling lines. Economic conclusions require comparable operating records linking fuel measurement, accepted output and conditions; unverified calculations are not public performance results. Emissions compliance requires qualified third-party testing or a formal acceptance report. Fan power, design configuration or historical contracts cannot establish it. This page explains the proposal and verification method, not completion or acceptance of all three retrofits.
