A group of forged-ball line retrofit documents covers bar discharge, air cooling before and after ball tempering, a tempering furnace and bagging equipment. Individual equipment operation must be followed by confirmation of old control programmes, station elevations, residence times and bagging handovers.

## Map the interfaces between new work and existing equipment

The walking-beam furnace retrofit concerns bars 4500–6000 mm long and 80–105 mm in diameter, with proposed internal cantilever-roller discharge. A separate agreement covers equipment before and after tempering forged balls of 110, 125 and 140 mm diameter. These documents address bar heating and ball heat treatment respectively. They do not make unlisted intermediate forming and transfer operations part of Suneng's new supply.

| Project section | Agreed proposal | Interface condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bar discharge | 8 cantilever-roller sets: 6 inside, 2 outside | Original furnace programme and discharge sequence |
| Pre-tempering air cooling and tempering | 10 m effective cooling section + 8 m effective tempering section | Shared chain speed and station elevations |
| Post-tempering air cooling | 2 units, each 15 m effective length | Existing ball tracks and loading equipment |

The agreement also lists approximately 50 m of new ball tracks, to be designed and installed to site conditions. For similar projects, first establish discharge direction, support/receiving method, height and maintenance space at every handover, then position equipment. Total track length does not identify which existing stations it can connect.

## Access to the old programme affects integration

Control modification for the new cantilever rollers depends on an unencrypted original programme supplied by the buyer. Before work, confirm that it is usable, modification is permitted and relevant actions can be interlocked with the new discharge mechanism. Cabinet or signal photographs are not equivalent to having the programme and conditions needed for complete commissioning.

The walking-beam agreement renews only specified walls, partition lining and discharge structures. Other sections, including roof lining, are reused, and the buyer supplies repair material for moving-beam support heads. Once the new rollers run, handovers still need checks with the existing walking action and repaired supports. Local repair is not complete replacement of the furnace.

## Shared chain speed links the 10 m cooling and 8 m tempering times

The pre-tempering cooler and furnace form one unit, with chain speed of 50–150 mm/min. Effective length divided by speed gives approximately 66.7–200 minutes for 10 m cooling and 53.3–160 minutes for 8 m tempering; the original table rounds these to 65–200 and 50–160 minutes. Both vary with the same speed. Time endpoints that do not correspond cannot be chosen independently.

These are geometric transit times through effective sections, not equal-duration holding after every ball reaches its target temperature. The agreement specifies cooling from 160°C to ≤80°C before low-temperature tempering at ≤300°C. These project conditions need checks against actual ball diameter, arrival temperature and loading, rather than being used as a universal process for every steel-ball material.

Likewise, 7 t/h is the agreed design capacity for the three 110, 125 and 140 mm ball sizes. Size-specific operating records must establish simultaneous compliance with cooling, tempering and output. Faster transport or two post-tempering coolers does not establish doubled whole-line capacity.

## Furnace temperature, ball-surface temperature and bag weight differ

The measurement table separates inserted Type K furnace thermocouples in tempering zones from infrared instruments in cooling sections. Each tempering section has one thermocouple for measurement and one for checking. Furnace readings and ball temperature must be distinguished, and an infrared surface reading does not establish core temperature. Acceptance also requires specified sample parts, measuring locations and product inspections.

Bagging requires automatic weighing, ball top-up and full-bag discharge, while bag attachment, removal and stacking remain manual. Packing accuracy is stated as ±1 ball for 1-tonne and 2-tonne bags. Weight verification should account for individual ball mass and bag tare; one ball cannot be converted to a fixed kilogram tolerance for every size. Manual bag changing and stacking also belong in continuous-receiving planning.

## Match installation and acceptance to each equipment section

The air-cooling/tempering agreement divides electrical supply: the buyer provides the plant supply to the control-cabinet main switch; Suneng supplies and installs cables and auxiliary materials from the cabinet to equipment interfaces. The buyer constructs foundations, cable trenches and pipe trenches using Suneng's foundation drawings. This defines whether power, routes and foundations are ready at new stations.

Walking-beam trials must check communications/interlocks between new mechanisms and the original system, cold movements and hot stability. Air-cooling/tempering equipment requires pre-shipment acceptance, trial production after installation and commissioning, and final acceptance to technical indicators after one month of normal use. These are planned inspections, not evidence that signing the agreements completed delivery or final acceptance.

Similar enquiries should include the existing layout, station inlet/outlet elevations, bar and ball sizes, original programme availability, and bag-changing/stacking arrangements. Identify retained equipment and altered handovers before finalising supply and trial-production plans.
