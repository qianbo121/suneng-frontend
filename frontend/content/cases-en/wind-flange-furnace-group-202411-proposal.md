A group of Suneng proposals for wind-power flange production includes 5 gas-fired chamber furnaces and one bogie-hearth electric furnace in each of two sizes. Equipment quantities can be totalled, but loading, temperature control and acceptance must remain linked to individual furnaces. Even the five equally sized gas furnaces have two different temperature-testing groups; one acceptable result cannot generically represent the entire purchase.

The group totals 7 proposed furnaces: 5 gas furnaces and two differently sized electric furnaces. That total establishes neither interchangeability nor a requirement for parts to pass through every furnace.

The gas furnaces list usual operation at 900–1280°C and maximum use at 1300°C. Both electric furnaces list maximum use at 1000°C. These are equipment-specific proposal ranges, not heat-treatment curves for arbitrary flange grades.

## Dimensions and loads excluding support blocks are conditional

All three parameter groups state that effective working dimensions exclude support-block height and load capacity excludes their weight. For actual flanges or ingots, list part envelope, support-block height, workpiece mass and block mass separately, then match them to loading drawings, supporting structures and charging-machine access. Both electric furnaces have 3.5 m effective height, but their widths, lengths and capacities differ. Height alone cannot establish fit of the same loaded arrangement.

Electric-furnace descriptions include workpiece support seats and hearth plates covering the bottom heating elements. Show the actual contacts between seats, blocks and plates on drawings. Existing racks with changed spans or support points require a new comparison. Proposal tonnage does not establish that any local contact can withstand the full load.

## Assign each of the five gas furnaces to its testing group

The gas-furnace parameter page specifies testing of 2 units to AMS 2750H and 3 to GB/T 30824, empty and by a qualified third party. It also lists furnace-temperature uniformity of ≤±14°C. These are the proposal's testing arrangements; the standard names do not establish certification or passed reports.

The final schedule should link the groups to equipment numbers, test zones, temperatures, records and responsibility for reports. Identical external dimensions do not justify one result with undefined coverage for all five. The two electric furnaces separately specify empty-furnace uniformity of ≤±10°C and AMS 2750H-related requirements. Retain their own agreements rather than applying gas-furnace grouping or values.

## Central management must preserve separate controls and records

The 13 m electric furnace proposes 3600 kW and 16 zones; the 11 m furnace proposes 2800 kW and 14 zones. Each has local temperature controls linked to central management. The draft calls for temperature curves, process alarms and electricity records. Acceptance and traceability should distinguish furnace number, batch and zone. Displaying both furnaces centrally does not make measuring points, zoning or abnormal-event records identical.

## Include site provisions and staged checks in the equipment schedule

The two electric-furnace quotations include design, manufacture, transport, installation and commissioning, but expressly exclude secondary power cables and cable trays between furnace and control cabinet, and equipment foundations/construction. Delivery to site therefore does not establish readiness for energising. Allocate cabling and foundation responsibility according to each furnace's position and capacity.

The gas-furnace documents separately define gas, compressed-air and exhaust interfaces and propose cold, hot and performance tests. Each furnace also requires mechanical/electrical documents, operating/maintenance information and energy-interface parameters. Similar furnace-group projects should first assemble an equipment-by-equipment list, workpiece/support-block layouts, testing groups and utility connections, then review final supply and acceptance plans. All these arrangements remain proposal requirements, not records of seven completed deliveries or acceptances.
