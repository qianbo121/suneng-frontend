Single-batch workpiece capacity is not enough to establish the interval between successive quenches. Suneng's 2016 quench-bath proposal lists a rated batch of 1.5 t, quenching-fluid temperature rise and cooling time, with agitation and a separate heat-exchange circuit. Check the incoming heat load, fluid conditions and next-batch arrival time together; do not schedule continuous production from 1.5 t alone.

## Specify batch capacity and batch interval separately

| Parameter | Historical specification | Essential conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rated quench load per batch | 1.5 t | Confirm actual workpiece material, entry temperature and treatment of fixture weight |
| Quenching-fluid temperature rise | ≤18°C | Must correspond to the same load and initial fluid conditions |
| Quenching-fluid cooling time | ≤2.5 h | Confirm cooling start/end conditions and external cooling-water conditions |

The source also lists a rated fluid operating temperature of ≤50°C and a maximum of ≤60°C. These cannot simply be combined into a cycle that “starts at 50°C, rises by 18°C and stays below 60°C”. Nor does ≤2.5 h establish hourly output. Define each parameter's initial fluid temperature, actual fluid quantity, incoming load and cooling endpoint before calculation and testing under a common operating condition.

## Distinguish agitation from heat exchange

The proposed agitation system has two 5.5 kW motors, propellers and draft tubes. The cooling system uses a 12 m² spiral-plate heat exchanger with a filter, circulating pump and valves. Agitation controls fluid movement inside the bath; the heat-exchange circuit transfers heat to the cooling side. Agitator motor power is not cooling capacity, and heat-transfer area alone does not prove the same recovery time for every incoming load.

The control description sends fluid through a strainer and pump to the exchanger when fluid temperature exceeds the set condition, then returns cooled fluid to prepare for the next quench. Confirm flow rates on both sides, cooling-water inlet conditions, filter maintenance and successive-batch heat loads alongside the equipment list. Pump motor power does not replace these selection conditions.

## Match tank dimensions to workpiece immersion

The cover gives a tank height of 2250 mm, while the specification table states 2.55 m; both give length and width as 2.7×2.4 m. The height discrepancy affects fluid quantity and immersion space. Neither figure is selected here as the effective depth, and usable fluid volume is not calculated from external dimensions. Show the actual workpieces, basket, working fluid level and required immersion relationship in the final tank drawing.

The source uses “quenching fluid”, with some passages referring to water flow. This does not establish a particular medium, grade or concentration for the current purchase. Confirm the actual medium, workpiece material and temperatures before checking agitation, heat exchange and fluid-temperature records. The tank proposal is not a quenching process suitable for every material.

## Include external cooling conditions at handover

The source assigns the external cooling-water-pool foundation and indoor equipment foundations to the user, with site equipment installation and commissioning proposed for Suneng. That foundation clause does not automatically allocate the entire external cooling facility to either party. Confirm the external pool and water-supply equipment list, then include actual batch intervals, recovered fluid temperature and validation methods in the commissioning programme, recording results under the same conditions.
