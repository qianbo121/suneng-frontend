Corrosion protection cannot be assessed from the equipment name alone. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s bogie-hearth furnace proposal dated 25 February 2024 specifies different materials for heating elements, the inner exhaust pipe and outer duct, using a double-wall duct for exhaust, make-up air and furnace-pressure control. First identify substances carried in by workpieces, emissions during heating and the medium contacting each component, then confirm materials, ventilation and downstream treatment. One material grade cannot establish corrosion resistance of the whole furnace.

## Assess corrosion exposure component by component

| Component | Proposed material | Location/function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electric heating elements | Cr20Ni80 nickel-chromium resistance strip | Around the chamber and beneath the hearth. |
| Inner exhaust pipe | 304 stainless steel | Carries hot waste gas; heat-transfer fins welded outside. |
| Outer duct | Welded carbon steel | Fresh air gains heat in the annular passage before entering the furnace. |

These names describe the proposal, without service-life testing or an applicability list for particular corrosive media. Confirm selection from surface residues, emission composition, component temperatures, residence and cleaning conditions. Heater material, stainless-steel pipe naming or maximum furnace temperature alone cannot establish long-term whole-furnace corrosion resistance.

## Exhaust and make-up air are separate paths linked by heat exchange

Hot waste gas travels through the inner pipe; fresh air is heated between the inner and outer pipes and enters the front/rear chamber regions. A centrifugal extraction fan serves the exhaust, a regulating valve controls the fresh-air inlet, and furnace-pressure feedback adjusts the fan. Confirm the objects and connections for exhaust, inlet and pressure control separately; these are not one recirculating-air passage.

The design proposes exhaust heat recovery, not verified purification or energy savings. Define who designs, supplies and installs ducting after the fan, treatment equipment and the final connection. Compatibility with existing extraction also depends on actual resistance and process needs.

## What operating information is needed for this 100 kW furnace?

The source lists a 1500 × 1200 × 1000 mm chamber, 100 kW heating, maximum operating temperature 1000°C and rated load 2 t. These historical equipment/loading parameters do not define treatment conditions for a new workpiece and its emissions. Provide material, surface condition, loading, process curve and substances to be discharged so the supplier can assess heaters, piping and exhaust.

If preserving or removing a surface layer is an objective, list the result requirement separately. The cited proposal refers generically to small and medium workpieces and supplies no medium-specific trials or post-treatment reports. “Corrosion protection” is not presented as completed coating treatment or verified applicability to a material category.

## Pressure regulation and fault protection need corresponding controls

The source specifies differential-pressure measurement, variable-frequency extraction control, overtemperature/broken-thermocouple audible and visual alarms, and heater disconnection. These describe normal pressure regulation and temperature protection, not established coverage of every extraction interruption, pressure-sensor fault or blocked duct. If continuous exhaust is essential, define detection, equipment response and site support for these states as verifiable control requirements.

## What should you send first?

Provide a representative workpiece, surface deposits or pre-treatment condition, the actual heating curve, loading drawing and site exhaust connection. For existing corrosion or fouling, identify affected locations and records; this is more useful than a general request for a corrosion-resistant furnace. Suneng can then allocate heaters, inner exhaust, make-up-air passage and external treatment before setting the configuration.
