Suneng's 2016 rapid solution-treatment furnace proposal for stainless-steel pipe fittings specifies a furnace load of 5–8 t per batch excluding fixture support blocks, while requiring a transfer fork truck with a maximum single-load capacity of at least 8 T under uniform loading, including fixtures. The figures are close but use different weight definitions. Count baskets and travelling fixtures in transfer weight; an 8 t furnace load does not prove that the truck can transfer 8 t of net workpieces.

## Check whether each load figure includes fixtures

| Item | Original proposal | Main check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rated furnace load | 5–8 t per batch, excluding fixture support blocks | List support blocks separately from net workpieces |
| Transfer truck load | Maximum single-load capacity at least 8 T, uniformly loaded, including fixtures | Check total actual workpiece/fixture weight and distribution |
| Transferred load | Workpieces and basket leave the furnace together and descend into water | Include baskets, trays and all travelling components |

“At least 8 T” is a minimum requirement for maximum single-load capacity; it does not fix the final truck capacity at exactly 8 T. It is therefore also incorrect to conclude that the truck necessarily cannot carry 8 t of workpieces. Confirm the final design rating and applicable loading conditions, then compare them with the total workpieces, basket and accompanying fixtures.

## Include the actual loading pattern

The truck clause specifies uniform loading and identifies steel-frame stiffness and capacity during hot transfer as key design concerns. Supply basket dimensions, workpiece arrangement, total weight and support positions during lifting. Total tonnage alone does not establish uniform loading. Actual structural capability remains subject to the final loading drawing and confirmed design values.

The chamber measures 4.7 m×2.4 m×1.8 m. The sequence includes horizontal truck travel into and out of the furnace and vertical fork-arm movement into water. The loading drawing must therefore check chamber fit, basket geometry and fork engagement together with entry, exit and lowering clearances. Chamber size is not used to infer maximum basket dimensions or unprovided clearances.

## Define transfer timing separately

The proposed duty is rapid solution treatment of stainless-steel fittings, with hot workpieces and basket lowered into water together. The source does not define the start action or final immersion state for total transfer time. Travel and lowering speeds describe individual motions, not the complete furnace-to-water interval. If the process requires a transfer limit, agree timing endpoints alongside actual basket loading, door travel and truck strokes.

## Match supply scope to the complete transfer sequence

Project composition includes the solution furnace, rail-mounted fork truck and solution-treatment water tank. The seller's list includes furnace, truck and drives; fixture support blocks are buyer-supplied. Although the water tank appears in project composition, it is not separately defined in the seller's itemised list. Complete design and supply responsibilities for the tank, connections and auxiliaries in the final revision. Neither inclusion nor complete buyer supply can be assumed.

The proposal schedules loaded heat-up commissioning after empty-furnace commissioning. Transfer validation should use jointly agreed loading and record workpiece/fixture weights and the motion sequence. These are purchasing-stage validation requirements, not completed load tests or solution-treatment results.
