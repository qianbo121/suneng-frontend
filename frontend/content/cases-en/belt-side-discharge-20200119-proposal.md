Suneng's 2020 roller-supported mesh-belt heat-treatment line proposal places a rotatable discharge hopper at the oil-tank elevator exit for side discharge of workpieces whose process does not require tempering on this line. The normal route continues into the tempering furnace. This provides alternative receiving paths; hopper rotation does not establish that a material may omit tempering or has completed all heat treatment.

## Define the process destination before choosing the path

| Location or function | Original proposal | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Straight-through route | Elevator discharges from oil tank to tempering inlet | Continue tempering as required by the part; check receiving conditions |
| Side route | Rotatable hopper for parts not requiring tempering | Equipment path only; not approval to omit a process |
| Oil-tank elevator | Effective width 1000 mm | Match loading, hopper and receiving equipment |
| Interlocks | Elevator fault alarm; drives interlocked progressively upstream from tempering | Final controls must define how diversion state participates |

The process description sends oil-quenched parts to tempering, while the structural section explicitly adds side discharge. These need not conflict: one describes the normal connection, the other an additional receiving option. Match each product's approved process to its discharge direction and define whether side discharge leads to another machine, collection or another approved next step. Diversion capability is not process conformity.

## Check hot workpieces and oil condition at side discharge

The elevator has 80 mm side guards, an inclined belt and overhead extraction, followed by the rotating hopper. Select receiving equipment against part shape, weight, arrangement and actual condition on leaving the oil. Confirm diverted passage, receiving height and surrounding space. The source does not guarantee all parts can side-discharge from one hopper dimension, and oil-tank area temperature is not workpiece discharge temperature.

The tank separately includes level detection, agitation, filtration and circulating cooling, with duty/standby pumps. Even when bypassing this tempering furnace, upstream oil-quenching conditions must still meet the part's process. Changing destination does not establish completed cooling. Elevator speeds conflict between summary and structure sections: 30–250 and 50–500 mm/min. Neither range is used here to calculate side-discharge time or maximum throughput.

## Include diversion state in control confirmation

The drives interlock progressively upstream from tempering; individual machines offer independent/interlocked operation, and the elevator has abnormal-running alarms. To use side discharge, final controls must identify direction, available receiving path and upstream response to interrupted receiving. General interlock wording is not evidence that these states are validated, and it does not provide a site switching sequence.

Upstream heating uses methanol and propane. One end of the discharge chute is inside the furnace and the other submerged in quenching liquid, with separate downstream extraction. The side-discharge mechanism is after oil-tank lifting; changing its position does not remove upstream atmosphere or liquid-level sealing requirements. Full controls and abnormal handling must retain those interfaces.

## Assess usefulness for a new project

Proposed output is approximately 1000 kg/h, explicitly dependent on shape, piece weight, piling and process. Supply each product's treatment route, side-discharge destination and receiving conditions before deciding whether diversion is needed. The proposal covers heating, oil tank, tempering and controls. Define whether site receiving equipment is included, plus power, cooling and exhaust connections, in the current supply table. Adjustable structures and output figures are not completed production validation for every product.
