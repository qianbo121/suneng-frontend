Lead-alloy melting capacity and molten-lead pump flow describe different stages. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s electrically heated proposal dated 21 February 2019 specifies a rated 20 t of lead per batch, while the pump is listed at a maximum 60 t/h and a head of 5 m. The first is a proposed batch quantity; the second concerns transfer equipment. Pumping capacity does not establish how much lead the furnace melts each hour. Separate melting batches from downstream withdrawal cycles before checking their coordination.

## Separate melting from transfer

| Proposed item | Source record | Assessment purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rated lead-melting quantity | 20 t/batch | Discuss batch quantity, not hourly output. |
| Molten-lead pump | Maximum 60 t/h; head 5 m; variable-frequency control | Assess withdrawal and transfer. Do not combine the two figures into a verified operating point. |
| Heating power | 300 kW | Equipment configuration, not actual consumption. |
| Lead-pot dimensions | Φ1700 × 1400 mm | Check separately against chamber, stirring and withdrawal arrangements. |

The proposal is for melting lead alloy and casting ingots. For continuous supply, list actual batch charge, initial condition, required liquid temperature, melting and holding times, then include dross removal, withdrawal and recharging on one cycle schedule. Only a complete cycle supports discussion of average molten-metal supply. Dividing 20 t by pump flow does not estimate melting time.

Pump selection also depends on the actual route. Provide elevation differences, pipe length, bends and required flow, then confirm the operating point. Although flow and head are both listed, the cited pages contain no pump curve and do not guarantee maximum flow under arbitrary piping conditions. If short-term downstream demand exceeds the rate at which melting replenishes liquid, first revise production coordination or holding arrangements, then determine equipment quantity.

## Leave access for charging, stirring, dross removal and withdrawal

The stirrer sits above the pot. Its mounting frame carries the stirrer and lead pump, with pinned connections to the furnace at both ends for dismantling and lifting. Charging can take place from one side. Manual dross removal is performed from both sides, through a dross opening and chute into a collection trolley. Space is reserved for movable covers on both sides, but the proposal explicitly does not supply them at this stage.

Check whether charging equipment can reach the opening, dross tools can operate, transfer piping obstructs maintenance lifting, and the dross trolley can be removed. A plan-view fit does not establish simultaneous operability. Resolve conflicts around the frame or define operating order; pump flow cannot solve access conflicts.

The source transfers pot loads through the base and support columns to the bottom steel frame and explicitly states that hearth brickwork does not carry the load. Foundation and maintenance assessments must follow this load path. Have the equipment designer check loads from the pot, operating molten lead and upper mechanisms. The 20 t/batch figure is not the entire foundation design load.

## Furnace temperature does not establish molten-lead condition

The 2019 proposal specifies a maximum chamber heating temperature of approximately 950°C and 2 furnace control zones, with holding uniformity ≤±10°C and control accuracy ≤±1°C. These concern furnace temperature. The source provides no equivalent measured molten-lead temperature spread or ingot-quality result here. Match target liquid temperature and withdrawal conditions to the alloy and casting requirements, with suitable measurement positions and verification methods.

The proposal specifies audible/visual overtemperature and broken-thermocouple alarms and automatic heater-power disconnection, with overtemperature monitoring separate from control monitoring. These heating protections do not replace interface checks for charging, dross removal and withdrawal. For the proposed open operating positions, the buyer should provide feed form, work positions and site collection/treatment conditions. Equipment and site providers must agree fume collection, exhaust destination, conditions for stopping charging or withdrawal during faults, and verification scope. The cited source lacks a complete design for these interfaces; reserved cover positions are not a complete enclosed collection system.

## List equipment supply and site services separately

Suneng proposes supplying the furnace, electrical controls, stirrer, manual dross-removal tools, molten-lead pump, secondary cables between furnace and cabinet, and technical documents. The buyer provides foundations, embedded parts and wiring from the distribution room to the incoming side of the cabinet's main switch. Proposed site power is 380 V, 350–400 kW. This supply condition serves a different purpose from the 300 kW heating rating; check the complete equipment load schedule rather than substituting either figure for the other.

The source proposes no-load operation, temperature checks in each zone and trial production as acceptance stages. For a new melting/casting project, agree input condition, melting cycle, withdrawal conditions and recorded quantities before testing, so results address continuous-supply compatibility. Proposed acceptance stages are not completed results.

## Two procurement questions

### Can a higher maximum pump flow reduce the number of melting furnaces?

Not by itself. Match downstream withdrawal demand to each complete melting cycle. Equipment quantity can be compared only when stage times, available liquid quantity and withdrawal windows are known. A pump transfers existing molten lead; it does not supply melting heat.

### What should be provided to start comparison?

Provide alloy and feed condition, planned batch quantity, target molten-lead conditions, downstream withdrawal timing and site layout. If piping or an ingot-casting line already exists, include elevations and interface positions. This identifies whether melting or transfer needs adjustment before refining access and responsibilities.
