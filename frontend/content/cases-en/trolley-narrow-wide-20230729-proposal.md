Two proposals specifying four temperature-control zones do not necessarily divide the heating space in the same way. In two bogie-hearth furnace documents from Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd., dated 29 July 2023, the heating-element sections respectively describe “four zones from front to rear” and “four zones across front, rear, left and right”. Buyers and process engineers should match those directions to the loading drawing and measurement locations before deciding what each zone covers.

## This comparison concerns the zone descriptions

Both proposals cover heating for workpiece heat treatment and list an operating temperature of 950°C and a rated load of 15 t, but their working dimensions differ. The sources do not establish that the equipment is interchangeable within a project. This page does not compare temperature uniformity or energy efficiency. The dimensions and zoning below are the figures in each proposal.

## Four zone labels do not constitute a zoning drawing

“Front to rear” and “front, rear, left and right” describe different spatial directions, but neither sentence defines the precise boundaries of each heating circuit. Do not divide the long chamber into four equal lengths or the wide chamber into four equal quadrants by assumption. Confirm the walls and hearth areas served by each zone's elements and their wiring assignments, then overlay the intended workpieces and fixtures.

Both furnace-shell sections also retain the wording “two control zones from front to rear”, inconsistent with the four-zone descriptions in the specifications and heating sections. This comparison preserves the scope of each original statement rather than selecting one as the final construction arrangement. The designer should reconcile them in one coordinated zoning drawing and control-wiring schedule.

## Match each probe to its representative location

Both system configurations list four programme controllers, four sheathed thermocouples and a paperless temperature recorder. These establish proposed control and recording equipment, but not the final spatial coordinates of the four probes. A comparison drawing can show zone numbers, associated heating circuits, probe locations and load positions, allowing checks on whether workpieces or fixtures change how well each probe represents its zone. This is a suggested design check, not a measurement layout already completed in the source.

Both proposals describe recording furnace temperature during heating, soaking and cooling. These records are not workpiece-core temperatures, and the presence of four probes does not prove that the entire load reaches the same temperature. Define the measurement points and decision criteria for actual workpiece validation separately against the workpiece requirements.

## Confirm the drawing before arranging validation

Suneng was to install and commission the equipment. The buyer was to prepare the site from the foundation information and, after empty-furnace commissioning, prepare for loaded heat-up commissioning. Both proposals list supplied documents including general arrangement and electrical schematic drawings. First complete and jointly approve the relationship between zones, measurement points and the intended load, then include the required verification conditions in commissioning and acceptance. Until loaded-operation records are available, the proposal figures remain a starting point for review.
