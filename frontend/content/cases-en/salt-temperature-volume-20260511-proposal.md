Suneng's 2026 electric salt-bath furnace sample specifies a maximum chamber temperature of 550°C, maximum salt-bath temperature of 400°C and a particular circulating-salt condition of 340°C. These concern different objects and cannot all be called “treatment temperature”. Likewise, separate the φ1500×1400 mm crucible interior, maximum liquid level and actual workpiece dimensions when assessing immersion.

## The three temperatures have different meanings

| Item | Original value | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maximum chamber temperature | 550°C | Capability of the furnace heating space |
| Maximum salt-bath temperature | 400°C | Source's bath limit; not replaced by chamber temperature |
| Circulating-salt temperature | 340°C | Overview's cycle condition, not a universal workpiece process |
| Immersion dwell for different sizes | 14–180 minutes | Size-dependent dwell; no product-by-product curves provided |

The overview also says workpieces undergo salt-bath heating below 550°C, while detailed parameters explicitly assign 550°C to the chamber and 400°C to the bath. Confirm the relationship in the adopted revision. The general sentence does not raise the bath limit to 550°C. If the required bath temperature exceeds confirmed conditions, reassess equipment and medium; a higher chamber capability alone does not establish suitability.

## Review crucible space, level and workpieces separately

The crucible interior is φ1500×1400 mm high. Maximum molten-salt level is approximately 100 mm below the rim, maximum salt volume is 2200 litres, and product dimensions are φ1250×550 mm high. These support initial space comparison, but subtracting diameters alone does not establish fit. Show supports, lifting fixtures, immersion direction and loaded liquid level in a section. Empty-crucible space is not the effective immersion space for arbitrary loads.

The overview's “maximum charge weight 1.7 T” is not explicitly defined as net workpiece weight or loading including fixtures. Its original scope is retained; 1.7 t is not listed here as workpiece load capacity. Clarify what is charged and whether salt or fixtures are included before relating it to workpieces and supports.

## Temperature records do not automatically establish every workpiece's state

The sample lists temperature setting, monitoring, recording, programme-completion indication and overtemperature/open-thermocouple protection, with two thermocouples in the system list. The reviewed source does not locate them or assign chamber control versus bath measurement. No duties are invented here. Use the final point drawing to match control, recording and bath measurement, then confirm dwell and results with representative workpieces. Remote curve viewing does not replace location and condition validation.

Materials are listed as steel and stainless steel, with “PTFE coating” in the process field. That phrase does not identify the coating operation being performed. Before selection, define workpiece state before and after immersion, salt medium and process purpose. Coating/medium compatibility, possible emissions and treatment scope also need project process documentation. This page organises temperature and space conditions without claiming coating removal, coating formation or achieved treatment effects.

## Confirm the process and loaded state before equipment interfaces

The sample includes a pit furnace body, heat-resistant steel molten-salt crucible and controls, with pneumatic lid lifting. An opening lid does not mean workpiece lifting fixtures are supplied. The buyer prepares agreed site utilities and installation crane. Provide workpiece drawings, incoming surface condition, target bath temperature and dwell basis, then review crucible, level, measurement and fixture interfaces. Empty/loaded commissioning and signed acceptance are planned, not completed acceptance evidence.
