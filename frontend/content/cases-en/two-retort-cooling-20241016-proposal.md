While one retort occupies the main furnace for heating, the other can undergo cooling, unloading, reloading and preparation that the process permits outside the furnace. To judge whether two retorts can reduce waiting, compare when the previous retort is ready to enter again with when the other retort is allowed to leave. Shared lifting equipment, gas and water connections, and preparation stations also affect the handover. A pit-type vacuum resistance furnace proposal issued by Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd. on 16 October 2024 specifies one main furnace, two vacuum retorts and two air-cooling vessels. This article uses that arrangement to explain how to assess alternating operation.

## 1. Two retorts share one heating position: identify which operations can overlap

In this proposal, Suneng places 200 kW of heating elements around the main furnace, with separate upper and lower temperature-control zones. Each retort has a guide cylinder, a lid assembly and a forced-circulation fan. The workpieces are loaded inside the retort, which provides the enclosure for the protective atmosphere.

| Equipment | Arrangement in this proposal | Effect on alternating operation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main furnace | One, rated at 200 kW, with two vertical control zones | The two retorts must use the same heating position in turn |
| Vacuum retorts | Two sets, with guide cylinders, lids and circulation fans | While one occupies the furnace, the other can undergo operations permitted outside it |
| Air-cooling vessels | Two | Provide external cooling positions; each retort's cooling time still matters |
| Vacuum and ammonia-dissociation systems | One of each | Connection sequences and use of these shared systems must be included in the schedule |
| Working dimensions | Φ1000 × 1600 mm | Loading must account for the guide cylinder, load-bearing base and fixtures |

This arrangement separates the position that supplies heat from the vessel that contains the workpieces. If a retort can complete its cooling outside the furnace, it need not continue occupying the main furnace. The other retort must be ready to use the heating position that becomes available. Equipment manufacturers also describe interchangeable retorts cooling outside the furnace while retaining a protective atmosphere. [Interchangeable retorts and external cooling](https://nabertherm.com/en/products/industry/thermal-process/processes-under-protective/hot-wall-retort-furnaces/pit-type)

The overlap is between operations on different retorts. One main furnace still provides only one heating position, and two air-cooling vessels do not increase heating power.

## 2. The handover depends on when the previous retort is ready

Assume retort B is ready to enter when retort A leaves the main furnace. Record two sequences on the same timeline: B enters and completes the required operations inside the main furnace until it is allowed to leave; A completes cooling, unloading, reloading, sealing and any atmosphere preparation that can take place before entry at the permitted external stations.

**If A becomes ready to enter after B is allowed to leave, the main furnace will wait for a retort. The difference between those times is the minimum waiting caused by A not being ready for that handover. Occupied shared lifting equipment or gas connections can add further delay.**

When comparing the timelines, preparation or controlled cooling that must take place inside the main furnace still counts as furnace occupancy. It cannot be counted as overlapping external work. Shared lifting equipment, the vacuum system, gas supply and water changeover facilities must also be scheduled according to which retort they serve and in what order.

Suneng's proposal specifies no more than three hours for the empty furnace to reach 950°C and no more than 20 minutes for preliminary evacuation. These describe empty-furnace heating and preliminary evacuation respectively. Adding them does not establish a complete production time for a batch of workpieces. Loaded processing, permitted external cooling, unloading, reloading and atmosphere preparation must be tied to the actual load and process curve.

If external cooling causes most of the waiting, first check the permitted cooling process against the specified process curve, then assess whether the cooling equipment provides the required capability. Retain the time required by the process. Where actual cooling fails to meet the requirement, compare cooling equipment, loading and site conditions. If unloading, reloading or atmosphere preparation causes the delay, address occupancy of the relevant stations and shared systems first.

## 3. External air cooling still requires internal atmosphere protection and seal cooling

Suneng provides two different circulation arrangements: the lid fan and guide cylinder organise gas circulation inside the retort, while the air-cooling vessel cools the outside of the retort. They serve different spaces. Workpieces and fixtures must leave passages for the guide cylinder's circulation path. When a retort moves to the air-cooling position, the internal protective conditions required for that stage must remain available.

The retort uses 10 mm thick 310S heat-resistant steel plate, with a corrugated shell for reinforcement and a load-bearing structure at the bottom. The upper flange uses a rubber sealing ring and water cooling. This water protects the seal area. Air cooling of the retort as a whole does not mean the seal-cooling water can also be stopped.

Atmosphere control extends into cooling. The proposal specifies automatic venting to regulate pressure as gas expands during heating, automatic nitrogen replenishment as it contracts during cooling, and a pressure gauge and safety valve on the lid assembly. It also specifies replacing the dissociated-ammonia atmosphere with nitrogen before “removal from the furnace.” Dissociated ammonia contains hydrogen; ventilation and leak detection are relevant system-design considerations. [Hydrogen safety design guidance](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/fuels/safe-use-hydrogen)

The operating layout should therefore show which gas-supply, vent and cooling-water branches connect to the retort at each position. In particular, the proposal's wording about purging before removal must be linked to a defined action: leaving the main furnace or opening the cooled retort for unloading. These must not be assumed to occur at the same time. Exhaust routing, atmosphere protection during transfer, protection after a power or gas-supply failure, and leak detection must also be coordinated with the responsibilities of the equipment supplier and site providers.

The proposal already lists a low atmosphere-pressure alarm, a furnace overtemperature alarm with automatic heating shutdown, cooling-water overtemperature and low-pressure alarms, and changeover to an emergency mains-water supply during abnormal conditions. Retain these measures in the interface design for alternating retorts. The site must provide the corresponding emergency water source and define hot-retort lifting equipment, travel paths, landing supports and personnel exclusion conditions. After a retort changes position, the relevant protection must still serve the retort in use.

## 4. Use the location of the delay to decide what to adjust first

| Observed situation | Compare first | Direction for action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A stage permitted outside the furnace takes a long time to cool, and the main furnace often waits for a retort | Required cooling curve, actual cooling time and the other retort's furnace occupancy | Retain process-required time, then assess whether cooling capability or batch scheduling needs adjustment |
| Cooling has finished, but the retort is still late for the next turn | Time for unloading, reloading, sealing, evacuation and atmosphere preparation | Improve coordination of the relevant stations and shared systems |
| The next retort is ready, but processing inside the main furnace is incomplete | Heating, holding and cooling that must take place inside the main furnace | Assess furnace occupancy first; additional cooling positions cannot shorten these operations |
| Gas hoses, water hoses or lifting equipment interfere during changeover | Retort-state diagrams, piping diagrams and lifting layout | Resolve interfaces and space requirements before scheduling alternating operation |

For procurement comparisons, put the main furnace, cooling and loading stations, shared gas supply, water circuits and lifting arrangements on one layout and timeline. With the agreed load, record the time, temperature and atmosphere state of each retort entering and leaving each station, alongside product inspection results. This helps identify whether waiting comes from process time, external preparation or shared facilities.

If you are considering one furnace with two retorts, start with workpiece and fixture loading details, the complete process curve, current cooling and loading times, and site lifting, gas-supply and cooling-water conditions. Suneng Industrial Furnace can use these to review the coordination of furnace occupancy and external operations. If some information is unavailable, existing temperature records and station photographs are a useful starting point.
