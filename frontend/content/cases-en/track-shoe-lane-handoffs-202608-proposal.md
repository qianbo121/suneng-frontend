A Suneng roller-hearth track-shoe line proposal runs from pallet pickup to the buyer's shot-blasting-machine inlet. It includes turning, three-lane heating and press quenching, six-lane tempering and merging into one lane. Interfaces must preserve workpiece orientation, lane assignment and downstream release conditions as well as conveyor elevation.

## Picking up a shoe does not complete feeding

The source covers single-, double- and triple-grouser track shoes, 600–1200 mm long, 200–350 mm wide and 30–80 mm overall height including grousers. Parts are stacked on pallets in alternating, interlocking orientations. Vision identifies position and orientation, a robot picks each part, and a turning mechanism places the grousers down while checking notch direction. The line requires correctly oriented parts; a successful lift is not permission to enter the furnace.

The draft requests additional three-dimensional drawings, pallet-stack details and physical samples. Provide parts per layer, interlocking orientation, suction-contact surfaces and clearance locations to mechanical and vision designers, then check representative models through pickup and turning. Height including grousers is not net plate thickness, and the two cannot be substituted in heat-treatment assessment.

## Match three press-quenching stations to their lanes

The roller-hearth draft uses three transverse, side-by-side press-quenching stations on a shared frame, one per lane, with a row of three shoes moving forward into their respective stations. A coexisting draft instead uses tandem stations and a lateral-transfer manipulator. Their routes and footprints differ. The three-lane straight-entry relationship applies only to the former, and cannot be combined with the other draft's mechanisms or prices.

The transfer-time calculation includes transition-roller conveying, positioning stops and die closure, ending at contact by the press head. Immersion is described separately. Procurement should mark furnace exit, positioning, press-head contact and immersion start on a timing diagram and define the contractual furnace-to-quench endpoint. Proposed transfer seconds are not evidence of completed quenching or acceptable quality.

## Redistributing three lanes into six requires ordered receiving

At the tempering inlet, a chain lift-and-transfer mechanism redistributes three incoming lanes into six. More lanes change distribution and the interval at each lane; they do not automatically increase upstream part count. One timing diagram should show each transfer, available positions, tempering-roller speed and waiting conditions between sections.

The draft displays furnace residence from effective length and roller speed. This represents time within a section. Whether it meets the process depends on steel grade, net plate thickness and the agreed curve. Geometric residence after three-to-six redistribution is not verified net holding time for every size.

## The merge ends where the buyer's shot blaster accepts parts

After cooling and air knives, the proposal includes six-channel stopping/positioning, six-lane chain cross-transfer and a merge roller conveyor. Parts are released in lane order into a single lane feeding the buyer's shot blaster. The source expressly makes the shot-blasting machine buyer-supplied; inlet elevation, permitted part orientation and interlock signals are also required from the buyer. After connection, verify waiting and recovery when the shot blaster stops or its inlet is occupied.

The layout sections total 72 m, equal to the planned length with no longitudinal margin. Shot-blaster inlet coordinates, pallet arrival direction and necessary maintenance positions should be drawn first. Comparing only the two furnace lengths cannot establish adequate space for turning, transfers, press quenching and merging.

## First checks for a similar project

Provide representative parts and three-dimensional drawings, pallet-stack photographs, permissible furnace-entry orientation, press-quench station layout and shot-blaster inlet information. Commission in the actual order: pickup/turning, three-lane receiving, three-to-six redistribution and six-to-one merging. Record pickup faults, withheld downstream permission and interruption recovery. Hardness, bending and heat-treatment results require separate acceptance against agreed materials and inspection conditions.
