Suneng's 2022 dual-strip continuous stainless-steel annealing furnace documents give a design output of 34.37 t/h for two strips in one furnace, with specified steel grade, dimensions and speed. Final acceptance separately specifies continuous-production trials using two SUS304 strip sizes. Distinguish the design-calculation material, actual acceptance-test sizes and whether output is counted per strip, per dual-strip furnace or across two furnaces.

## Separate one dual-strip furnace from two furnaces

The agreement's “output calculation (single furnace)” uses J3A strip at 2.3 mm×600 mm, a constant speed of 26.6 m/min and HBW≤224. It gives 34.37 t/h explicitly labelled “dual strip”. That figure is not for one strip and must not be multiplied by two again. A two-furnace total also requires each furnace's conditions and simultaneous operating time.

The annual calculation specifies 8000 h of net production and 275,000 tonnes/year of standard material. Multiplying 34.37 by 8000 gives approximately 275,000 tonnes, showing the dependence on the stated hourly output and production time. The 750 t/d in the model heading does not account for shutdowns, specification changes or actual production hours in a schedule.

The source defines TV as strip speed multiplied by thickness. Both SUS304 test conditions give 55, while the design section gives 61. These conditions are not equivalent. TV alone omits width, single/dual-strip operation and quality requirements, so it cannot establish tonnage or product conformity. The 55 here is an arithmetic check of the stated figures, not a change to the design criterion.

## Reconcile representative sizes and speeds

The design calculation uses 600 mm width, but the main parameter table gives a representative size of 620 mm×2.3 mm. Later whole-line criteria give 600 mm×2.2 mm at 27.85 m/min. The basic width range ends at 610 mm, yet final-acceptance material is 620 mm wide. Maximum process speed also appears as both 33 m/min and 31 m/min. Both parties should reconcile every item in a final specification table rather than combining the widest size, highest speed and most favourable output into one guarantee.

## Agree an executable acceptance condition

The proposed process includes preheating, heating and subsequent cooling. For capacity assessment, put grade, width, thickness, strip count, continuous speed, heating/cooling requirements, hardness or surface requirements, test tonnage and downtime treatment in the same row. The agreement separates individual-equipment, cold-run, hot-run and final-acceptance stages and requires jointly signed stage reports. These are agreed procedures; no reports proving that these stages passed are provided.

Review Suneng's role against the agreement's furnace sections, cooling equipment, controls and installation clauses. The list includes reused and buyer-provided items. Record test materials, reused-equipment condition and interfaces together. A historical technical agreement helps organise checks but does not prove compliance for new materials, new output requirements or later major retrofits.
