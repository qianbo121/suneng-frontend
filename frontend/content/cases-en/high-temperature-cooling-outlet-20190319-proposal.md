Suneng's 2019 proposal for a 65 kW high-temperature bogie-hearth furnace specifies heat-resistant steel pipes inside the chamber, supplied with cold air by a high-pressure blower. The same paragraph also refers to “displacing hot air inside the furnace”. The inlet is described, but drawings must establish where the pipes discharge. That determines whether the arrangement exchanges heat through pipe walls; the name “finned tubes” alone is insufficient.

## Define the outlet as clearly as the inlet

The source's single cooling-system paragraph proposes internal heat-resistant steel pipes, describes a high-temperature finned-tube heat-exchange arrangement and supplies cold air into the pipes from a high-pressure blower. It establishes airflow into the pipes, not direct cold-air injection into the chamber.

| Item | What the document establishes | What remains unclear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal components | Heat-resistant steel pipes, described as high-temperature finned-tube heat exchange | Layout, outlet and connection to the chamber |
| Cold-air inlet | High-pressure blower supplies air into pipes | Final outlet location and discharge method |
| Cooling description | Hot furnace air displaced through the pipe arrangement | Whether “displacement” means furnace-gas exchange or a general description of heat transfer |

The paragraph uses both “heat exchange” and “displacing hot air”, without explaining their physical connection. Neither should be selected by assumption to draw a complete airflow circuit. Obtain a drawing showing inlet, pipe outlets, chamber boundary and discharge points, identifying connected air streams. Only then can it be determined whether cooling air enters the chamber. Any workpiece atmosphere requirement must be agreed separately; this proposal gives no protective-atmosphere conditions.

## Define cooling against loading and the measured object

The table gives a maximum temperature of 1200°C and heating power of 65 kW. Neither establishes actual cooling rate. The cooling section specifies no start/end temperatures, loading state or time to target. Define the actual workpieces and load, the required temperature interval, and whether furnace or workpiece temperature is the criterion before assessing the arrangement.

The paperless recorder can record furnace heating, soaking and cooling. This provides a route to reviewing furnace curves, but does not prove that workpiece cores have reached the same temperature. If the purchasing criterion concerns workpieces, specify their measurement points and recording method in the final revision.

## Complete connection drawings and inspection conditions

The source calls for loaded heat-up commissioning after empty-furnace commissioning and supplies a general arrangement, electrical schematic and operating manual. Include cooling-pipe connections and verification of the agreed cooling conditions in jointly confirmed technical documents. Planned heat-up commissioning does not establish validated cooling performance.
