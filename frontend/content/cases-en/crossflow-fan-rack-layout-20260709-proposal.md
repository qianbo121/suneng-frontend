A Suneng oven proposal for cross-flow fan impellers targets 120 transfer-rack frames per batch in a 5 × 4 × 3 m chamber. A loading drawing must verify that quantity: frame arrangement, projecting parts, loading access and blockage of side or bottom air outlets cannot be assessed from total chamber volume alone.

## The 120-frame target needs a loading arrangement

The source workpieces are air-conditioner cross-flow fan impellers made of AS resin with 30% glass fibre. The oven has one chamber, a fixed enclosure and batch loading. A statement that it can run continuously does not change this loading method or establish a continuous conveyor.

A single-layer, non-overlapping floor-area check using the proposed 610 × 610 mm frame footprint gives approximately 44.65 m² for 120 frames. This already exceeds the 20 m² floor area of the 5 × 4 m chamber before air passages and clearances are deducted. Thus, 120 frames cannot be explained by ordinary single-layer placement. Permitted stacking, nesting or a different counting basis requires fixture drawings; this article does not invent an arrangement.

## Parts must leave effective supply and return-air space

The circulation section proposes side or bottom air supply and top return, with outlets that do not blow directly onto the fan blades. Frames and parts therefore need more than mechanical clearances: plot them against the supply and return openings to identify blocked areas when fully loaded and any row exposed to concentrated direct airflow. This uses the source's proposed flow directions without claiming a verified complete airflow arrangement.

The proposal also specifies temperature uniformity of ≤±5°C in the effective working zone, verified at 9 measuring points. Points should correspond to the agreed effective zone and loading condition. Empty-oven commissioning curves or instrument displays alone cannot establish acceptable blade temperatures and dimensional results with 120 frames loaded. The loading drawing, measuring-point arrangement and loaded-test records need joint confirmation.

## Align level loading and civil-work boundaries on one drawing

The source calls for a flat, hardened indoor floor and frames pushed horizontally into and out of the oven. Installation terms allocate foundations and other civil works to the buyer, but separately allocate civil works associated with recessing the oven to the seller. Define hearth elevation, the doorway transition, recessed sections and each party's work rather than assigning everything generically to one party.

For similar enquiries, first provide frame drawings and the complete envelope with impellers loaded, target batch quantity, whether stacking is permitted, and actual handling arrangements. Establish a workable load before reviewing heating and airflow capacity. This article retains the proposal's targets and verification relationships; it does not present 120-frame loading, stress relief or dimensional stability as achieved delivery results.
