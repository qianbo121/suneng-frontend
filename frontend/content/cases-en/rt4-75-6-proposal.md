For a bogie-hearth furnace heating shafts and small parts, first compare the space, supports and temperature conditions using a loading layout that includes the fixtures. Then compare instruments, testing and supply scope against the actual measurement requirements. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s RT4-75-6 proposal dated 2 November 2021 specified a 900×600×600 mm effective heating zone, a 500–650℃ operating range and 75 kW heating power. Whether its proposed 600 kg rated load included fixtures was not defined. The quotation covered equipment design, manufacture, transport, installation and commissioning, providing a starting point for similar enquiries.

## Establish a comparable loading arrangement before comparing equipment

The proposal used a stationary furnace and a moving bogie carrying the workpieces into and out of the furnace for batch heating. For initial selection, show the combined workpiece and fixture envelope, total batch weight and support positions on one loading drawing, then check the following.

| Check | RT4-75-6 proposal, 2 November 2021 | Comparison for a current project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effective heating zone | 900 long × 600 wide × 600 high, mm | Allow for fixtures, spacing and circulation passages; this is not the maximum workpiece envelope |
| Proposed rated load | 600 kg; fixture inclusion unspecified | List workpiece and fixture weights separately and check support positions, load distribution and operating state |
| Operating temperature | 500–650℃ | Compare with the material and established process requirements |
| Rated temperature | 700℃ | Keep this distinct from operating temperature; it is not the actual process temperature |
| Heating power | 75 kW | An equipment configuration value, not actual electricity consumption |

If the combined envelope cannot be arranged properly, or weight is concentrated on an unverified support, revise the loading layout or confirm furnace dimensions and load-bearing design before comparing controls. Similar total weights do not imply similar load distribution; dividing the total evenly cannot replace a check of each support.

The reviewed proposal and quotation did not detail the temperature, load distribution, or load-bearing conditions during stationary heating and bogie movement associated with the 600 kg rating. Give the supplier the intended temperature range, support drawing and movement conditions. The sources also lacked specific material grades, complete process curves and batch cycles. Establish the process route and required cycle from the current requirement; neither follows from temperature range or batch weight alone.

## Check supports and circulation passages on the same drawing

The proposed bogie used dense refractory bricks at load-bearing locations and lightweight refractory bricks elsewhere. A perforated heat-resistant steel support and air-distribution structure was intended to carry workpieces and form hot-air passages. The proposal specified SUS304 heat-resistant stainless steel and stated suitability for long-term use at the rated 700℃. This described the proposed structure and conditions; it did not establish local load capacity for arbitrary support arrangements.

Match the fixture base to the support layout: do loads land at the designed locations, and are perforations or air passages obstructed? If they do not match, first review fixture supports, placement or required space. This identifies a direction for further design, not an approved modification.

The walls, roof and door used aluminium-silicate fibre modules. The parameter table specified insulation thickness ≥300 mm and high-temperature curing of the internal surface to improve resistance to circulating-air erosion. Resistance strips were arranged around the chamber and beneath the load, with heating elements also in the door. Assess these arrangements together with loading and circulation; actual temperature distribution still requires appropriate testing.

The quotation included a circulation fan with water-cooled sealing and guide plates. Furnace airflow and the fan's cooling-water connection are separate requirements. Fan overheating and overload protection were listed, but the heating response to loss of airflow or abnormal cooling water was not detailed. Define supply and return conditions, equipment-side connections, abnormal-condition response and recovery. “Circulation fan included” does not establish that these interfaces are resolved.

## Align the measurement object and method before comparing temperature figures

Control-point stability over time, measurement-system deviation and spatial temperature distribution address different questions. Page 3 listed the following figures; page 6 also described temperature-control adjustment accuracy. Define each term before comparing numbers.

| Original indicator | Value in the 2021 RT4-75-6 proposal | Definition and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temperature-control accuracy | ±0.5℃ in the parameter table; “temperature-control adjustment accuracy ≤±0.5℃” in the structural description | It was not clear whether this meant fluctuation, indication error or deviation from a reference measurement; do not relabel it as instrument accuracy |
| System accuracy | ±1.7℃ or ±0.3% | Measurement chain, reference, percentage basis and rule for choosing between the two values were not detailed |
| Furnace temperature uniformity | ≤±5℃; nine-point empty-furnace measurement | Deviation reference, measurement area and test temperatures were not detailed; this is not a loaded-workpiece result |

For stability over time, agree how control fluctuation will be evaluated. For spatial distribution, align sensor positions, deviation reference, test temperature and loading state. Only then can the proposed figures be compared with current requirements; choosing the smallest “accuracy” number is insufficient.

The proposal included a side thermocouple entry and a system-check port ≤50 mm from the control-thermocouple opening. Support tubes and compression fittings located process and test thermocouples, with insulation wool sealing gaps during calibration. Check port positions, probe location and sealing accordingly. Test access does not mean a test was performed or passed. No corresponding test report was obtained; these remain proposal figures and access provisions.

## Match measurement requirements to sensors, instruments and interlocks

The 2021 quotation separately listed one Eurotherm 2604 programme temperature controller, another instrument of the same model for overtemperature alarm, and one Yokogawa FX1006 temperature recorder. The two identical-model instruments served different functions; verify each instrument's wiring and role.

Three type-N thermocouple assemblies were listed. One dual-element assembly was intended to control and record a location approximating the median temperature; another dual-element assembly would alarm and record at the high point; a single-element assembly would record at the low point. High and low positions were to follow the latest temperature-uniformity survey.

If different recording positions are required, revise the measuring points, wiring and recording channels before selecting instruments. Instrument counts help define responsibilities, but complete protection also depends on sensors, switching devices and recovery logic.

The proposed interlocks permitted heating once the door was clamped shut, cut heating as the door rose, and permitted bogie movement only after the door reached its raised position. Overtemperature would trigger audible and visual alarms and cut heating, with automatic recovery below the setpoint. Electrical specialists should compare normal heating, loading and fault recovery separately with current requirements. These are historical design descriptions, not operating instructions or verified protection results for a current furnace.

The door used proposed chain lifting and an electromagnetic-brake motor; the bogie was self-propelled. Position retention on power loss, personnel isolation during handling and maintenance, and recovery conditions were not detailed. Ask the supplier to propose design and verification arrangements, and confirm site cooperation responsibilities against actual handling and maintenance methods before adopting the arrangement.

## Compare quotations together with equipment, site and testing responsibilities

The historical RT4-75-6 quotation dated 2 November 2021 included the fan and lifting accessories, plus equipment design, manufacture, transport, installation and commissioning; equipment foundations were excluded. This scope applied to the historical configuration, not current commercial terms.

“Lifting accessories included” in the quotation and the customer's responsibility to provide an overhead crane for installation and commissioning were separate provisions. The sources did not detail the accessories, their interface with the site crane or separate pricing. Do not interpret this as Suneng supplying the factory overhead crane. If the customer now supplies an item, separately confirm who provides it and who designs and verifies its interfaces; the supplier must then define the revised price scope. Do not subtract an assumed component price from a historical total.

The listed furnace base was an equipment component, distinct from site foundation construction. The technical proposal required the customer to provide air, water, electricity, gas and an overhead crane for installation and commissioning, while Suneng would provide training and accompanying documentation. A current quotation should allocate unloading, internal transport, external wiring and piping, and special tests, consistently with the cooling-water and control interfaces above. See [information needed for an industrial-furnace quotation (Chinese)](https://www.jssngyl.cn/zh/articles/gongye-lu-baojia-canshu).

## Can nine-point empty-furnace measurement represent loaded workpiece results?

Not directly. Empty-furnace temperature and loaded-workpiece temperature require verification against their respective measurement objects. The 2021 uniformity figure specified nine-point empty-furnace measurement; the commissioning section separately called for loaded heat-up commissioning after successful empty-furnace commissioning. If actual workpiece temperature or time to temperature matters, agree measurements with representative workpieces, fixtures and loading, and compare them with process requirements.

To establish temperature within a section, consider material, section geometry, heating method, loading and the target criterion together. Do not derive holding time from external dimensions alone. Nor does the distinction between furnace and workpiece temperature establish that the proposed furnace figure meets the current process. If the available evidence covers only empty-furnace results, add the required loaded verification as a separate testing task.

This historical proposal supports initial screening for batch heating of shafts and small parts: establish comparable loading and capacity first, align measurement objects next, and then allocate equipment, site and testing responsibilities.

Send dimensioned workpiece photographs or sections, a loading sketch, separate workpiece and fixture weights, and support details. Include the material, treatment purpose or preceding and following operations, and any established temperature, heat-up or holding requirements. For cycle assessment, also provide parts per batch, the target cycle or timing between operations. Unknown items can remain open while the available information identifies the next checks.
