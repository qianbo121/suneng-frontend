A Suneng proposal for three chamber quench-heating furnaces uses one rail-mounted fork manipulator to serve all three and a shared water-quench tank. Review process completion, manipulator availability, door opening and tray pickup, transfer and immersion, and tank readiness for the next batch as one sequence. Travel speed alone does not establish the quenching handover.

## Separate heating does not remove the discharge queue

The proposal has three identical chamber furnaces. Parts sit on heat-resistant steel trays, which the manipulator loads, retrieves and transfers. The draft allows only one furnace to discharge for quenching at a time and gives quenching the highest manipulator-task priority. This is a scheduling requirement, not proof that a discharge task can start immediately while another movement is in progress.

Integrated checks should include conflicts between loading, empty-tray return and the next furnace discharge. Define when the manipulator is released, when a door starts moving and permissible waiting after process completion. Staggering must not arbitrarily extend holding or discharge waits for a material; those times belong in its process documents.

## A 120-second target is not a confirmed process limit

The draft gives ≤120 s as a design target and describes pickup, travel, alignment and immersion assuming approximately 8 m from the furthest furnace to the tank. It also requires the buyer to confirm materials and the maximum permissible quench-transfer time. The target is therefore neither an established material-specific quench delay nor merely the time to travel 8 m.

Acceptance documents should define start and stop events: whether timing includes door opening, pickup and queuing, and whether immersion ends at first contact with the medium or when the full tray reaches its agreed immersed position. The draft does not reconcile these into one complete definition, and this page does not supply an assumed confirmed answer.

## Full-tray immersion needs a common load basis and tank-recovery check

The requirements table states maximum total load of 5 t, but later manipulator and heat calculations use 5 t of workpieces plus approximately 0.6 t of tray. These bases conflict. The record cannot establish 5 t as net workpiece capacity. First list the total of parts, tray and additional fixtures, then use a consistent input for hearth, tray, forks and tank design.

The tray enters the water with the parts and is included in the draft's heat input per immersion. Tank availability for the next furnace depends on the previous batch's water-temperature rise and cooling recovery as well as full-tray fit. Review actual loading, discharge temperature, permissible initial quench-water temperature and minimum batch interval. Draft water-volume and heat-exchange estimates are not verified selections.

## Buyer construction does not necessarily mean buyer purchase of every item

The supply description assigns water-tank and cooling-system construction to the buyer, with Suneng providing layout, elevations, embedded-part information, heat-exchanger selection and manipulator-alignment data. However, outstanding items also expressly require written confirmation of procurement and installation responsibility for the tank body, agitators, heat exchanger and cooling tower. The word construction alone does not establish that the buyer purchases every cooling component.

Similar enquiries should provide loaded-tray drawings and total weight, permissible process transfer time, expected curves for all three furnaces, and tank/building layouts. Check the furthest furnace's full movements and task conflicts first, followed by tank recovery, responsibilities and integration records. These establish whether shared equipment suits the requirement.
