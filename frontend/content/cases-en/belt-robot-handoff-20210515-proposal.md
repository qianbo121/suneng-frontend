A Suneng mesh-belt tempering-furnace proposal links discharge sensing, belt stoppage, door opening by the line controller and robot unloading in one sequence. Reviewing this equipment requires separate agreements for standalone furnace operation and coordinated whole-line operation.

## Identify who acts after the belt stops

The source requires a discharge door that allows the robot gripper to reach approximately 200 mm into the furnace, with a workpiece sensor approximately 200 mm inside the door near the outlet. Detection stops the belt and sends a signal to the industrial control system. That system then opens the discharge door and instructs the robot to unload. Once removal is complete, the door closes automatically. The 200 mm figures describe proposed pickup and sensing locations; they are not universal workpiece-positioning dimensions independent of robot access.

Procurement and integrated commissioning should distinguish workpiece detected from safe permission to reach in. Furnace, robot and line-control representatives should jointly confirm door-open position, removal-complete and next-feed conditions, particularly responses to failed removal, pickup timeout and interruption during the sequence. These are interface checks derived from the described sequence, not a claim that the source already provides a complete signal table or fault-recovery programme.

| Handover stage | Proposed arrangement | Boundary to confirm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival at discharge | Detection stops the belt and reports to line control | Sensor position must suit the actual part and gripper |
| Door opening and pickup | Line control opens the door and instructs the robot | How fully open position and pickup permission are confirmed |
| Pickup complete | Door closes automatically after removal | Completion signal and subsequent feeding conditions require definition |
| Line-controller failure | Furnace can operate separately under manual control | Verify standalone operation and restoration of line operation separately |

## Standalone heating does not complete line integration

The proposal requires the furnace to cooperate with feeding, pressure-forming and robot systems. It also requires manual standalone operation to process parameters if the whole-line industrial control system fails. These are different operating states. Continuous standalone operation cannot replace integrated commissioning.

The document also calls for zone temperatures, belt speed and inlet/outlet arrival signals to be uploaded to line control, which may control, record and monitor the furnace. An agreed data schedule should distinguish display-only information, adjustable settings and movement-permission signals. A network-interface requirement does not establish an agreed communication protocol, address table or completed site commissioning.

## Confirm heating parameters and belt-stop timing together

The proposal lists an effective heating zone of 3300 × 400 × 200 mm, 60 kW heating power and 3 temperature-control zones. It describes heating general metal parts and the blanks and intermediate forms of an unspecified product. This article retains that generic description rather than inferring material grades or a dedicated product from the furnace name.

The basic requirements and drive section give belt speed as 20–300 mm/min, while the parameter table gives 20–200 mm/min. The adopted range needs reconciliation. More importantly, waiting for discharge also extends the residence of other parts inside the furnace. Check robot pickup time, belt-stop frequency and the heating curve together. Heating length divided by one belt speed does not establish actual heating exposure or stable production capacity.

## Connect Suneng's equipment scope to whole-line acceptance

The stated equipment composition is the mesh-belt tempering furnace and control system. The presence of other line equipment does not mean Suneng supplies it all. The technical document requires operating, maintenance, installation and electrical information. Final-acceptance clauses require participation by the relevant line suppliers and countersignature by the automatic-control system integrator confirming data exchange and information functions. These are proposed obligations, not completed acceptance.

For a similar enquiry, first assemble workpiece travel orientation, robot pickup range, anticipated cycle and the party responsible for line control. Then check standalone scope, joint-commissioning responsibilities and data interfaces. This develops the heating requirement into defined upstream and downstream connections while preserving the boundary of actual verification.
