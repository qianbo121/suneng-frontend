When comparing high-temperature furnaces, first establish whether workpieces must enter water quickly after discharge and which mechanism will transfer them. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s documents dated 12 February 2023 contain a box furnace with fork extraction and a bogie-hearth furnace for the same project. The former proposes fork extraction followed by lowering into water; the latter carries workpieces into and out of the furnace on a bogie. The available records do not establish whether these were alternatives or parallel installations. This article compares handling routes and supply boundaries, without claiming interchangeability or suitability for the same process.

## Compare handling routes before parameters

In the box-furnace proposal, stainless-steel workpieces rest on internal support blocks and are extracted by forks into a buyer-supplied water tank. The bogie proposal uses the bogie for both support and furnace travel. If the requirement includes a discharge-to-water time, comparison must cover extraction, travel, lowering and the tank interface. Chamber size and heating power alone omit essential transfer interfaces.

The table compares handling scope in the two historical proposals, not equivalent equipment or complete processes. The box furnace is intended for periodic rapid loading/unloading and heat treatment, with stainless-steel workpieces taken into water after heating. The bogie furnace is intended for heat-treatment heating. The box overview states a maximum below 1200°C, while its table lists a maximum operating temperature of 1250°C; these statements are unresolved. The bogie proposal lists an adjustable operating temperature of 1190°C and a maximum working temperature of 1200°C in its overview. Check each use and temperature against the actual process; model names or overlapping temperatures do not establish interchangeability.

The box dimensions preserve the source's length × width × height order of 1.3 × 6 × 0.6 m. The quotation heading likewise states length 1300 × width 6000 × height 600 mm. The text does not map length and width to the door or travel direction, so they must not be swapped simply because width is larger. Site planning still requires a direction-labelled equipment drawing.

Working dimensions describe the proposed region, not maximum permissible workpiece dimensions. Show the combined workpiece-and-fixture envelope, centre of gravity and supports on one layout to compare loading, support and movement. The box proposal additionally requires a fork-handling capacity of at least 5 t for a uniformly distributed load excluding fixtures. This does not establish permissible local load on each fork tine.

## Include hot extraction and the tank in rapid-immersion scope

The box proposal includes hydraulic fork lifting, water-cooled forks in the quotation, a pneumatic safety pin locking the raised door, and interlocks between door position, fork position and heating. These separately concern hot-load support, raised-door retention and heating permission; describing them simply as “automatic control” loses those distinctions.

For a new rapid-immersion requirement, jointly check hot workpiece and fixture weights, fork contact positions, transfer start/end events and tank layout. The source does not provide complete interfaces for retaining loads during power or hydraulic failure, isolating movement for maintenance, or tank level, heat removal, water replenishment, steam and splashing. It therefore supports comparison of proposed handling routes, not confirmation that an existing arrangement meets a particular immersion cycle.

## Comparing bogie support, heating and measurement

The bogie proposal places resistance strips along the chamber sides and beneath the bogie hearth, using load-bearing refractory masonry and 30 mm-thick heat-resistant hearth plates. The actual loading drawing must show both support and space for heat transfer beneath the load.

The bogie configuration lists two control zones, two type S thermocouples and one two-channel recorder. Its table specifies furnace uniformity of ±8°C and furnace control accuracy of ±1°C. The box proposal specifies uniformity during holding of ≤±8°C and furnace stability of ≤±1°C. These names concern different assessed quantities. Reference bases, sensor layouts and load conditions are incomplete, so they must not be merged into one “measurement accuracy” for comparison.

## Reading the historical quotations together

The RX4-850-12 high-temperature box-furnace quotation dated 15 February 2023 includes 13% VAT and proposes design, manufacture, transport, installation and commissioning. Baskets and the water tank are buyer-supplied. External secondary wiring and cable trays between furnace and control cabinet are excluded.

The RT4-300-12 high-temperature bogie-hearth resistance-furnace quotation of 15 February 2023 also includes 13% VAT and proposes design, manufacture, transport, installation and commissioning, excluding external secondary wiring and trays between furnace and cabinet. The quotations concern different handling configurations. Adding them does not produce a complete production-line quotation, nor can their entire price difference be attributed to one mechanism.

## Two common selection questions

### Does a ≤30 s criterion suit any water-quenching process?

Not automatically. Match the process-defined timing start and finish to the proposal's “discharge to water” description, then compare hot load, travel and tank conditions. If complete immersion is the required finish event, define the corresponding transfer verification method explicitly.

### Are workpiece length, width and height enough to compare handling methods?

They allow only an initial space check. Further comparison needs material and cross-section, fixture weight and support drawings, process temperature and holding requirements, whether immersion is required and its timing rules, and existing tank and furnace-front access conditions. If information is incomplete, start with existing loading drawings and process requirements to identify additional transfer interfaces.
