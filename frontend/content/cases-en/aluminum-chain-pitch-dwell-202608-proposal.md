A Suneng discussion draft for an aluminium-alloy chain-conveyor furnace proposes discharge every 20–22 seconds per part, 4 parts per row and a combined heating-and-holding time of 120 minutes. Whether these requirements work together depends on actual fixture-row pitch and effective furnace length. Workpiece drawings are still missing, so calculations based on a provisional 100 mm pitch do not establish compliance with the site's space allowance.

## Convert the per-part cycle into the interval between rows

With 4 parts per row, the source calculation table gives a row-discharge interval of 80–88 seconds. This initially assumes all four parts are removed at the same per-part rate. Robot waiting, empty positions or multiple-part gripping requires recounting from the final movements. The row interval and one robot movement are not inherently equal.

## A larger pitch needs a longer zone for the same residence time

Using 21 seconds per part as an example gives one four-part row every 84 seconds. At 100 mm pitch, average advance is approximately 71.4 mm/min, and 120 minutes corresponds to approximately 8.57 m of effective travel. This is a geometric calculation from pitch and time, not a measured equipment speed or proof of completion at the required workpiece temperature.

The source also notes that increasing pitch to 120 mm or 150 mm increases the required effective length for the same discharge cycle and furnace residence. Actual part geometry, locating fixtures and gaps between rows are therefore needed before procurement. A fixed furnace length cannot justify assuming that larger parts still fit a 100 mm pitch.

## Distinguish effective length, machine length and site footprint

One passage gives effective furnace length as approximately 8.6 m; another uses 8.7 m plus the end sections to derive approximately 10.3 m overall, yet describes this as consistent with a 10 m reference footprint. The existing figures do not establish fit within 10 m. A final general arrangement should reconcile the two effective lengths and include feed/discharge ends, doors and robot working space. Site requirements must also determine whether exceeding the reference dimension is permissible.

The drive table separately states one tooth per advance and a 50.8 mm step, while fixture pitch is provisionally 100 mm. They need not be equal, but the design must explain how many advances bring a row to pickup, how waiting enters the average speed and how pickup position is confirmed. This article neither changes two 50.8 mm steps into 100 mm nor claims completed positioning integration.

## Residence time still needs workpiece-heating verification

The draft concerns heating 7xxx-series aluminium-alloy billets for a forging line, with interlocks between the discharge door, indexing movement and robot. Its 120 minutes is the total heating-and-holding time. Actual heating and residence after reaching the required temperature must correspond to material condition, individual weight and temperature records. Sufficient time inside the furnace alone does not prove that every billet meets downstream forging requirements.

An enquiry or configuration review should provide part and row-layout drawings, the permissible footprint, robot pickup orientation and required process curve. The source proposes on-site commissioning with the automation integrator. These inputs turn provisional calculations into equipment conditions that can be checked.
