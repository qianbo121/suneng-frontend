A revised Suneng track-link quenching-and-tempering proposal connects two heat-treatment lines to one forging line, with a shared loading robot dividing the flow. A slower average arrival rate on each furnace line does not mean the shared robot may run at half speed. Selection must consider total incoming rhythm, both lines' receiving states and the actual load including grippers.

## Draw the forging line and both furnace lines together

The project concerns XDL228–XDL350 track-link forgings weighing 10–44 kg each. The proposed sequence is post-forging temperature-controlled conveying, robotic distribution, heating, quenching, tempering and water cooling. The revision provides one residual-heat temperature-control conveyor, two treatment lines and one shared robot each for loading and unloading. These robots have different duties; they are not two loading robots, one per line.

## Why does shared loading remain at 22 seconds when each line receives every 44?

For XDL228, the source gives an overall forging interval of 22 seconds per part. With equal alternating distribution, each line receives a part every 44 seconds on average, but the shared loading robot still handles one every 22 seconds. The 44 seconds applies only to an individual furnace line and cannot relax the robot's complete pickup-and-placement cycle.

For XDL317, the table uses 30 kg per part and 36 seconds per part. Total arrival is 30 × 3600 ÷ 36 = 3000 kg/h, or 1500 kg/h per line with equal division. This applies to that model and equal-split assumption. It does not mean all five models run at the same tonnage or that one line can absorb the entire flow when the other stops.

## Check the robot with its gripper and actual route

The revision uses a 52 kg end load for robot selection checks and requires inclusion of thermal-insulation accessories, gripper centre-of-gravity offset and dynamic inertia against the manufacturer's load curves. The 44 kg figure is only the heaviest workpiece. Comparing a nominal robot payload with bare-part weight does not complete selection.

The draft says screenshots of the central gripping area are available for discussion, while complete three-dimensional models remain outstanding. Finalisation must confirm contact faces, centres of gravity of all five parts, the furthest station and interference when entering either line. Robot time should include identification, gripping, transfer, placement and withdrawal, rather than an unloaded arm swing alone.

The same revision gives a single-cycle target of no more than 18 seconds in the robot table and no more than 20 seconds in its item-by-item response. These need reconciliation. This article retains the 22-second overall arrival condition without presenting either target as achieved timing.

## Define destinations for temperature exceptions and line stoppages

The upstream conveyor proposes inlet/outlet infrared measurement, an off-temperature sorting outlet and a separate discharge for forging setup pieces. Coordinate them with shared-robot receiving: which parts may enter, which go to designated baskets and whether diverted parts still occupy the normal pickup position. Infrared readings concern their measurement locations; they do not establish the same temperature throughout a forging's core.

The source provisionally uses approximately 400°C entry as the main condition and retains discussion of cold-part capability. Stoppages of forging, either furnace line or the robot change waiting time and residual temperature. Receiving, buffering and feed-stop conditions must be checked against actual product curves. Use of forging residual heat does not establish full output for cold parts at the same rhythm.

## Acceptance must cover full distribution

The draft plans sectional trials, integrated checks and loaded trial production of five models. Shared-loading acceptance can therefore cover normal alternating feeding, unequal waiting states, one-line stoppage, off-temperature diversion and recovery after interruption. Record arrival intervals, actual robot movements and each line's receiving state for every case, then compare against the agreed process and output conditions.

An enquiry should provide measured weights and three-dimensional models of all five parts, actual forging cycles, discharge positions, permissible intermediate waits and the production plan during a single-line stoppage. These determine payload, reach and distribution logic. The earlier whole-line quotation cannot be applied unchanged to a revision with different lengths and two-line capacity relationships.
