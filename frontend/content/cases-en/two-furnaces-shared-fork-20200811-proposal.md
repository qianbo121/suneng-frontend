Suneng's 2020 heat-treatment equipment proposal lists two gas-fired heating furnaces, two enclosed cooling boxes and one shared loading/unloading fork truck. Those quantities do not establish continuous output. Put each batch's heating, transfer, cooling-box occupancy and unloading on one schedule to check whether the shared truck and cooling stations can keep pace.

## Identify equipment occupied by each batch

| Station | Proposed quantity or condition | Turnaround check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gas-fired heating furnaces | 2 | Actual heating, soaking and discharge-permission conditions |
| Enclosed cooling boxes | 2; proposed cooling from process temperature to ambient in 5 h | Entry time, start/end temperature and release time for each batch |
| Loading/unloading fork truck | 1 shared unit | Travel to each station, pickup/setdown and empty return time |

Two boxes provide two load positions, but are not necessarily dedicated one-to-one to the furnaces or guaranteed to finish batches together. Obtain batch times from actual process sheets and schedule potentially overlapping truck duties: charging, furnace discharge, transfer into cooling boxes and unloading. The source contains no validated automatic schedule.

## Cooling-box indication does not prove the workpiece core is cool

A fan at the lower rear wall supplies a plenum and floor ducts, then passes air through a perforated plate into the loading area. Separate exhaust extraction and spray dust removal are provided. Check airflow space against actual loading and fixture obstruction. The 5 h figure requires defined entering workpiece temperature, load and endpoint. Equipment dimensions or fan counts do not guarantee identical cooling times for every product.

One roof thermocouple indicates cooling-box temperature. Furnace and cooling-box temperatures are sent to the truck touchscreen. Displaying them does not measure workpiece cores or establish complete logic for automatic unloading once a temperature is reached. Permission to proceed must follow the current process and measurement plan.

## Reconcile loading definitions separately from motion permissions

The heating-furnace table states 5 t per batch including brackets and support blocks. The cooling-box introduction and table differ, respectively excluding and including those items. The truck structure requires at least 6 t, uniformly loaded and including fixtures. Similar numbers do not prove every station can accept the same batch. Nor is “at least 6 t” an absolute 6 t upper limit. Reconcile net workpieces, fixtures and total transferred weight in the final revision.

Motion conditions include allowing fork entry only at the door's highest position, lowering the door only after forks fully withdraw, and starting heating only after closure. These are proposed transfer/heating interlocks. Include position detection, station states and fault handling in commissioning. Truck travel speed alone cannot establish total load-change time.

## Bring supply and exhaust interfaces into the site layout

Suneng was to supply furnaces, cooling boxes, the truck, exhaust ducts, spray dust removal and controls. The buyer prepares foundations and agreed utilities. The cooling-box section explicitly makes post-spray wastewater treatment user-supplied; incorporate it in the general arrangement and site connections. Historical low-NOx, uniformity and energy-saving claims are not measured conclusions here. Actual turnaround and treatment quality require agreed charges, trial plans and records.
