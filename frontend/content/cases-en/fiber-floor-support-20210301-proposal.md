Fibre hearth insulation does not mean the fibre carries workpiece weight. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s bogie-hearth tempering proposal signed 1 March 2021 adds transverse I-section supports and load-bearing columns within the fibre lining, with column feet bearing on longitudinal/transverse frame beams. For wide-bogie loads, identify the force path first, then assess insulation and circulation around it.

## Trace load-bearing members separately from insulation

The proposed path is workpiece support to transverse I-sections, then columns, then the bogie's bottom longitudinal and transverse steel beams. Aluminium silicate fibre modules insulate the frame interior. Overlay workpiece/fixture feet on the support drawing to check whether contacts lie on the intended load-bearing members.

If feet miss the transverse sections or new spacers/stands are added, contact positions and force paths change. The equipment designer must assess bridging and support. “All-fibre hearth” does not mean the entire surface accepts direct loads, and a total below the rating does not permit arbitrary concentrated loading.

## Show supports and circulation space on one loading drawing

The source specifies 600 kW, 4 front/rear control zones, and 4 × 5.5 kW circulation fans with guides. Added transverse spacers or enclosed fixtures require both structural checks and assessment of changed airflow space. Fan count does not define four independent circuits.

Uniformity is ≤±5°C in the table and ≤±10°C in the control section. Reconcile final acceptance conditions. Workpiece contacts can first be assessed against transverse supports; thermal performance needs testing under the relevant load and temperature. Support geometry alone establishes neither workpiece temperature spread nor hardness.

## Which support information must be agreed before installation?

Suneng proposes assembly, layout and foundation drawings and installation/commissioning after arrival. Loading information should give geometry, weight and feet; equipment drawings should locate transverse sections, columns and frame beams. Align those drawings before empty and loaded heat-up commissioning, so successful empty movement is not mistaken for verified real loading.
