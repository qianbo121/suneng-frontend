An August 2026 Suneng proposal for wound cylinders assigns two furnaces to first-stage curing and one to second-stage curing. Assess the handover between the two stages before effective operating time, transfer and fixture traceability. The three furnaces do not process three independent streams of finished products.

## Assess two process stages before three machines

The proposal concerns wound cylinders no longer than 1200 mm, no greater than 350 mm in diameter and no heavier than 8 kg. Furnaces No. 1 and No. 2 perform first-stage curing in parallel, with proposed residence of 2 h at 80°C and 2 h at 120°C. No. 3 performs second-stage curing with 1 h in each segment. These are the proposal's process conditions, not curing recipes for other resins or cylinder types.

| Item | Proposal record | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-stage curing | 2 furnaces in parallel | Two segments totalling 4 h per furnace |
| Second-stage curing | 1 furnace | Two segments totalling 2 h, receiving the first stage's output |
| Proposed positions per furnace | 56 groups, 5 cylinders each | Verify fixture positions; this is not annual output |

Assuming all groups contain 5 cylinders, all 56 groups belong to the relevant residence process and operation is continuous and stable, each first-stage furnace theoretically passes 70 cylinders/h, or 140 cylinders/h combined. The second-stage furnace also passes 140 cylinders/h. Matching the two stages under these assumptions does not produce 280 finished cylinders/h, because the same product must undergo the next stage.

## Annual output first requires a defined operating-hour basis

The proposal uses 300 days/year and 22 h/day, giving theoretical throughput of approximately 924,000 cylinders/year at each stage. This assumes all 6600 hours are effective steady production; starts/stops, changeovers, material waits and downstream blocking have not yet been scheduled for the customer.

If 300 days × 22 h is scheduled time and only 95% supports effective production at that rate, conditional output becomes approximately 877,800 cylinders/year. If the 22 h already excludes losses, multiplying by 95% again is inappropriate. Confirm which time basis the target uses. This page does not present the proposal's 900,000-cylinder figure as achieved annual output.

## Identical equipment does not remove work-in-process constraints

The three furnaces are proposed as identical, with process roles changed through recipes. If one first-stage furnace stops, changing the second-stage furnace temporarily to first-stage duty does not automatically restore second-stage capacity. Review already first-cured work in process, permissible waits, transfer buffers and capacities of both reassigned stages together. Identical equipment does not mean the line cannot stop.

Fixtures must match actual cylinders. The proposal lists 56 racks with 5 positions each, customised to winding fixtures, with indexed positioning and mechanical stops. Fixture drawings, robot gripping points and site layout remain unconfirmed. Nominal positions do not replace full-envelope, gripping and interference checks.

## Link zone records to each cylinder's process history

The draft proposes 10 independently controlled temperature zones, curves linked to fixture numbers and actual-position tracking. Check which furnace and stage each cylinder passes through, the recipe used and how records continue after pauses or transfers. Zone-temperature displays alone do not prove completion of the required curing duration for that cylinder.

Distinguish local storage from replay after a network outage. Local curve/alarm storage and export are retained, but 72 h offline retransmission is excluded from pricing under the requirement's instruction not to consider it for now. Local retention does not establish automatic upload with original timestamps after reconnection. Reconcile that function separately in data-interface acceptance.

## Map transition zones and supply interfaces before finalisation

The proposal specifies full-load ±3°C for holding plateaus and recommends a separate criterion for the transition from 80°C to 120°C. That recommendation remains unconfirmed, not an agreed acceptance rule. Define the start of each residence period, the positions occupied by transition and whether measurements concern furnace space or fixture locations travelling with cylinders. The theoretical throughput does not replace this check.

Provide cylinder and winding-fixture drawings, both curing curves, effective annual operating hours and the workshop layout. Suneng proposes three furnaces with transport, control and integration arrangements. Physical supply boundaries for robots, transfer equipment, external exhaust treatment and site energy must be checked against the final list. Whole-line commissioning does not make the furnace supplier responsible for the entire cylinder-manufacturing line.
