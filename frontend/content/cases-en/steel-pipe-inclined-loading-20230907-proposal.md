Chamber depth and whole-pipe length are different dimensions. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s bogie-hearth heating proposal dated 7 September 2023 specifies a 4 m-deep chamber and 4–13 m pipes, inserted at an incline with protective grooves at the internal contact end and soft-contact support beneath the opening. First establish the length requiring heat and the loading orientation, then check internal/external supports. The 13 m range does not mean an entire pipe fits inside or heats uniformly in a 4 m chamber.

## Show the complete pipe and the section requiring heat

The source gives φ20–φ89 mm diameter, 3–6 mm wall thickness and 4–13 m length, with chamber depth 4 m and width/height both 1.5 m. Pipe length describes intended workpieces; chamber size describes equipment space. Read both with the inclined-loading arrangement. For new parts, mark the target section, inserted length, incline direction and external remainder. Maximum pipe length does not establish whole-part heating capability.

| Position | Proposed arrangement | Meaning for the loading drawing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal pipe-to-bogie contact | Heat-resistant steel protective groove | Check contact, diameter fit and restraint against movement. |
| Lower furnace opening | Insulated load-bearing support plate 600 mm above the chamber floor | This height does not define a universal loading angle. |
| Top of lower door | Refractory fibre-cloth cushion for soft contact | Check the pipe's opening passage and support contact. |

These positions jointly determine passage through the opening. Changed diameter, thickness, length or quantity requires renewed checks of internal seating, opening contact and external supports. One support point cannot replace whole-pipe load/stability assessment. The source supplies no project-specific inclination or complete external-support drawing; no universal angle should be calculated and published from these dimensions.

## Match upper and lower doors to the occupied pipe space

The upper door lifts; the lower door is fixed to the moving bogie and has a soft top cushion. A fibre-cloth heat curtain beneath the upper door reduces heat loss. Check pipe passage, upper-door closing boundary and flexible coverage. The curtain does not establish a tight seal at every angle, and its flexible material is not added rigid load support.

Elements around the chamber and beneath the hearth form front, middle and rear zones; heat-resistant steel cover plates protect the bottom. Zoning permits separate regional adjustment, but target-section temperature assessment still requires actual insertion length, support obstruction and sensor positions. Three furnace-control points do not represent temperatures along the entire pipe.

## Keep the two heating-time requirements separate

The source requires the furnace to reach 600°C within 1.5 h and separately the workpiece to reach 400°C within no more than 1 h. Different objects and targets cannot be merged into “an entire 13 m pipe reaches 600°C in 1 h”. Define initial condition, quantity, target section, sensors and timing events before assessing applicability.

| Key information | Recorded value | Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chamber depth | 4 m | Used with inclined loading; not whole-pipe length. |
| Pipe length range | 4–13 m | Not the range of uniform heating along a complete pipe. |
| Heating power | 240 kW | Front/middle/rear zones; not actual energy use. |

## Progressing loading and site interfaces

The proposal permits heating only after the upper door is compressed in position, cuts heating when it rises, and permits bogie travel after it is fully raised. With pipes extending through the opening, check door limits, pipe restraint, external supports and personnel space together. Empty-bogie operation alone is insufficient.

Suneng proposes site installation/commissioning; the buyer prepares facilities and provides air, water, electricity, gas and overhead-crane support. Start with pipe drawings, target section, batch quantity, permissible deformation and process curve, plus space outside the opening and existing stands. Assess this loading drawing before deciding whether grooves, cushioning and split doors can be reused.
