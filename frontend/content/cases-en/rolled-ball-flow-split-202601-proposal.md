An 80–100 mm rolled-ball tempering-line project places an adjustable splitter at the pre-tempering air-cooler outlet. One branch feeds the associated tempering furnace; the other leads to another furnace 14 m away. The split must match both receiving capacities. Both branches cannot simultaneously be assigned the whole line's output.

## Allocate one incoming ball stream between the branches

The original agreement specifies 14 t/h for 80, 90 and 100 mm steel balls for each of three machines: pre-tempering air cooling, tempering and post-tempering air cooling. The side outlet permits adjustable distribution, but this does not establish that two tempering furnaces can each receive 14 t/h simultaneously. First establish total incoming flow to the pre-tempering cooler, then allocate it between branches. The other furnace's effective capacity, process and return route require supporting information.

The furnace 14 m away describes a receiving location, not a 14 m chamber length or an automatically included additional purchase. The equipment list contains one pre-tempering cooler, one tempering furnace and one post-tempering cooler. Cross-station connections require a site layout.

## Different chain speeds mean different treatment conditions

Each machine has its own drive and speed adjustment. All list motor frequency of 20–60 Hz, yet chain speeds differ. Coordination should use actual distance travelled per minute, ball loading on the belt and accumulation at interfaces. Equal frequency does not mean equal conveyor speed. After changing the split, check concentration of loading on either branch and starvation of the other.

Effective lengths and speeds give transit times of approximately 31.5–94.4 minutes before tempering, 66.7–200 minutes in tempering and 52.8–158.3 minutes after tempering. These differ slightly from the source's approximate ranges of 30–90, 70–210 and 50–150 minutes. Final documents should reconcile timing boundaries and actual speed ranges. These calculations describe geometric transit, not verified holding after target temperature or cooling performance.

## Address carried-over water together with receiving elevations

The pre-tempering cooler connects to a drum downstream of quenching. The source specifically requires accumulated quench water in the receiving chute to be returned to the upstream water tank and support for adjacent quench-water pipes. The interface therefore includes carried-over water and existing pipework, as well as ball flow. A chute based only on the distance between machines is insufficient.

The proposed belt elevations are 2350 mm at the pre-tempering cooler outlet, 1650 mm at both the tempering-furnace inlet and outlet, and 1050 mm at the post-tempering cooler inlet. These give nominal drops of approximately 700 mm and 600 mm. Transition-chute geometry, ball sizes and site receiving positions still need design. A height difference does not establish that any inclined chute will avoid jamming and distribute balls evenly.

The agreement also proposes an emergency ball track and receiving basket. Design should define the abnormal-condition destination, holding capacity and conditions for returning to the normal route. This page identifies the interface without turning the source's sliding-plate description into loaded site operating instructions.

## Verify treatment after changing the split

The project requires pre-tempering air cooling from 160°C after quenching to ≤80°C, followed by low-temperature tempering at ≤300°C. Post-tempering cooling is assessed from 200°C to ≤50°C. After any split change, each destination still needs checks of actual treatment conditions, product size and output records; correct splitter movement alone is insufficient. These temperatures belong to this agreement, not a universal process for other materials or ball diameters.

Suneng is to fabricate inter-machine inlet/outlet chutes and install the equipment package, with the buyer providing site cooperation. Final acceptance uses unloaded and loaded trials and actual post-trial performance. Establishing how much the other furnace can receive requires its process, receiving capacity and route drawing, followed by joint loaded verification. A splitter alone does not establish increased line capacity.
