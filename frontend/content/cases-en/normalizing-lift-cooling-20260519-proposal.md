## One conveyor section governs both cooling exposure and handover

| Proposed item | Figure or configuration | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effective lifting-belt width | 1100 mm | Match transverse loading, side guards and upstream/downstream chutes |
| Belt inclination | 20° | Check workpiece orientation and movement on the incline |
| Side guards | 120 mm high | Address lateral loss; not all slipping or transfer issues |
| Forced cooling and measurement | Fans above belt; working-area temperature monitoring | Confirm representative workpiece cooling; area temperature is not load temperature |

The source combines inclined lifting, side guards and overhead forced cooling in one mechanism. Match representative loading to the belt and check accumulation or reorientation at transfers and how mutual shielding reduces exposed cooling area. Guard height alone does not establish prevention of longitudinal slipping or all part collisions. These are structural review questions requiring actual-load validation.

## Select speed for cooling and receiving together

The process sends normalising discharge into rapid cooling, then directly from the elevator onto the tempering-inlet conveyor. Belt speed is adjustable, but the parameter summary and structural description give different minimum speeds, 30 and 50 mm/min. Neither can be used to promise maximum cooling dwell without reconciliation. Confirm the final speed range, effective air-exposed length, loading pattern and tempering-inlet receiving conditions together.

The overview specifies a 1000 mm normalising furnace, 1100 mm elevator belt and 1200 mm tempering furnace. Increasing downstream width does not automatically spread parts evenly or prove higher output. Check chute dimensions, discharge direction and receiving area. The elevator's rotatable discharge outlet must match the layout drawing.

## Verify area temperature separately from workpiece temperature

The rapid-cooling unit has one thermocouple explicitly for working-area monitoring, with overtemperature, overload and abnormal-running alarms. It provides area information, not proof that every surface or core reaches the pre-tempering requirement. Approximately 1000 kg/h also remains conditional on workpiece shape, dimensions, individual weight, piling and process. Final acceptance includes distortion and hardness. Agree representative parts and conformity criteria before checking cooling and transfer.

## Define Suneng's and the site's preparations

The proposal includes a normalising furnace, rapid-cooling unit with elevator, tempering furnace and controls, with individual and interlocked operation. Suneng proposes positioning, installation and secondary wiring; the buyer prepares primary workshop power, utility connections and acceptance workpieces. Final controls should define coordination on upstream feed stoppage, elevator faults and inability of downstream equipment to receive parts. “Interlocks provided” does not prove every abnormal condition has been validated.

Provide representative dimensions, piece weights, loading pattern, cooling requirements between normalising and tempering, and the layout between furnaces. First assess inclined lifting and discharge suitability, then speed, cooling exposure and process acceptance. Approximately 1000 kg/h is not a guarantee for all workpieces before these conditions are defined.
