Extending a bogie-hearth furnace requires more than another section of steel shell. Suneng's 2019 retrofit proposal increases effective working length from 7 m to 10 m, adding a bogie section, wheels, seals, 300 kW of heating and two control zones, followed by retesting of the complete furnace. The review must cover integration of both new and existing structures and controls.

## Review dimensions, heating and control under one retrofit scope

| Item | Proposed change | Review boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effective working length | 7000 mm to 10000 mm | Width 3000 mm and height 2500 mm retained |
| Heating power | 750 kW plus 300 kW, totalling 1050 kW | Check power supply and control for new and existing systems together |
| Temperature control | 6 existing zones plus 2, totalling 8 | Retain 6 existing control points and add 2; do not test only the new cabinet |

Rated temperature remains 700°C, and the overview proposes increasing maximum loading to 50 tonnes. This does not scale every original specification in proportion to length. Confirm post-retrofit loading against actual workpieces, fixtures and supports. An extra 3 metres does not establish throughput or cycle time.

## Connect the new bogie section to tracks and seals

The furnace is opened in its middle-to-rear portion to insert a section; a section is also inserted into the rear of the bogie. Two rows of wheels and corresponding extensions to the load-bearing tracks are added. Side seals retain cylinder clamping and reuse the existing cylinders. Before construction, check that the original frame, tracks, connections and cylinders suit the final layout. Reuse does not remove the need for inspection.

The source also increases the working chamber and bogie working surface from 7.24 m to 10.24 m, whereas effective length in the table changes from 7 m to 10 m. These are different dimensional definitions. Keep effective working space distinct from structural dimensions; 10.24 m is not the new effective heating length.

## Two added control zones, but cooling arranged for the whole furnace

One two-zone control cabinet is added and integrated with the original system. The industrial computer is reused with upgraded software and operating screens. Check old and new zone names, measurement points, outputs and records so operators can identify each temperature's location. Adding two displayed numbers does not complete the control retrofit.

The extended furnace's cooling arrangement is divided into four sections. Roof extraction outlets use an external fan blowing cold air into hot-exhaust ducts to create suction, with cooling rate adjusted as required. This whole-furnace arrangement is not equivalent to the eight temperature-control zones. Air blown into exhaust ducts is not direct air impingement on workpieces. The specified cooling rate of ≤50°C/h applies only over 600–400°C, not every temperature range.

## Validate the complete modified furnace

The proposal gives post-retrofit furnace-temperature uniformity of ≤±10°C. This is a proposed criterion for whole-furnace testing under jointly agreed conditions. Define measurement points, temperatures and loading in the test plan; the figure is not an existing measured result.

The source explicitly requires renewed performance testing of the entire furnace and updated assembly drawings, electrical schematics and operating/maintenance documents. Tests should reflect the final effective zone, load and controls, including operation across old/new connections. Historical six-zone records do not establish eight-zone performance, and separate testing of the new section does not replace whole-furnace verification.

Review responsibilities against the retrofit list. Suneng was to design, manufacture, supply, install and commission the equipment and modify the original furnace. The buyer prepares foundations and site conditions and supplies power/control cables, conduits, trays and cable carriers between cabinet and furnace; the seller installs them. The buyer handles process commissioning with seller assistance. No construction or whole-furnace test report is provided here, so these agreements are not reported as completed retrofit outcomes.
