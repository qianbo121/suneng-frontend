When two recorder types are supplied, first identify their sensor inputs. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s two box-furnace proposals dated 11 April 2022 cover 180 kW and 130 kW units, with a paper recorder for control points and a six-channel paperless recorder for workpiece-monitoring points. Compare sensors, channels and measured objects first. “Dual recording” does not automatically mean backup data or verified workpiece temperature.

## Connect sensors, channels and measured objects

| Recording arrangement | Source description | Mapping to confirm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper temperature recording | Heating, holding and cooling at temperature-control points | Control point to recorder channel. |
| Six-channel paperless recording | Monitoring-point temperatures on heated workpieces | Workpiece to monitoring thermocouple to channel. |

Both proposals list six high-temperature mineral-insulated thermocouples for workpiece monitoring. Six channels describe recording capacity; six sensors describe hardware. Their actual locations and represented workpiece regions need a loading/sensor drawing. Surface, contact and internal temperatures differ; workpiece monitoring is not automatically core measurement.

To establish whether a workpiece experienced a required temperature process, assess its monitoring records. To review furnace control, inspect the corresponding control-point records. Backup or cross-checking requires confirmed common measurement objects, time alignment and data paths. The two recorder types alone do not establish that relationship.

## The same recording approach does not mean the same load conditions

| Furnace proposal | Rated power | Rated furnace load | Table chamber dimensions, length × width × height |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 180 kW box furnace | 180 kW | 1.5 t | 1900 × 1350 × 850 mm |
| 130 kW box furnace | 130 kW | 1.0 t | 1900 × 900 × 600 mm |

Both are proposed for high-temperature treatment heating of small and medium metal parts, with a 1200°C upper temperature limit. Chamber dimensions describe equipment space; usable working dimensions must allow for the agreed placement and heating requirements. Chamber size does not directly establish workpiece envelope or net loading. Both use front/rear two-zone heating. Map sensors to those zones, but do not infer the six workpiece-sensor positions from zone count.

## Assess recording within the heating and loading arrangement

Both proposals specify hearth plates with a total design thickness of 30 mm, hooked sectional joints and a reinforcing grid underneath. The source describes load support, heat dissipation and reducing scale entry into the bottom elements. Select monitoring locations using actual placement and supports on those plates; a channel list without representative load positions is insufficient.

Water-cooled, variable-speed circulation fans are proposed, with different speed arrangements for low and medium/high temperatures. These are historical design descriptions, not operating instructions to copy. Agree fan, cooling and loaded verification conditions in the new design. Uniformity clauses contain different conditions and values; a smaller deviation from one clause is not a guarantee for the entire temperature range or workpieces.

## What usable acceptance records should be retained?

Suneng proposes site installation and commissioning; the buyer prepares facilities from foundation information and supplies air, water, electricity, gas and an overhead crane. Empty commissioning, loaded heat-up commissioning and acceptance against the agreement are proposed steps, not completed records. For dual recording, additionally define the channel schedule, sensor layout, sensor/recorder verification documents and identification of records from the same batch.

Provide workpiece/loading drawings, process curve, locations requiring records and the acceptance basis. First decide which temperatures must serve as acceptance evidence, then specify channels and equipment. Furnace trends and critical workpiece temperatures require different measurement arrangements.
