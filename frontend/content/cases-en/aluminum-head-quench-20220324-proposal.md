A continuous venting path is needed inside an inverted dished head, and the vent structure, water-entry direction and basket descent must work together. Jiangsu Suneng Industrial Furnace Co., Ltd.'s aluminium-alloy dished-head quenching furnace proposal, dated 24 March 2022, incorporates a segmented tubular venting device with openings into the basket to discharge internal gas during rapid immersion. The tank also has mechanical agitation directed towards its centre and upward water jets from beneath the basket. Equipment selection should assess both transfer speed and the conditions that let water reach the inner surface.

## 1. Suneng places the venting device inside the basket to support water entry into inverted heads

An inverted dished head acts like a cover with its opening facing down. If trapped air has no clear exit during immersion, it affects water entry into the cavity. Suneng's proposal expressly describes the segmented tubular venting structure with openings as a way to discharge internal gas promptly so that the workpiece can make timely, adequate contact with water.

Assess this equipment against the actual head loading. Changes in head depth, opening, stacking arrangement or support positions may change where gas collects and how it can escape. The basket drawing should show a continuous route from the cavity into the venting structure, through its connecting sections and out of the outlet. Workpieces, spacers and supports must not obstruct the water-entry or venting locations.

The proposal specifies the structure and its purpose but provides no hole diameters, quantities or layout that can simply be applied to other heads. A procurement comparison should therefore result in basket and venting drawings matched to the actual head, rather than only a quotation checkbox for a venting device. Before reusing an existing basket for another size, assess whether its vent paths and securing points suit the new load, then decide whether to retain, modify or replace it.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Segmented tubular venting device with openings inside the basket | Discharges gas from inside inverted heads | Whether each enclosed cavity has an appropriate venting path |
| Four 7.5 kW mechanical agitators | Push water through guide tubes towards the tank centre | Whether the actual basket obstructs the main water passages |

## 2. Verify the 15-second transfer and timely wetting of the inner surface separately

The proposal specifies a workpiece quench-transfer time of no more than 15 seconds. Its structural description also states that the load descends rapidly, then switches to a lower, adjustable speed as it approaches the quench-water surface. This arrangement addresses both rapid transfer and movement near the water surface and into the intended position.

The 15 seconds is a proposed transfer criterion. Acceptance arrangements should define the starting and stopping events, distinguishing door operation, descent, contact with the water surface and the specified immersion endpoint. Movement time for the lifting mechanism alone cannot prove that water has reached every inner surface promptly.

Assessing the venting device requires observing water entry and gas discharge with the complete load, together with appropriate workpiece temperature measurement and product inspection under the agreed process. Bubbles at the water surface alone do not prove that all inner surfaces requiring cooling meet the requirement.

**If transfer time meets the agreement but inner-surface cooling does not, continue investigating venting and water-entry paths, load obstructions and local water flow. Simply increasing descent speed cannot replace these checks.**

## 3. Trapped gas changes buoyancy: securing the load requires more than its dry weight

After immersion, both the workpiece and basket experience buoyancy. Gas trapped inside the head occupies space that water could otherwise fill, increasing the displaced volume. Under hydrostatic conditions, buoyancy can be estimated as water density × gravitational acceleration × displaced volume. [Buoyancy and displaced volume](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/14-4-archimedes-principle-and-buoyancy)

As an order-of-magnitude example only, suppose that at a particular immersion position, trapped gas occupies an additional 100 litres, or 0.1 m³, compared with the fully flooded condition. Taking water density as approximately 1,000 kg/m³ and gravitational acceleration as 9.81 m/s², the additional static buoyancy is approximately:

**1,000 × 9.81 × 0.1 = 981 N, or about 0.98 kN, equivalent to the weight of approximately 100 kg of water.**

This is not the project's trapped-gas volume or design load. It only illustrates how venting affects forces. Actual calculations must also account for displacement by the workpiece and basket, weight distribution, and dynamic effects from descent, water flow and vaporisation. This example cannot directly determine clamping force or hydraulic-cylinder selection.

When head depth, stacking or support arrangements change, review venting and load restraint together. If net upward force or uneven loading could lift or tilt a workpiece, adjust securing, support or venting arrangements and check the forces on the corresponding mechanisms. Start with the actual contact points between the workpiece and basket, rather than relying only on the equipment's stated load tonnage.

## 4. Water circulation and tank cooling serve different purposes

In this proposal, Suneng uses four mechanical agitators to push water through guide tubes towards the tank centre. External circulation pumps organise upward jets from the centre beneath the basket.

Vent paths give gas a route out; agitation and jets organise water flow. They must work together with the actual load. After changing the basket or head size, reassess the relative positions of jet centres, openings and spacers. A running pump does not prove that water-entry conditions inside the cavity are unchanged.

The tank cooling system removes heat. The proposal specifies a water-temperature rise of no more than 5°C for a single quench. Separately, with water above 25°C, it specifies cooling capability of at least 5°C per hour. The first relates to heat entering the water from one batch of workpieces and fixtures; the second affects water temperature before the next batch. Dividing these figures does not establish a whole-line output of one batch per hour.

Evaluate these requirements against actual loading, immersion conditions, water quantity, initial water temperature and cooling conditions. Record the highest water temperature during quenching and the temperature before the next immersion. Even when tank temperature meets the agreement, assess the interaction of venting and jets using workpiece cooling and product results.

## 5. Validate venting, lifting and protection with the load

The proposal includes a safety pin in the lifting mechanism and a limit cylinder at the basket's highest position, providing support for prolonged load holding and shutdown maintenance. The trolley has travel limits and a door interlock. The tank has a digital level indicator, temperature display and overtemperature alarm. The control system also lists interlocks between the fan, heaters, door, hydraulic system and quench tank, along with protection such as cutting off the main circuit on overtemperature.

These measures directly concern hot-load handling. Validation should also address load holding after loss of power or hydraulic pressure, permitted movements when the tank is out of position or its level is abnormal, personnel exclusion, and protection against steam and splashing within the corresponding controls and site arrangements. A listed level indicator does not justify claiming an undocumented low-level quench-inhibit function.

| Change or observation | Check first | Required outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A deeper head or a different opening | Cavity shape, vent inlets and outlets, and load restraint | Venting matched to the new workpiece, with forces reassessed |
| More layers or different spacers | Water entry, vent paths and support obstructions at each layer | Adjust loading or the basket, then verify cooling and product results |
| Transfer time meets requirements, but local cooling remains inadequate | Inner-surface water entry, venting and local water flow | Adjust paths and layout rather than only descent speed |
| One batch performs adequately, but water temperature rises over successive batches | Heat entering the tank, actual water quantity and sustained heat removal | Adjust cooling equipment or batch scheduling |

Suneng's proposal includes the quenching furnace, tank, lifting mechanism and control system in the supply scope, with site work such as foundations, rails and embedded items coordinated under the parties' agreed responsibilities. Use the same representative workpieces and basket for loading, venting and motion validation, and relate heat-treatment results to equipment-movement records.

If you plan to quench inverted aluminium-alloy dished heads, start with head drawings, material and heat-treatment requirements, individual weight, stacking arrangement, photographs of the basket and supports, and existing transfer and water-temperature records. Existing drawings and loading photographs are also a useful starting point.
