Reversal in a regenerative furnace is more than alternating ignition between two sides. Suneng's forging-furnace proposal describes one side firing while the other exhausts flue gas and stores its heat; after reversal, their roles switch. Review reversal mode, exhaust-temperature monitoring and valve actions together. A reversal interval alone does not establish heat recovery or satisfactory workpiece heating.

## The two sides exchange firing and exhaust duties

Hot flue gas passes through the exhaust-side passage into a regenerator, while cold air passes through the previously heated regenerator on the opposite side before combustion. Reversal exchanges the firing and exhaust duties of the two sides. Drawings should show air, fuel and flue-gas paths at each stage, rather than treating reversal as simply switching two burner groups on and off.

## Three described modes do not establish one completed control system

The control section introduces time-based, temperature-based and manual reversal. The parameter table also mentions forced reversal when exhaust temperature exceeds its limit. These describe proposed control approaches but do not specify the primary mode for a new project, settings, or responses to valve-position or sensor faults. Obtain a control description defining modes, triggers and action boundaries, then use it to prepare commissioning checks.

The source separately addresses natural-gas pressure, combustion-air pressure and furnace-pressure indication and control, with protections including flame monitoring and valve closure on power failure. Reversal, furnace-temperature regulation and combustion protection must work together under the final system drawing. One temperature reaching its setpoint does not establish all combustion conditions. No purge duration, reversal interval in seconds or site valve-operating procedure is added here.

## Assess exhaust temperature within the recovery process

The described recovery process alternately stores flue-gas heat in regenerators and releases it to combustion air. Actual recovery requires gas consumption, output, air and flue-gas measurements under the same loading and production conditions. A momentary exhaust temperature or control screen does not establish a fixed whole-furnace energy-saving rate. Historical reference photographs and screens in the source are not treated as acceptance data for this furnace size.

The document contains both below 150°C and below 200°C exhaust-temperature descriptions. Zoning is described both as “1 zone” and “each burner independently forms a temperature zone”. Reconcile measurement locations, operating conditions, zones and burner circuits for a new project. Do not select the more favourable figure or count nozzles in reference photographs to complete the equipment list.

## Include control handover and performance validation in scope

The seller's list includes the electrical control system and secondary service lines. The buyer's scope limits the installation distance between control cabinet and regenerative combustion equipment to 5 m, with excess cabling supplied by the buyer. The gas flowmeter is also user-supplied. Check these interfaces against the actual layout and measurement duties. “Secondary lines included” does not cover arbitrary distances or every meter.

Acceptance calls for empty-furnace commissioning followed by loaded commissioning, five agreed trial-production batches, and verification of the process curve, natural-gas consumption, furnace-temperature uniformity during soaking and workpiece temperature differences. The seller was to commission a qualified organisation for uniformity testing under buyer supervision. These are planned tasks, not completed results. Acceptance records should link actual billets and test conditions to reversal and temperature records.
