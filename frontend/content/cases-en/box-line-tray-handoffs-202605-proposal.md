A Suneng chamber-furnace line proposal connects heating furnaces, oil and water quench tanks and washing stations with an automatic fork-transfer vehicle. The washer separately has a gantry hook frame. Smooth whole-line movement requires checks of every receiving method, loaded-tray weight and available space. Repeated 600 × 600 × 500 mm dimensions do not establish compatibility.

## Fork support changes to hook handling inside the washer

The configuration includes high- and medium-temperature chamber furnaces, a tempering furnace, oil and water tanks and washing equipment. The fork vehicle transfers loads between stations. The oil and water tanks have separate lifting mechanisms; the washer's gantry hook frame moves parts between washing and rinsing tanks. These are different movement interfaces, rather than one set of forks performing every lift.

Tray drawings should show lifting surfaces, fork-entry direction, hook points and support surfaces after placement. Delivery beside the washer does not establish that hooks can engage. Define which mechanism bears the load during handover, which withdraws and whether station signals permit the next action.

## The 600 mm tray footprint is not the complete multilayer envelope

The draft lists lower and upper trays, posts and height-adjusting rings; some trays have frames or mesh baskets. The 600 × 600 mm footprint describes only the plan size. Loaded height includes tray structure, posts, parts and projections. Check the effective zones of furnaces, quench tanks and washer separately. Similar table dimensions do not remove the need for a complete loading drawing.

Two or more levels change not only part count but also centre of gravity during fork handling and hook conditions. Use representative parts to draw loaded envelopes and supports, then check door openings, forks, tank lifts, gantry travel and clearances. Multilayer capability does not establish fully loaded operation at every layer count.

## What do 200, 500 and 350 kg refer to?

The high-temperature furnace expressly allows 200 kg excluding the tray. The automatic fork vehicle lists 500 kg, the washer hook frame 350 kg, while the purchased-component table specifies a 300 kg-class fork. These different load objects are not fully reconciled. This page neither adopts 500 kg as whole-line payload nor assumes that a smaller number is necessarily inadequate.

Required inputs are net part weight, each tray level and additional fixture mass, fork extension, centre of gravity, hooking arrangement and hot conditions. Identify the object behind every rating, then trace the same loaded batch through each stage. A furnace's permitted net part weight is not automatically the washer frame's allowable total lifting weight.

## Record inter-station travel and quench transfer separately

The proposal states quench transfer ≤45 s and inter-station transfer ≤180 s. The latter cannot replace the former. Without agreed timing boundaries, travel speed alone cannot establish compliance. Commissioning should identify whether timing includes door opening, pickup and tank descent, and the immersion state at which it stops.

Central controls include unique batch identifiers, task priorities, timestamps and alarm records, useful for checking a batch's actual station route. Whether washing is required and whether oil or water is used are workpiece-process decisions. Washing, transport waits and tempering should match within one batch record. The equipment list is not a universal fixed process for all parts.

For similar projects, submit typical parts and multilayer loading drawings, complete weights, required routes and permissible transfer times. Use specimens for continuous verification of fork support, handover, lifting, washing and tray discharge. The proposed trial assessment of at least 5 furnace batches is an acceptance plan, not proof of completed delivery.
