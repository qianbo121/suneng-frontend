A Suneng quenching-and-tempering line proposal for 40Cr bolts is based on 500 kg of workpieces, while its oil-quench heat calculation separately includes 600 kg of trays and baskets. Even with unchanged workpiece output, changes in the hot load carriers entering the oil alter the thermal load. Review the tank using workpieces, actual load carriers and cooling between batches together.

## The 500 kg figure is workpiece mass, not the whole batch's heat load

The proposal uses a sealed multipurpose chamber furnace, oil quenching, washing and tempering equipment. The loading section lists four grid-tray levels and illustrates 500 kg of parts spread over two levels. The oil-quench calculation separately includes 600 kg of load carriers. That 600 kg is a calculation input for this draft, not a universal fixture weight for every 500 kg furnace. Adding the two figures does not create 1100 kg of workpiece capacity.

## Changing the rack requires thermal and passage checks

The draft separately calculates workpiece enthalpy change and load-carrier mass, specific heat and temperature change, combining them into approximately 605 MJ, equivalent to 168 kWh, per batch. This estimate depends on actual carrier weight, immersion temperature and calculation endpoint. This article retains the principle of accounting separately for fixtures, without treating the estimate as measured heat absorbed by the oil tank. Temperature references for parts and fixtures must also be reconciled.

The chamber and tray plan dimensions are both stated as 2000 × 1200 mm, with guide rails, push/pull and lifting mechanisms also provided. Matching nominal dimensions do not establish adequate travel clearance. Drawing dimensions should account for rack envelope, projections after loading, guides and the front-chamber passage. More levels or a heavier rack requires reassessment of both immersion load and movement of the entire tray.

## Reconcile oil volume, agitation and heat exchange in one version

The cover still specifies an 8 m³ quenching-oil tank, while the summary and chapter 5 state 12 m³. Neither the cover value alone nor the later value constitutes a completed design sign-off. Confirm usable oil volume, geometric tank capacity and actual liquid level, then reconcile the document.

Chapter 5 proposes agitators with flow-guiding tubes and a 180 kW plate heat exchanger. Agitation should correspond to basket position, obstructions and oil-flow distribution. The heat exchanger's nominal rating requires defined oil- and water-side temperatures, flows and operating conditions. Dividing 168 kWh by 180 kW gives an ideal arithmetic result of approximately 0.93 hours; it does not guarantee restoration of the required oil temperature for the next batch within one hour.

## Acceptance requires actual loading and cooling records

The draft leaves size distribution, batch composition, oil brand/grade and site circulating-water conditions for confirmation and proposes loaded trial production. Fix representative parts and carriers first, then record each total load, oil temperature before and after immersion, cooling-recovery time and corresponding product inspections. These determine whether the tank suits the agreed batches. This article does not treat calculations about through-hardening a particular bolt diameter as a confirmed material-capability boundary.

The supply section assigns the first oil fill, site circulating water and power supply to the buyer. An enquiry with existing rack weight and loading drawings, planned batch intervals and site cooling-water conditions helps define equipment and site responsibilities together. The two-furnace daily production arrangement still needs scheduling against the complete batch process.
