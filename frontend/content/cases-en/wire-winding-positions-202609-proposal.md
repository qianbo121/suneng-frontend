A Suneng stainless-steel wire annealing proposal lists 20 independent muffle tubes, while the requirement specifies only at least 10 take-up heads. The key questions are how many wires run simultaneously, how many strands each take-up machine handles and whether each strand has independent tension control. Machine count is not the number of simultaneously active take-up positions.

## Distinguish 20 tubes, 10 machines and 20 working positions

The draft concerns intermediate annealing of stainless-steel wire, predominantly grade 430, at diameters of 2.5–4.5 mm. It proposes one wire through each of 20 independent muffle tubes and two take-up arrangements: 20 single-position machines or 10 double-position machines. For the latter, each machine's two positions must independently take up two wires at the same time. If they are merely operating and standby reel positions for one strand, they cannot count as 20 simultaneous strands.

## Independent tension feedback needs a clear strand-by-strand map

The draft lists independent take-up drives and tension feedback for each strand, using a dancer arm or floating pulley to adjust speed as reel diameter changes, plus individual wire-break detection. Trace each tube outlet through guiding, pulling, traversing and the take-up reel. Identify the affected strand during a fault or reel change and the response of adjacent strands. A statement of 20-strand synchronisation does not prove integrated commissioning of these actions.

An inlet butt-welder and threading device are also listed as proposed rethreading provisions. They do not establish that every broken wire can be restored without stopping under hot, gas-filled conditions. Threading, reel changes and fault handling require whole-line operating procedures and control interlocks. This article does not expand brief proposal descriptions into operating instructions.

## Eight tonnes needs a time unit before setting speed or reel-change frequency

The copied requirement states expected output of 8 tonnes without a time unit. The proposal provisionally assumes 8 tonnes/day over 24 continuous hours, approximately 333 kg/h overall or 16.7 kg/h per strand if 20 strands share equally. If the intended requirement is 8 tonnes per 8-hour shift, the target is approximately 1000 kg/h, three times as much. This conversion does not imply that tripling current equipment speed would preserve annealing results.

Under the assumptions of 8 tonnes/day, 20 equal strands and continuous operation, a 500 kg take-up reel fills in approximately 30 hours. This average is not an actual reel-change schedule: strand sizes, reel capacities, stoppages and full-reel handling also matter. Running only 10 strands cannot inherit the whole-line output calculated for 20; individual-strand capability and process conditions need reassessment.

## Reel capacity also affects the site layout

The source proposes compatibility with 600, 700 and 800 mm reels, but 1000 kg coil weight remains subject to complete reel drawings. Equal outer diameters may conceal different barrel diameters, traverse widths and allowable loads. The draft's approximate 950 kg estimate does not establish the 1000 kg requirement. Reel bodies are buyer-supplied, and full-reel lifting arrangements need definition.

With 10 take-up positions on each side of the outlet, the draft allocates a 14 m take-up area and approximately 49 m overall longitudinal layout, including payoff, washing, heating, cooling and guiding. The 49 m is a planning figure for that layout; an 8–10 m furnace-length requirement is not the whole-line footprint. A different available length may justify rearranging take-up, but real reel-change, guiding and lifting space must remain, rather than merely compressing a drawing.

## Information needed to finalise the proposal

First confirm whether output is per day or per shift, simultaneous wire count and take-up format. Then provide strand sizes, complete reel drawings and the workshop layout. Process inputs include actual grades, prior deformation conditions and required properties after annealing. Proposed acceptance uses continuous-operation statistics, retained surface-reference samples and product-property tests. These records must correspond to the final operating combination; tube or take-up counts cannot replace them.
