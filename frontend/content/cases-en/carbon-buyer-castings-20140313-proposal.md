Buyer-fabricated heat-resistant parts involve more than a single hearth plate. Suneng's 2014 gas-fired baking furnace proposal for carbon and graphite products assigns three items to the user: panels described as “heat-resistant steel cast-iron panels”, bogie heat-resistant steel edge castings, and fixture support blocks. Locate each item in the drawings and incoming inspections instead of checking only the total equipment price.

## Locate the three buyer-fabricated items separately

The proposal covers similar heat-treatment duties for various carbon and graphite products, with a chamber measuring 3 m×1.6 m×1.3 m. The furnace-body price section lists the panels; the bogie section separately lists heat-resistant steel edge castings and fixture support blocks. All three are user-fabricated. The panel name alone does not establish that it is a bogie hearth plate, and the latter two items must not be merged into one fixture.

## Thickness alone is insufficient for fabrication

The structural description makes the cast heat-resistant steel bogie edges part of the overall frame, while the quotation assigns fabrication to the user. Obtain final component drawings specifying material, shape, connections, fits and inspection requirements before manufacturing and assembly. A quoted thickness range is not a complete fabrication drawing. Do not import a heat-resistant steel grade from another project.

Check support blocks separately against the workpiece and fixture layout. The rated load of 10 t is explicitly for uniform loading and excludes fixture support blocks. Submit block weights, support positions and workpiece contact conditions for design confirmation. “10 t” alone does not establish suitability for any concentrated loading pattern.

## Other responsibilities remain separate

The supplier was to install and commission the equipment and provide relevant drawings; the buyer was to prepare foundations and site support. The responsibility section expressly assigns supply and installation of power and control cables between cabinet and furnace to the supplier. Heat-treatment process commissioning belongs to the buyer, assisted by the supplier. User fabrication of some heat-resistant parts does not transfer all wiring or equipment design to the buyer.

The source does not fully assign preparation and approval of every component drawing or inspection of incoming buyer-made parts. Complete that responsibility schedule before fabrication, including delivery dates and assembly checks. Do not wait until furnace installation to discover that edge castings, panels or support blocks differ from the equipment drawings.

## Limits of this reference

This page helps define three user-fabricated interfaces in a historical quotation. It does not establish that a particular carbon or graphite product can be produced directly under this proposal. The source does not provide enough product-structure, loading or atmosphere information for that conclusion. Where the product or process imposes requirements for combustion atmosphere, volatiles or surface condition, submit them for separate confirmation by both parties.

The acceptance section proposes verification of process curves, industrial-utility consumption and workpiece temperature differences during soaking. These clauses are not an acceptance report. Fixed energy-saving percentages or uniformity statements in the proposal are not rewritten here as completed test results.
