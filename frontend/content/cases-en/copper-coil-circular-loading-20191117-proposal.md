A Suneng copper-wire coil annealing proposal lists both a 2000 × 2000 × 1900 mm chamber and effective working dimensions of Ø1710 × 1600 mm. Racks and coils must be checked against the permitted loading zone, rather than assuming all of the square chamber is available to the workpieces.

## Chamber fit does not establish compliance with the loading zone

The proposal concerns copper-wire coil annealing in an oxygen-containing atmosphere, with a working range of 550–600°C and rated temperature of 650°C. Its chamber furnace and hot-air circulation do not provide a configuration basis for protective-atmosphere bright annealing. First confirm the process objective, then place coils and rack together on a loading drawing.

| Check | 2019 proposal record | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effective working dimensions | Ø1710 × 1600 mm | Check the rack-loaded arrangement; do not substitute square chamber dimensions |
| Chamber dimensions | 2000 × 2000 × 1900 mm | Chamber space; corners and overhead space are not automatically usable loading zones |
| Load per batch | 5.5 t = 1 t rack + 4.5 t copper wire | Identify net copper-wire and rack weights separately |

Ø1710 describes a circular section, whereas 2000 × 2000 describes a square. Even if a coil fits the square chamber, it and its rack must still meet the stated effective working zone. Loose turns, rack cross-members, fork-entry features and projecting lifting lugs all need the actual loading drawing. This article does not subtract the two dimensions to prescribe usable clearance on each side.

## A different rack requires a new check of the 4.5 t wire allowance

The source splits 5.5 t into 1 t of rack and 4.5 t of copper wire. A heavier rack cannot retain an interpretation of 4.5 t of wire plus any rack. Besides total weight, check support locations, coil centre of gravity and fork handling. Addition is a load account, not a contact or local-capacity assessment.

The draft separately lists fork-arm capacity of 8 t, described as uniformly distributed and including fixtures. This concerns the loading/unloading mechanism and does not increase the proposed furnace load from 5.5 t to 8 t. Furnace loading, hearth support and handling equipment must each satisfy their conditions; the largest number cannot serve as the whole system's limit.

## Bottom supports must also leave hot-air passages

The proposed heat-resistant steel blocks have an I-shaped section with ventilation openings in the web and rest on grooved hearth bricks. Internal baffles guide circulating air through the working zone. Review support contacts, fork access and blockage of openings by baseplates, loose wire or spacers together. Sufficient block height alone is not enough.

These are proposed structures. Effective-zone uniformity and actual heating of copper-wire coils still require checks under agreed loading. Circulation fans and ventilation openings do not establish acceptable results for every coil diameter, winding tightness or stacking pattern.

## Three furnaces sharing one fork handler need consistent batch records

The group lists three chamber furnaces with one laterally travelling carriage and a longitudinal fork-type trolley. When handling rack-mounted coils at different furnace positions, confirm rack identity, load weight and furnace number as well as positioning and insertion distance. The proposed supervisory computer displays station states and records each furnace's temperature curves. Integrated commissioning can check whether those records correspond to actual batches.

For similar projects, provide maximum coil envelope, total loaded-rack weight, rack and bottom-support drawings, fork positions and actual annealing curves. Check the effective loading zone first, then loads and bottom airflow, followed by trials and temperature-record checks with agreed representative loading. The assessment will then correspond to the batch actually intended for the furnace.
