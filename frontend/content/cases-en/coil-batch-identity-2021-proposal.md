A 2021 Suneng stainless-steel coil solution-treatment proposal turns horizontal incoming coils upright, then transfers them through conveyors, a loading/unloading table and a fork vehicle into a working furnace. After heating, they move to a water tank. Batches of 2–4 coils pass several handovers, so orientation, position and material identity must stay aligned for process records to identify the actual coils.

## Map coil orientation and every handover

Incoming coils first turn through 90° and travel upright to the loading/unloading table. The table moves laterally between conveyor and fork positions, and the fork vehicle carries parts to the furnace. Discharge uses the unloading table, rollers and a turning mechanism to restore horizontal orientation for collection by an external forklift.

| Key item | Proposal record | Main check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turning angle | 90° | Contact, support and clearance in horizontal and upright orientations |
| Batch quantity | 2–4 coils per furnace | Maintaining each coil's batch association through grouping and separation |
| Immersion timing | ≤40 s | From coils leaving the internal support columns until fully immersed in the solution-treatment water tank |

Every handover needs review. Show coil outer and inner diameters, height and contact areas on turning, conveying and fork drawings. Define when the receiving mechanism supports the load sufficiently for the previous one to withdraw. The draft prohibits contact scratching during handling, but that is a quality requirement, not an outcome established by fitting a turner and rollers.

## A loaded-platform signal does not identify the batch

Loading and unloading share one table, with fixed limits/guides and load-pressure detection to determine its working state. This helps indicate receiving availability. Coil identity, material and process assignment depend on material-tracking records; the two information types must correspond.

The tracking section proposes manual scanning at the inlet and manual label application at discharge. Scanned information includes a heat-treatment process code used by controls to call the process. It also calls for records of the furnace used and corresponding curve. Further rules must define grouping 2–4 coils into a batch, changing associations after temporary splitting or furnace reassignment, and matching outlet labels to incoming identifiers. Functional descriptions do not replace these rules or test records.

Automatic identifier capture and discharge inkjet marking require separate design to final requirements, and the production-management system has separate pricing. Distinguish manual scanning from automatic tracking during selection. Data-upload capability does not include every scanner, marker and management-software component.

## Put the 40-second boundary on actual movements

The fork-vehicle parameter table starts ≤40 s when coils leave the furnace's support columns and ends at full immersion. It excludes queuing before door opening and does not start with the open-door signal. For multi-furnace scheduling, check advance vehicle positioning, door movement and pickup-to-immersion separately against the batch process.

Vehicle quantity is not fully reconciled: the overall list states 1 set, while another table states one in phase one and one in phase two. This does not establish the capacity of ten furnaces sharing one vehicle or automatic doubling of handling after expansion. Final layout and equipment counts must precede checks of waits and successive-batch timing.

## Match batch counts to actual loading and the water system

The draft gives maximum furnace throughput per batch as 8000 kg, while fork capacity is at least 8 t, uniformly distributed and including fixtures. These cannot become 8 t of parts plus any fixtures. Selecting 2–4 coils per batch still requires actual coil, spacer and fixture weights and supports to meet fork-vehicle conditions.

The cooling text routes the solution-treatment tank through a settling tank to plant utility cooling. Another passage describes independent settling tanks and cooling towers for individual furnaces. This article retains the disagreement rather than combining both into a fixed scope. Submit coil grouping, station drawings and process timing first, then reconcile water-system configuration and responsibilities with Suneng.
