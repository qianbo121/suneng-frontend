A Suneng heating proposal for open-die forging of aluminium bars lists furnaces A and B, with a bogie-hearth version and a fixed-hearth version with charging machine compared for each. There are two equipment items, each with two options, not four furnaces to purchase. Compare charging machines, racks and site handling conditions alongside the furnace bodies.

## Define each furnace's load before comparing hearth types

The draft concerns pre-forging heating of aluminium-alloy bars 200–500 mm in diameter at working temperatures of 350–520°C. Furnace A is calculated for a 1000 kg load. Furnace B's actual load is unspecified and provisionally estimated at 200 kg. A's 1000 kg cannot be assigned to B, nor does B's smaller size make 200 kg a confirmed requirement.

The source lists a rated temperature of 600°C and a working range of 350–520°C. These remain proposal ranges and do not replace pre-forging process curves for individual aluminium alloys.

## Compare heat carried out using the actual racks

Furnace A's heat-balance table uses a 550 kg bogie and a 150 kg fixed-hearth rack respectively, with calculated cold-start loaded heating times of 2.7 and 2.3 hours. These are design calculations, not evidence that the fixed-hearth version has tested 17% faster. Identify which components actually enter and leave with the parts, their masses and initial temperatures, and distinguish cold starts, continuous hot-furnace production and handling waits.

The hearth description proposes supporting bars on heat-resistant slide rails and leaves bars per batch and placement for confirmation. These inputs affect both support locations and charger access. Rail spacing, charger forks and minimum bar diameter should appear on one layout; total tonnage or chamber volume alone is insufficient.

## Check existing handling equipment before reducing supply

Charger specification remains open: fully automatic integration with the forging line or a manually operated electric fork-type unit, together with pickup height, reach and position relative to the forging press. Existing equipment must be checked against the furnace opening, rack pickup locations and position interlocks. Calling two devices forklifts does not establish that both can place and remove material inside a fixed-hearth furnace.

The scope includes internal slide rails, but loading fixtures and racks are separately excluded. Define hearth mechanism, charger, workpiece supports and external lifting interfaces individually. Fixed hearth with charging machine does not mean that all site handling equipment is included.

## Keep quantities and delivery terms consistent when comparing historical prices

In the August 2026 proposal, selecting bogie hearths for both A and B gives a combined price of CNY 1,109,000; selecting fixed hearths with chargers for both gives CNY 1,384,000. Both use FOB Chinese-port terms, meaning comparison under the draft's stated shipment-port delivery conditions. The difference is CNY 275,000. The draft's approximate prices for the two chargers total CNY 281,000, which is different; the entire package-price difference cannot be assigned to that same charger cost. Current selection requires a new quotation against the final list.

The combined scope includes factory tests, calibration information and technical documents, while on-site installation and commissioning are separate, with further boundaries for sea freight and related site costs. The draft also contains both ±5°C and ±7°C uniformity requirements. The acceptance value and applicable test conditions need reconciliation. After choosing the configuration, arrange tests using agreed loading and processes; report names or zone counts do not replace results.
