A Suneng proposal groups circuit boards, waste plastics, organic coatings and photovoltaic panels into four test lines for one project. The first three use continuous-feed capacity, while the photovoltaic line converts batch weight into hourly throughput. Distinguish those measurement bases before comparing actual material, loading and complete cycles; four kg/h figures alone are not a valid comparison.

## Define continuous throughput and batch-equivalent throughput separately

The source targets 300 kg/h each for circuit boards and waste plastics and 1000 kg/h for organic coatings. Photovoltaic panels are loaded whole onto racks for intermittent pyrolysis, followed by an oxidation furnace. These are proposal targets, not acceptable throughput established by completed trials. Material form, loading method and downstream equipment also differ between lines.

## 840 kg divided by 2.8 hours describes one batch assumption

At 35 kg per panel and 24 panels per batch, the load is 840 kg; divided by 2.8 hours, it gives 300 kg/h. This requires 24 panels to fit in practice and successive batches to follow the agreed complete cycle. A separate reference to approximately 2 hours of pyrolysis is not the entire batch cycle and cannot exclude handling, purging or other necessary waits.

The draft proposes two-level racks and 17 m effective furnace length but provides no final rack arrangement or clear door-chamber dimensions sufficient for checking. Panel length and width, vertical and rack-to-rack spacing, supports and entry/removal passages should share one layout, followed by treatment-uniformity verification. A weight calculation for 24 panels proves neither fit nor adequate treatment on every level.

## Establish the actual material layer before residence and length

Continuous-line calculations use estimated bulk density, layer thickness and surface loading. The source already requests measured bulk density, moisture and composition. Baled materials also require bale dimensions and compaction. At the same kg/h, looser or thicker loading changes required conveyor space. The draft's assessment of temperature at the layer centre still needs representative-material trials.

Increasing belt speed is not an unconditional way to raise output while retaining treatment results. Nor should specified residence be shortened merely to retain a proposed furnace length. Some work-in-process quantities and time bases in the draft remain unreconciled. This article retains the inputs requiring measurements and does not present estimated lengths as final designs.

## Agree equipment interfaces and product trials separately

Suneng proposes furnaces, conveying, inlet/outlet sealing, oil/gas extraction and condensation equipment. Crushing, baling, robot-loading hardware and downstream sorting are buyer-supplied, with a separate boundary for off-gas treatment. Throughput calculations must therefore establish upstream feeding and downstream receiving conditions. Pyrolysis-gas composition, pressure and oxygen monitoring, and off-gas handover parameters require joint confirmation during subsequent design; nominal throughput cannot replace them.

The source treats oil yield and quality as exploratory test-line objectives without advance commitments. Equipment temperature/residence checks, material-treatment results and downstream emissions verification each need corresponding records. This article explains scale and interface assessment without presenting non-condensable-gas combustion, oxygen-control provisions or downstream oxidation as verified self-sufficient heating, stable oil production or compliant emissions.
