## Establish whether quantities are per zone or per furnace

The technical requirements specify two control zones, and the detailed section describes three thermocouples for a control zone. The purchased-parts list states two duplex and one single-element thermocouple, while the shell section lists only one thermocouple mounting socket. Confirm which entries are per-zone quantities and which are whole-furnace totals before checking probes, sockets, extension leads and recording channels. Neither a total of six probes nor rejection of all measurement requirements based on the single-socket statement is justified.

The recorder is described as 12-channel in one place and 6-channel in the purchased-parts table. This does not change the need to assign clear duties to hot and cold measurement points, but it affects wiring and recording. The final schedule should reconcile points in each zone, alarm and recorder wiring, and actual channel allocation together, avoiding discovery after delivery that a required measurement has no recording channel.

## Assign duties after confirming quantities

The control-zone section describes three thermocouples: a duplex control thermocouple connected to the temperature controller and recorder; a second duplex thermocouple at the hot point connected to the overtemperature alarm and recorder; and a single-element cold-point thermocouple connected to the recorder. Hot and cold points do not necessarily mean the furnace top and bottom. The section requires locations established by the most recent temperature-uniformity survey. Confirm the applicable report together with the current layout revision.

## Test ports require corresponding test results

The proposal provides thermocouple entry ports for temperature-uniformity testing and a system-calibration port near each zone's control thermocouple. These establish proposed test interfaces. Port locations, instrument models or cited standards alone do not prove that testing has been completed. Hot- and cold-point locations should be traceable to test records applicable to the equipment in its current condition. If probe locations or loading conditions change, determine any additional verification under the agreed requirements.

The 16-point no-load test, effective heating zone and normal load are three separate checks. Loaded commissioning should use the agreed workpieces and fixtures, followed by confirmation of which results support actual production decisions. Empty-furnace uniformity cannot replace inspection of workpiece material properties.
