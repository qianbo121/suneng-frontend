A Suneng chamber-furnace proposal for heating titanium-alloy forgings and other large metal forgings places heating elements on the door. A heat shield behind the raised door separates its hot face from roof-mounted thermocouples, compensating cables and nearby equipment. Reviewing this arrangement requires checking both the heating layout with the door closed and the direction of the exposed hot face with it open. Disconnecting heating power as the door rises does not mean the hot face has cooled.

## The shield addresses the hot face in the open-door position

| Proposed feature | Source arrangement | Relationship to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Five-sided heating | Both sidewalls, rear wall, door and hearth | Assess the closed-door heating surfaces separately from the hot face exposed when the door rises |
| Heat shield | Removable structure above the furnace opening, directly behind the raised door | Whether it separates the hot face from roof thermocouples, compensating cables and adjacent equipment |
| Heating zones | Detection, display and regulation in 4 zones | Zone instruments do not remove the need to protect the installed sensors and cables |

The source identifies the shield's purpose precisely: at the door's highest position, its hot face faces the thermocouples and compensating cables above the roof. An insulating structure made from an angle-steel frame and rigid fibre board is therefore placed at that location. A sectional drawing should show the final raised position, heating-face orientation and equipment above the furnace together, so that shield coverage can be checked against actual heat exposure. Merely stating that an insulation board is included is insufficient.

The shell description also mentions a roof insulation board to protect nearby equipment from rearward radiation from the hot door. The two descriptions concern similar objects, but the source lacks sufficient part numbers to establish that they are two separately supplied assemblies. Compare the general arrangement and supply list to avoid counting them twice or assuming that a local shield covers the entire roof.

## Disconnecting power and shielding residual heat serve different purposes

The proposal permits heating only after the door is fully clamped and disconnects heating power automatically when the door rises. Door lifting is permitted only after the auxiliary clamping mechanism has reset; lowering is permitted only after the maintenance safety pin has reset. These are proposed movement permissions, not records of functional tests. The shield addresses the spatial relationship between hot surfaces and adjacent equipment. Electrical interlocks cannot replace it, and the shield cannot replace door-position, clamping or safety-pin signals.

Procurement checks can address two separate items: the permission table and responses to abnormal door or mechanism states; and shield coverage and cable routing with the door open. Residual heat remains after power is removed. Whether approach or maintenance is permissible cannot be determined from power isolation alone. This page specifies neither temperature thresholds nor hot-maintenance procedures.

## Removability is a maintenance provision, not a reason to omit isolation

The source makes part of the shield removable to provide access to the door heating elements. The design should identify the removable sections, the areas exposed after removal and how protection is restored to its original position. This does not justify promising maintenance without cooling or claiming that the proposed access arrangement has already reduced downtime. Final maintenance conditions must agree with the equipment maintenance documentation.

## Verification must relate to the actual load, measuring points and drawings

The technical text lists 600 kW, a rated temperature of 1200°C and a uniform-temperature zone/effective chamber size of 3200 × 2600 × 1200 mm. Proposed uses include pre-forging heating and heating for normalising, annealing and tempering. This is an intended application range, not a statement that every titanium alloy uses 1200°C or the same process. The proposed furnace-temperature uniformity of ≤±8°C is to be measured by a qualified third party. Test temperature, effective zone and loading conditions still need definition; five-sided heating alone does not establish uniform forging temperatures.

Temperature detection uses duplex Type S thermocouples, with one circuit connected to control and recording instruments and the other to monitoring instruments. This remains a heating-space measurement arrangement. Although protection of the roof thermocouples and compensating cables has a specific purpose, it does not establish workpiece-core measurement. Calibration, interlock tests and loaded temperature verification should each have their own records.

Suneng's document proposes equipment design, installation guidance, commissioning and drawing documentation. The buyer is to provide site conditions according to the layout and utility information. Initial discussions should include a loading drawing, space in front of and behind the door and above the furnace, temperature-cable routes and maintenance access. These can then be checked against protection and movement permissions with the door raised, making the shield a verifiable configuration.
