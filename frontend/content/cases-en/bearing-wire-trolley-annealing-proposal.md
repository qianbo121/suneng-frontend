A 2017 proposal for a new bogie-hearth spheroidising annealing furnace for bearing-steel wire specifies an effective chamber of approximately 10×3×2.2 m, a maximum design temperature of 850°C and a maximum load of 30 t excluding fixture support blocks. These conditions illustrate loading and equipment arrangements in the historical proposal; they do not represent actual customer output or acceptance results.

This page uses only publicly disclosable parameter extracts from that year's new-furnace proposal. Later retrofit documents are handled separately. Customer identity, contracts and original drawings are outside this page's public scope.

## Match coils to the bogie

Coil geometry, rack structure, load distribution and spacing jointly affect working space, heat transfer and handling paths. Coils of the same total weight can occupy different spaces and require different heat-treatment times because their dimensions and arrangement differ.

The source lists workpieces of φ1.3 m diameter and 1.7 m height. The effective height of 2.2 m is explicitly measured from bogie surface to furnace roof. Include supports and coil placement within that space. The difference between the two heights is not entirely available for extra workpiece height, and 30 t alone cannot determine the number of coils in the current project.

The bogie description places its edge precast refractory blocks 20 mm below the central refractory layer, explaining that this protects the cast-iron edge plates during loading and unloading. For a new project, confirm workpiece and fixture positions relative to the central load-bearing surface and edges for the intended loading and crane-placement method. The entire bogie outline is not a surface on which loads can be placed arbitrarily.

First define usual and maximum coil dimensions, individual coil weight, rack weight, number of layers and transfer method, then assess bogie loading and chamber conditions.

## Define the spheroidising annealing objective

Confirm steel grade, incoming microstructure, wire diameter, target microstructure and downstream processing requirements before defining heating, soaking and controlled cooling. Furnace-temperature records, workpiece heating and product microstructure/hardness inspections concern different measured objects and acceptance conditions.

Typical industry GCr15 process curves, microstructure ratings and hardness ranges can support process discussions only where applicable. They are not reported here as inspection results achieved by this historical project.

## Use maximum loading correctly in capacity calculations

Include loading, unloading, heating, soaking, process cooling and waiting in the full batch cycle, then calculate from actual accepted output. The 30 t figure is the proposal's maximum-load definition, not a directly transferable net workpiece quantity per batch or a daily-output guarantee.

That 30 t maximum excludes fixture support blocks. When estimating net workpiece quantity, still verify the actual weights and distribution of fixtures and supports against the bogie design and effective zone.

## Information to prepare before an enquiry or retrofit assessment

- Steel grade, wire diameter, coil dimensions and individual coil weight.
- Usual and maximum loads, racks and loading arrangement.
- Incoming condition, process objectives and existing curves.
- Utility conditions, workshop space and handling arrangements.
- Temperature-testing, product-inspection and supply-scope requirements.

A retrofit assessment also needs current photographs, drawings, control documents and operating records. Evaluate new-furnace and existing-furnace retrofit documents separately; new design parameters do not establish the existing equipment's condition.

## Is this evidence of an accepted installation?

This is a historical proposal record, without delivery or acceptance evidence for the project. It helps explain equipment conditions and purchasing checks. Delivery status, actual output, microstructure, hardness, uniformity and specific energy consumption each require their own supporting records.
