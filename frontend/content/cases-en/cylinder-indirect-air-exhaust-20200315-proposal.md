Suneng's 2020 curing-furnace documents for fully carbon-fibre-wrapped gas cylinders separately describe combustion-flue-gas heat exchange, internal hot-air circulation and furnace exhaust. The stated 15–20 cycles per minute refers to hot-air recirculation, not fresh-air changes or exhaust capacity. Identify each airflow and outlet before checking the configuration against resin emissions, loading and site exhaust connections.

## Separate the three air and gas streams

| Stream | Original proposal | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Combustion flue gas | Discharged after heat exchange through a stainless-steel heating chamber and exchanger | Not direct flue-gas supply to the workpiece heating space |
| Internal hot air | Repeated circulation through fans, ducts and guide hoods; 15–20 cycles/min | Not 15–20 complete fresh-air changes per minute |
| Furnace exhaust | Separate induced-draught exhaust fan on a main duct, with programmed volume and duration | Does not establish downstream purification |

The source explains indirect heating as combustion flue gas heating the inner liner and air in the exchanger, with a circulation fan returning heated air to the chamber. “Indirect” describes the proposed heat-transfer method. It does not guarantee that flue gas can never enter the chamber in actual service, that products cannot be contaminated or that all emissions comply. Gas-path integrity and performance after manufacture require inspection records.

## Recirculation rate is not exhaust air-change rate

The 15–20 cycles/min appears with circulation fans, sidewall air entry and flow guides. Furnace exhaust has separate fan, main duct and programme requirements. To assess removal of curing emissions, confirm actual exhaust flow, make-up air source and exhaust duration at each stage. Large circulation-fan airflow cannot be entered as exhaust volume.

The application is fully carbon-fibre-wrapped cylinders, with adjustable process temperature from ambient to 200°C. Actual resin, released substances and process stages need current evidence. Equipment temperature range does not establish a suitable formulation, and circulation rate cannot determine combustible-gas concentration. A combustible-gas alarm is included, but the text only addresses the monitored probe area. Complete detector locations, alarm logic and actions require design documents.

## Match resin collection to airflow passages

The bottom flow-guide plate's holes are designed with resin blockage in mind. A drawer-type drip tray beneath the plate collects resin for removal and cleaning. Review collection and cleaning access together with potential obstruction of airflow by fixtures or accumulated resin under actual loading. The tray collects drips; it does not replace removal and treatment of gaseous emissions.

## Complete site interfaces and inspection scope

The seller's list includes the curing furnace and combustible-gas alarm, while the mobile loading trolley is user-supplied. The final heating/exhaust scope should assign the combustion-flue outlet, process-exhaust outlet, external ducts and downstream treatment equipment. The phrase “exhaust treatment system” in an equipment introduction does not prove that every external system is included.

The parameter table requires a third-party furnace-temperature uniformity report, and acceptance clauses also schedule no-load checks and trial operation. These are proposed delivery checks, not an existing project report. Confirm actual loading, temperature measurement locations and exhaust conditions during process review before defining validation criteria. A furnace-uniformity report does not replace exhaust-system or cylinder-curing quality inspection.
